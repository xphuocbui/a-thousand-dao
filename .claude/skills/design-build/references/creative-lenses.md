# Creative Lenses

The easy answer to any seed is its most literal one: a fire build is more fire damage. These lenses
force a different angle. Use a different lens for each direction, and pick the ones that pull hardest
against the seed. For the rule-by-rule prompts that produce modifiers, see `modifier-forge.md`.

## Mechanical lenses

- **Invert the resource.** What if the build wants the thing others fear: an empty pool, low health,
  a curse on itself, a long fight?
- **Change the shape of time.** Does it open with everything, build for 20 seconds, or strike in one
  pulse every 10? Cooldown rhythm is a personality.
- **Attack a different target.** Not health. Their linh lực, their ward, their timers, their
  preparation, their soul, their will to stay.
- **Turn a rule into a promise.** Take something the game treats as a limit and build around it: the
  always-on basic attack, the wait of a guarded art, the one-ward rule.
- **Make the weakness the identity.** Start from what it cannot do, and let that be why it is feared.
- **Chain, don't stack.** A applies a status, B consumes it, C refunds B. The build is the chain.
- **Use the tags.** Conversion, generating and overcoming cycles, variant elements. Lava is more
  interesting as Fire and Earth at once than as a third colour.
- **Win before the fight.** Preparation, ground, poison on the blade, the formation already laid.
- **Fight with a cost outside the fight.** Lifespan, heart demon, vitality, karma with a sect, a
  debt owed. The price arrives seasons later.
- **Borrow strength.** A beast, an artifact that does the fighting, a formation, an enemy's own force.
- **Pick the neglected channel.** Everyone thinks of art hits. What about the basic attack, ticks, ward
  drain, the companion or a soul strike?
- **Stack the useless stat.** Find what no build wants: unused thần thức, pill toxicity, a failed
  breakthrough. Make it the whole point.
- **Spend the overflow.** Go to where a cap clips and ask what the clipped amount could become.
- **Change what winning is.** Not their health at 0. Their linh lực at 0, a stalemate, a mutual knockout.

## Lore lenses

- **Who wrote it, and why is it wrong?** Most great techniques are flawed copies, unfinished, or made
  to solve one problem. The flaw is the build's sacrifice.
- **What was it for?** A farming method turned to killing. A funeral rite. A way to keep a dying
  master alive. Function explains mechanics better than theme does.
- **Who hunts its users?** Every strong path has enemies with a reason.
- **What does it do to the person?** Cultivating grief, hunger or patience changes someone. Show that
  in a mechanic, not only in the description.
- **Whose name is on it?** A lineage, a heresy, a banned sect, one person's last wish.
- **The second reading.** Something that means one thing at first and another once you know its
  history. "Mercy" as a technique that keeps enemies alive for a reason.

## Fusing game and lore

The best builds cannot be separated from their story. Test each direction: if you swapped its lore
for another's, would anything feel wrong? If not, the lore is paint. Push until a mechanic only makes
sense because of the story, and the story only feels true because of the mechanic.

- From a game concept, ask: who would need this badly enough to invent it?
- From a lore concept, ask: what would this feel like as a decision, not as a number?

## Tone

Xianxia in the register of a long, patient cultivation novel: mortal struggle, slow power, cold
choices, real cost. Original throughout: no names, sects, characters or signature techniques from
existing works. English first, with Hán-Việt in brackets.

Names read like a manual's title: element or image, then form, then a word such as Quyết, Công,
Kinh, Chân Kinh, Bí Điển or Tâm Pháp. Prefer a concrete image (Withered Reed, Ninth Bell, Salt Road)
to stacked superlatives (Supreme Ultimate Heavenly Demon).

## Things to avoid

- A list of bonuses with one theme word on each.
- Directions that differ only by element.
- Three arts that do the same thing at different sizes.
- A build with no real weakness.
- Lore that restates the mechanics.
- Numbers doing the work a rule should do.
