### 214 Băng Tâm Các (new seat, bang_tam)

**Look (new):** Băng Tâm Các's white halls climb a black lava mountain on a sheet of blue glacier ice, raised on the ruin of Hồ Thiên Phái, with a great Vessel Hall on the summit and a tall white pagoda behind it. At the mountain's foot the green-tiled tip of one old pagoda roof still stands out of a bare field of black lava, and a ring of jade-green light glows up through the ice.

**Lore (new):** Băng Tâm Các, the Pavilion of the Ice Heart, is a great sect (đại tông môn) of sword cultivators (kiếm tu); its name comes from the old line that the ice heart sits in the jade vessel. In 470, when the sect claimed the empty ice of Ngọc Hồ Sơn, Tần Ngưng Sương moved it onto the ruin of Hồ Thiên Phái and laid the Jade Vessel Array into the ice over the buried vent; the steam stopped and the birds came back. Sở Ngọc Hồ has led the sect since 1010 as keeper of the Jade Vessel, with Tiết Hàn Mai as first elder. Hồ Thiên Phái, buried by Chúc Long Sơn in 425 with its founder Khúc Hồ Già, lies beneath the halls, and the sect leaves the ruin as it lay.

### 302 Bình Thủy Môn (new seat, binh_thuy)

**Look (new):** The Tide Hall stands on agarwood stilts above low brackish flats of grey grass, cut by creeks that fill and empty with the tide. A raft track runs off toward Wave Rock, and giant crabs still roam the mud around the stilts.

**Lore (new):** Bình Thủy Môn, founded in 520 by strangers far from home and sworn to Lạc Hà Tông, has kept its Tide Hall here since 1116, on agarwood stilts above the crab flats of Cự Ngao Chử. Its head Bùi Phiêu Bình chose the flats for the Still Moon Tide Scripture, a canon that fills at the turn of the tide, and in 1115 he fought the Claw King for two tides and killed it at slack water. He drove its shell plates into the mud as the Slack-Water Weirs, and the sect's boats moor at Lãng Thạch. The old hall above the landing is now the Strangers' Hall.

### 260 Hàm Nguyệt Trang (new seat, ham_nguyet)

**Look (new):** Dark timber halls under grey tile stand on tall piles over the middle of a round, still pool behind a bar, joined by long covered walkways, and birches ring the shore. The water is so calm that every roof and the full moon lie in it twice.

**Lore (new):** Hàm Nguyệt Trang is a small water-arts sect (tông môn) sworn to Tùng Đào Cung, living on spirit fish (linh ngư) and lake pearls. Its founder Đới Thiềm Quang raised the moon halls on piles over Trầm Bích Đàm in 412, because the moon lies stillest here, and sank the Sunken Disc under them to hold the moonlit qi. Its mistress Kiều Nguyệt Châu has led it since 1150 and doubled the pearl harvest. The sect keeps its boats and its pearl trade at the Shore House on the lake.

Only a cart track from the manor on the forest road reaches it, and visitors who sit too long in the shade wake with a headache and no memory of the hour. Those who stay too long at the manor are said to end up tending the beans.

### 193 Lễ Tuyền Phái (new seat, le_tuyen)

**Lore (new):** Lễ Tuyền Phái, a minor sect (tiểu tông môn) of healers under Phượng Minh Cung, keeps its Dawn Hall (Triêu Đường) on this shore, where in 950 Mai Lễ Tuyền delivered Hoa Triêu Dương, now head of Phượng Minh Cung, at dawn as the clouds turned red. A bronze mirror that came with the gift hangs under the hall's eaves and gathers the lake's dawn light. The plain folk say the east's dawn clouds answer Lạc Hà's sunset clouds in the west, and pilgrims of the Palace come at dawn to watch them.
