No one in this file belongs to a sect, and most of them were refused by one or walked out of one. They are what the
sects leave over: a ferryman who carried the wrong crates, a scribe turned out for selling unstamped talismans, a
deserter who let an array slip, a girl the palace's fire would not take. They live in the places the sects do not
bother with: the free ferry under a cut bridge, a refugees' harbour that will not speak of its plain, a market that
opens only in the rain, a burned salt town with an empty harbour, the quays of a sect that sailed off the edge of its
charts. They are tied to each other because the south is small for anyone who cannot fly.

## 2. Where they live, and how the land made them (R1)

The loose are born where everyone else is born, so their roots follow their villages, and nothing filters them
afterwards. That is why the odd roots end up here.

- **The Ngư Ca river and the island (regions 10, 11, 13)** run Wood 30-47 % and Water 20-39 %. The ferry people, the
  rope-weavers and the island's vine-climbers are Wood and Water like the river villages, and most of them were turned
  away by nobody: they never asked.
- **The steppe of Thu Thủy Loan (region 12)** is Earth country (33-46 %). The diggers of Vô Định and its moss gatherer
  are Earth-rooted, and Lạc Hà would have looked at them on Placement Day had they come.
- **The karst north of Nam Phố (546, 549, 551, 548)** carries Darkness at 8-25 variant qi. A Water and Earth child
  born there may wake Darkness, as Nam Phố's ink-grinder did after he joined. Giản Vạn Lộ woke it as a boy and could see
  in the caves; the hall that took him for his Wood turned him out later for a different reason.
- **The Pivot's highlands (14-15)** are Earth and Metal. A drill-mine on the Tập Trận seam holds wind qi at 30, and the
  miner's daughter born there has a Wind root the Pivot could not weigh.
- **The thunder ridge (19) and Hắc Phong (702)** hold Thunder at 23-40. A child with Fire and Metal in the root born
  at Hắc Phong or the Tử Tinh mine may carry Thunder. The Đồ of Hắc Phong do, and their Fire is a family thing (Đồ Vô
  Cữu's is 62), not the land's. Bành Tiểu Điện's Fire (30) is thin, as the mine's 10 % would make it; her Thunder is
  the mine's.
- **Phượng Minh's coast (16, 17, 20)** is Water country (Water 33-56 % in the pepper and fishing villages) under a Fire
  sect. Its Water children reach the palace's outer gate on their Wood and fail the Feather Kindling; Lan Bất Nhiên is
  one. This is the R1 × R2 Fire tension the Phượng Minh writer reported, seen from below.
- **The rain forest (18)** is Wood and Water; its loose people are runners, toad-catchers and buyers' guards who live
  off the Night Rain Market.

## 3. How they live

- **No gate, no lamp.** A loose cultivator has no life lamp in a vault, no ration of pills and no hall to bury them.
  The south's loose say "unlit" for themselves, half proud: the Tower lights a lamp for every sworn member (N-thinh_vu-06),
  and the unlit are the ones whose deaths nobody feels from afar.
- **Methods are bought, kept or remembered.** The commonest main method here is a sect's outer primer, taken out of the
  gate by someone who failed or left: Lãng Đào's pole primer, the Pivot's Weighing Stone, Phất Y's dry-road sword,
  Phượng Minh's wutong breath, Xuyên Lâm's sandal steps. The rest are market copies (the Thousand-Bolt Scripture, the
  Hunter's Reach Canon, the Hollow Bell Canon) or a village breathing method with no name (the stand-ins).
- **The markets are their halls.** Tương Phùng Thị (49), Vật Hoa Thị (50) and its season auction, Hạc Đinh Thị (51),
  Thiều Âm Thị (53), the apricot market of Hạnh Hoa Thị (54) and above all the **Night Rain Market** of Thu Trì Thành
  (42), which opens only after dark and only in rain (A315) and asks no names (A313). Most of the people in this file sell
  there, buy there, or guard someone leaving it.
- **Customs.** A loose cultivator does not ask another's sect, past or master; the question is what you are selling.
  A debt between loose people is paid in kind and witnessed by a third. A loose cultivator who dies on the road is
  burned where they fall and their ring goes to whoever burned them, which is why the road's honest people carry a
  flint and its dishonest ones carry two.

## 5. The circles

### The Thủy Khẩu crossing (region 13, the Ngư Ca border)

A river mouth Lạc Hà dug out by hand in 720 (A196), a toll post that counts Tùng Đào's barges, and below it the one
crossing on the split-toll border that charges nothing. **Yến Hồi Độ** (A) runs it. He carried the Đào Sa raiders and
their crates over in 1173, pulled Ngô Bất Tán out of the river in 1174, saw Lô Tiểu Ca fall, and has kept a stern lamp
lit in daylight ever since. He knows both halves of the Ferry War: he was the first man told to collect Giang Cửu
Khúc's unset toll, and he kept Kha Triêu Phi's slip. His deckhand **Trịnh Tiểu Độ** comes from the last ferry family of
silted Dã Độ. The hemp runner **Mao Đinh Lang**, a Đào Sa orphan who fished nails beside Lữ Tẫn Chu, brings Tang Ma
rope down to the water by night for the far-bank smuggler Đinh Vạn Tác (loose-north), who takes it to Lãng Đào's rope
master, and the ferryman looks the other way. The rope comes from
**Phó Kết Thằng** of Tang Ma, who wove the bridge of 1174 and keeps Lô Tiểu Ca's lamp lit on it. The toll pilot Vi Lan
Châu lets the free boat pass uncounted and chases the smuggler.

### The diggers of the miasma's edge (region 12: Tân Khẩu 135, Vô Định 223, Hồng Vũ 222, Vật Hoa Thị 50)

**Khâu Bạch Thủ** (A, Kết Đan) is the grey-haired boy of A214, three hundred years on. He digs Vô Định's ash, sells at
Phan Vật Hoa's season auction, and lives in Tân Khẩu, the harbour that will not speak of the plain, where he is the one
man who does. The Miasma Watch turns back everyone but him. He knows the miasma was laid, by whom, and where the blue
sword lies, and he has told no one. His disciple **Khôi Thảo Nhi**, niece of the Watch's scout Hàm Tú Sa, picks the grey moss that Yên Quang boils into the
tincture Hình Lạc Nhạn breathes. **Mẫn Bổ Võng**, his neighbour, is the mother of Đồ Tiểu Liên and of the boy the
miasma took in 1196; she has asked him to look for the bones. **Cừu Quỷ Hỏa** digs burned Hồng Vũ Thành by the spring
ghost lights (A187) and undersells the old man. The Pivot's barrow-keeper Quý Thủ Lũy, on the far edge of the same
plain, has been his friend since she found him in 900.

### The Night Rain Market's regulars (Thu Trì Thành, 42; region 18)

**Giản Vạn Lộ** (A), the map-seller, a Nam Phố scribe turned out in 1174 for selling a copy of the reed-bed order, with a Darkness root and a forged cloud seal,
sells nine false maps for every true one and prices them alike. One of the three mountains Triệu Huyết Y has narrowed her master's abode to is his invention. **Đoan Mộc Thính** (B), a Wind-rooted fortune-teller who
is never wrong about the weather, sings the treasure that his maps lead to; in the dry season she works the music
market of Thiều Âm Thị. **Lộc Bất Quy** (B) guards buyers who leave carrying too much, and his disciple **Từ Bán Kiếm**,
who learned five layers of Phất Y's dry-road primer, walks behind him. **Mộc Tẩu Vũ**, who failed the Tower's Bell Test, runs parcels
to Lục Nhĩ Thính's ferry inn and tells her what the buyers said, without knowing whose ear she is. **Mễ Thiềm Nhi**
sells toad venom from Độc Thiềm Lâm to the market's alchemist Ô Thiềm Tử. They all owe Kiều Mãn Lâu something.

### The ridge hunters and the Thạch Môn camp (region 19, Kinh Trập Sơn)

**Đồ Liệt Cơ** (A, Kết Đan), the last chief of the Hắc Phong rogues, has held Thạch Môn Trại for 250 years. The fort sits on the road that comes down from the Pivot's
plateau to the apricot market and the Rain Tower. Her camp robs the Pivot's ore buyers and the caravans on it, never the
Tower's trains, and the Tower lets it stand as a wall on the Pivot road. Khoái Thiết Ưng (demonic), the stockade boy who
fled north in 940 with the captain's sabre while she went south with eleven others, holds his own peak in the north. Her people: **Trương Dã Đạo**, a Xuyên Lâm guide struck from the road books; **Lỗ Tam Chùy**, a runaway
miner with a thread of Thunder; **Quản Thập Nhất**, who keeps the books and fences through Hạnh Hoa Thị at one stone
in eleven; **Phù Hỏa Sinh**, a Lai Nghi caravan guard who was taken and stayed. Below the camp, in the potters' village
of Ô Diêu, the widow **Sầm Vãn Nương** (B) raises **Bành Tiểu Điện** (B), a Thunder root born at the Tử Tinh mine. The
Tower's Thunder Hall wants the girl, and so does the camp. The hunter **Ân Thủ Sơn** (B), her dead husband's sworn
brother, hunts the tiger king that killed him and has asked the widow twice. **Bành Nhị Hổ**, the dead man's brother,
would sell the girl to the Tower for a stipend.

### The dark coast (regions 17, 20)

**Biện Ám Phàm** (B) runs two unlit boats out of burned Hải Diêm Trấn: Night Rain goods south to Phượng Minh's ports,
pearls north. She learned dark sailing from her family, who learned it in Thôn Hải's hold. Since last winter she has run
the southern leg past Liệu Phàm Bảo, steering by Kiển Mục Hải's lamp: hers are the unlit sails in his letter. Every autumn she collects Vọng Triều's head's oil at the Hải Diêm landing, the price of 913. Her diver **Yên Thủy Hoa** comes from Âu Lộ, the
village that swore never to dive. Further south, **Lan Bất Nhiên** failed the palace's Feather Kindling three times and
gathers spice with **Liên Quế Chi** at Hương Liệu; **Đan Tẫn Lão** picks the ruin of Xích Thành Môn and sold Hùng Phần
Vũ a carved wall-stone. Inland at Kích Khánh Đường, **Chúc Dư Âm** (B), the musician of 1195, plays every night for Lộ
Văn Khánh's pen and believes his sister knows he is safe. Đoan Mộc Thính's Wind has twice carried his flute to her across the ridge.

### The island's divers (region 10, Cô Vụ Đảo)

**Uông Tầm Phàm** (B) lives alone on the empty quays of Bích Không Cảng and dives the reefs where Cô Phàm's charts stop.
His father sailed on Lận Viễn Phàm's first boat in 1158 and did not come back. He means to sail the third boat Hầu Bách
Chu has built and told no one about, against the lost captain's daughter who means the same, and the vine-climber **Tuyên Đằng Nhi** means to
go with him.

## 9. Story hooks

1. **The whole of the Ferry War.** Lữ Tẫn Chu learns that the ferryman of Thủy Khẩu carried his father's killers and
   kept the slip. Yến Hồi Độ will tell it once, both halves: Kha Triêu Phi's "reed beds" and Giang Cửu Khúc's unset toll.
   Told to the right people it ends the second Ferry War before it starts; told to the wrong ones it starts it. (Yến,
   Lữ Tẫn Chu, Lô Xướng Vãn, Kha Triêu Phi, Giang Cửu Khúc, Châu Phi Vân; places 139, 21, 13.)
2. **A map of the plain.** Khâu Bạch Thủ decides to sell his true map of Chiết Kích Nguyên once. Hình Lạc Nhạn wants it
   for the sword, Quý Thủ Lũy for her dead, Hình Trọng Sơn so no one has it, Giản Vạn Lộ to resell it. Whoever buys it
   learns the miasma was laid by Lạc Hà. (Khâu, Hình Lạc Nhạn, Quý Thủ Lũy, Hình Trọng Sơn, Giản; places 135, 223, 582.)
3. **The boy in the grey.** Mẫn Bổ Võng believes her son is alive in the miasma, as Khâu was in 900. Khâu half believes
   it too. (Mẫn, Đồ Tiểu Liên, Khâu, Hình Lạc Nhạn; places 135, 223.)
4. **The rope that was cut.** Đồ Vô Cữu is sent south after Lộc Bất Quy and comes within a day of Thạch Môn Trại. His
   sister must decide whether to meet him, and he must decide whether to bring the White Wind Hook. (Đồ Liệt Cơ, Đồ Vô
   Cữu, Lộc Bất Quy, Kiều Mãn Lâu; places 389, 42.)
5. **The node that slipped.** Phương Đoạn Vũ learns who let the four out of the pass in 1156, and that the Pivot's hound
   is in Thu Trì for him. The Tower owes a deserter of its enemy a life. (Lộc Bất Quy, Phương Đoạn Vũ, Đồ Vô Cữu, Từ Bán
   Kiếm; places 42, 211.)
6. **Three claims on a thunder girl.** The Thunder Hall, the bandit mistress and her own uncle each want Bành Tiểu Điện;
   her mother wants none of them. The player can carry her to Xuyên Lâm (which takes any root but cannot teach Thunder),
   to the Tower, to the camp, or hunt the tiger king with Ân Thủ Sơn so the family can pay its own way. (Bành Tiểu Điện,
   Sầm Vãn Nương, Đồ Liệt Cơ, Hầu Vọng Xuân, Bành Nhị Hổ, Ân Thủ Sơn; places 880, 389, 231, 874.)
7. **The abode map.** Triệu Huyết Y comes to the Night Rain Market for a fourth map of her master's abode. Giản Vạn Lộ
   has the true road and has sold her three false ones. Đoan Mộc Thính sees the rain coming and tells him to leave. (Giản,
   Triệu Huyết Y, Đoan Mộc, Lục Nhĩ Thính; place 42.)
8. **The unlit sails.** The player stands a night on Kiển Mục Hải's tower and sees Biện Ám Phàm's boats steer by his lamp.
   Put the lamp out and they wreck; leave it lit and the Night Rain Market's poisons reach Phượng Minh's ports, and follow them to the landing where Vọng Triều's own oil waits. (Biện,
   Kiển Mục Hải, Yên Thủy Hoa, Trạm Châu Nhi, Kiều Mãn Lâu; places 795, 776, 217.)
9. **The flute at Kích Khánh Đường.** Đoan Mộc Thính tells Chúc Hiểu Nghi what she hears on still nights. (Chúc Dư Âm,
   Chúc Hiểu Nghi, Lộ Văn Khánh, Đoan Mộc Thính; places 724, 53.)
10. **The third boat.** Hầu Bách Chu shows the boat he has built. Uông Tầm Phàm, Tôn Niệm Triều and Sa Kình Nhi all mean to sail, and
    Tuyên Đằng Nhi means to go with Uông. Lận Viễn Phàm can pay for one crew. (Uông, Tuyên, Hầu Bách Chu, Tôn Niệm
    Triều, Lận Viễn Phàm; places 226, 302, 72.)
11. **The carved stone.** Đan Tẫn Lão finds a second wall-stone in Xích Thành's ruin, and this one names the Hall of
    Spoils. Hùng Phần Vũ would pay to keep it quiet. (Đan Tẫn Lão, Hùng Phần Vũ; places 215, 29.)
