## The group

A cultivating clan (tu tiên gia tộc) is a family that has held one town's land or trade long enough for its children
to cultivate as a matter of course. It is not a sect. It has no gate, no lineage and no admission rule. It has a seat
(a house in a town), a trade that pays for pills, a small method of its own or a bought one, and a habit of placing
its children: sons and daughters go to the sect gates of their region, marry into sect families, or stay to run the
trade. A clan's power is the sum of where it has put its children.

**Counts.** 50 living (Kết Đan 5, Trúc Cơ 15, Luyện Khí 30), tiers A 6, B 8, C 36; 7 dead ancestors (4 B, 3 C).
Members already in sect files stay there and are tied from the clan side: Tiêu Vấn Phong, Tiêu Hòe Sinh (Tầm Kiếm),
Tiêu Ngọc Hòe (Lê Hoa Am), Tiêu Bạch Lộ (Băng Tâm), Tiêu Kiến Quang (dead, Tầm Kiếm file); Ôn Song Ngọc, Ôn Tiểu Kiều
and Tưởng Ngọc Cân (Chân Kim); Tạ Quy Yến (Lạc Hà) and Tạ Quy Hồng (Nam Phố); Lục Đạo Hoa (Cốc Vũ); Hỗ Kinh Điểu
(Vân Khởi); Đới Liệt Cương (Phần Vũ).

**How the land shapes them (R1).** Clan seats are towns, and towns sit on even or river land, so clan roots are mixed
and follow the town: Water-Wood on the northern and western rivers (Hòe Âm 39/30, Phong Lâm 36/32, Phong Đăng 34/31,
Yên Ba 38/29), Wood-Earth with Fire on the Pivot's grassland (Song Kiều 26/26/19), and Earth-Fire with everything on
the silk plain (Tang Điền 27/21/19/17/15). Roots run in families: parents and children share elements, and a spouse
from another village brings a new one (Tống Thanh Miêu's Metal into the Lục, Lỗ Hạnh Nương's Fire into the Ôn). That
is the clans' quiet tragedy: a family born on one land has the same root every generation, and a sect whose lineage
wants another element keeps saying no to it.

**Customs most clans share.** A clan head's word binds the family's children until they join a sect. Marriages are
made for land or trade and recorded with the town. Clan children who cultivate are sent to the nearest gate first and
to a vassal when they fail, and a clan keeps count of how many it has inside each sect. The dead are buried on clan
land, never in a sect's ground.

## 1. The Tiêu of Hòe Âm (蕭氏, 槐蔭)

**Seat and land.** Hòe Âm Trấn (66), a port town at the river mouth where the coast road turns inland for the
southern massif and Lê Hoa Am (15). Water 39 %, Wood 30 %, Metal 10 %: a Tiêu is Water-Wood with a thread of Metal,
and every living Tiêu shows it. The clan holds the **locust road** from the landing to the foot of the hermitage path,
the valley fields along it, the dye works, the boats at the landing and the **post inn** where the Pavilion's envoys
change horses on the way south. Tầm Kiếm Đường (406) sits twelve days up the road (about 180 km) on the clan's old hunting lodge.

**History.** Tiêu Chủng Hòe, a hunter who sold herbs, planted the locusts in 520 so travellers to the passes would
walk in shade, and his hamlet took the trees' name (A102). The rule since: each Tiêu child plants a locust on the road
the year it is born and is buried under it. In 930 his descendant Tiêu Kiến Quang saw a sword's cold light behind a
seal on the mountain and ran (A108); he searched for eighty years. His son **Tiêu Thừa Âm** buried him under the road
in 1010 (N-clans-02) and has headed the clan since. In 1150 Thừa Âm sent his grandson Tiêu Vấn Phong, twelve, to the
hermitage gate, where the abbess Thái Lê Tuyết turned him away (A110). The clan's answer was a rule (N-clans-06):
daughters to the hermitage, to be inside, and sons anywhere else. In 1162 Thừa Âm gave the boy the hunters' lodge for
Tầm Kiếm Đường (V069, N-clans-07).

**Trade.** Locust buds (hòe hoa), dried and boiled for the yellow dye Hòe Âm's boats carry south and sold as a styptic,
mostly to Băng Tâm's guest alchemist Viên Chước, who is three harvests behind on his bill. Furs come down the road
from the hall. The inn takes the Pavilion's envoys (Ngụy Thanh Hàn has slept there on every journey south for a
century) and the hall's clients.

**Method.** *Locust Shade Breathing* (Hòe Âm Tức Pháp, Profound, Water and Wood): sat at noon under a living locust,
drawing Water qi down through Wood as a tree draws water up. It is slow and steady, and useless while the holder burns. The clan
has never taught it outside the family.

**Marriages and grudges.** Wives come from the valley villages (Hà Thu Đình from Hàn Khê Thôn). The clan's grudge is
with **Lê Hoa Am**: a public grievance (the boy refused for being a boy) and a private one (Thừa Âm still wants the
cave). Thừa Âm sends the abbess a jar of locust honey every solstice, unsigned. The abbess and the Pavilion's first
elder Tiết Hàn Mai were girls together under the Tiêu locusts in the 760s, which the clan has never forgotten.

**The truth of 1150.** Thừa Âm told the boy to ask the abbess about the cold light before anything else. The abbess
turned him away for the question, not his sex (le_hoa §9). Thừa Âm half knows it. The clan, the hall and Tiêu Vấn
Phong believe the other story, and Thừa Âm means to test it again: he will send **Tiêu Minh Khê**, sixteen, to the gate
in 1202, the year of the abbess's walk to the seal, with the same question (tam_kiem hook 6).

**Inside.** Thủ Đạo, the heir, wanted his son at the inn and has spoken to him only at the New Year since 1162. Thủ
Tuyền sent one daughter to the hermitage and lost the other to the vats: Tiêu Uyển Âm refused to go in 1195 and has
not been forgiven. Thủ Tân sent his son to the Pavilion. The Tiêu now have a child in every house on the massif.

## 2. The Ôn of Song Kiều (溫氏, 雙橋)

**Seat and land.** Song Kiều Trấn (82), the Pivot's market town on the river road below the Ngọc Hành mines, where the
miners' ore is weighed on open scales in the square (A219). Grassland: Wood 26 %, Earth 26 %, Fire 19 %. The Ôn are
Wood-Earth with Water or Fire. They hold the **twin bridges** and both toll houses, the barge moorings, and the rope
village of Thằng Ma Thôn (708) by marriage.

**History.** Ôn Giá Hồng, a mason's son, built the twin bridges in 900 (one for the ore carts, one for everyone else)
and kept the tolls (A229). Every load of Ngọc Hành ore crosses an Ôn bridge before it reaches the Pivot's scales, and
the miners say they pay twice. He drowned in the flood of 1112 holding the north bridge's eastern pillar (N-clans-04).
In 1095 his son **Ôn Trọng Lương** married his daughter Ôn Thục Hà to the son of Tưởng Bách Luyện, head of Chân Kim Các
(N-clans-03), and the assay house became kin and partner in the tolls (chan_kim §8).

**Trade.** Tolls, mainly on ore; bridge and quay building for hire; hawsers from the Lỗ ropewalks. Chân Kim takes one
Ôn child a year into its outer gate, eye or no eye (chan_kim §6): Ôn Song Ngọc and Ôn Tiểu Kiều went that way, and Ôn
Bích Đê goes next year.

**Method.** *Twin Span Pillar Method* (Song Kiều Trụ Pháp, Yellow, Earth and Wood): stand waist-deep in moving water
and sink the qi into the riverbed like a pillar. It trains the body more than arts. The head formed his core on a
bought Mountain Heart Canon and keeps the pillar method for the family.

**Marriages and grudges.** The Tưởng of Chân Kim (kin since 1095). The **Pivot's assayers**: Đặng Quân Bình refuses the
ore toll rise every spring, and Ôn Thế Hoành and the scale-master Hồng Chuẩn Bình argue over the first cart every
morning. The miners, who pay.

**Inside.** The north bridge's eastern pillar has been cracked since 1112. Only the head and the mason Ôn Lập Trụ know.
The toll rise the head asks for each spring is the money to rebuild it before a loaded cart finds the crack. Thế Hoành
wants his daughter Song Ngọc home to take the toll houses, and his son Hữu Toán wants them himself. Ôn Thục Hà has seen
both houses' books (the Ôn tolls and the Tưởng scales) and has told neither what the other's say.

## 3. The Tạ of Phong Lâm (謝氏, 楓林)

**Seat and land.** Phong Lâm Trấn (79), a river port in the maples, Nam Phố Đường's town in Lạc Hà's west. Water 36 %,
Wood 32 %: the Tạ are Wood-Water, Earth thin or absent. That is the clan's whole trouble with Lạc Hà, whose inner gate
wants Earth. They own a hall with swallows under its eaves, a school and a maple grove, and little else.

**History.** In 470 the Tạ came to Phong Lâm, once great in another land, with nothing but their name, and the swallows
of their old hall followed them (A193). Tạ Bất Vong never said which land or what fell. In 1000 the Vương, their old
rivals, wrote for the first time in four hundred years (A200), and the Tạ have answered every letter since. In 1160 the
Vương stopped writing (N-clans-09). Only the head knows.

**Trade.** Letters and learning: the clan school teaches the town's children, the Tạ cut its seals and write its
petitions to Nam Phố and Lạc Hà, and the maple grove's sap and timber are rented out. They are poor in the way only an
old name can afford.

**Method.** *Returning Swallow Breath* (Quy Yến Tức, Profound, Wood and Water), the one thing carried out of the old
hall besides two heirlooms: the breath goes out and comes back with a little more, and its arts strike twice. The
manual stops at the ninth layer, and the clan believes the second half stayed behind.

**Customs.** Every Tạ child is named for a bird that returns (Quy Yến, the swallow; Quy Hồng and Quy Nhạn, the geese;
Quy Hạc, the crane; Quy Hộc, the swan). Nests are never touched. A Tạ answers every letter.

**Marriages and grudges.** Wives from the flower and fish villages (Hoa Khê, Ngư Lương). **Lạc Hà**: the clan's
grandsons fail the dusk test on Earth and are placed with vassals (lac_ha §4). Tạ Quy Yến goes this spring, and
**Châu Phi Vân** of Nam Phố has asked for him by name. The head wants him in a great sect's inner gate and has
written to Lạc Hà's head Tô Vãn Chiếu, unanswered. The boy's father would rather he went to Nam Phố and was happy.

**Inside.** For forty years the head has written both sides of the Vương correspondence, in two hands. His
granddaughter Tạ Quy Hộc reads the letters before he does and has noticed the Vương's hand changed in 1160. The Old
Hall Pair (fan and ink jade) is split between the head and his elder son, and by custom it is whole only in the next
head's hands.

## 4. The Lục of Phong Đăng (陸氏, 豐登)

**Seat and land.** Phong Đăng Trấn (92), a river-mouth grain town under Kinh Trập Sơn, in Thính Vũ's south. Grassland,
Wood 34 %, Water 31 %; the Lục are Wood-Water. They hold the paddies on both banks and three hundred tenant
households, the Thiều among them. Cốc Vũ Quán (441) sits on the bamboo hill above and calls the rain.

**History.** Lục Đa Cốc cleared the first paddies, and in 600 his harvest was so rich that the Tower took its grain tax
from the Lục first (A288). It has every year since, in kind, before any other farm in the south. The abbess of Cốc Vũ,
Dương Hảo Vũ, was born on the Lục tenancy in the drought of 830 (coc_vu). In the drought of 1171 rain fell on the Lục
bank and not on the tenants' bank (N-clans-08). Three Thiều children died that winter.

**Trade.** Rice and grain, taxed first. The surplus the Tower's counters do not count goes to market.

**Method.** *Seed Store Method* (Tàng Chủng Pháp, Yellow, Wood): qi is gathered in the autumn sittings and sealed like
seed grain, and drawn on only when needed. Every Lục child learns it before letters. The head formed his core on a
bought Verdant Cycle Scripture and a great many pills.

**Marriages and grudges.** **Thính Vũ** taxes them first, and no Lục has ever been taken into the Tower: its gate wants
Metal at 30, and the Lục have none. The heir's wife Tống Thanh Miêu brought the only Metal into the family, and her
daughter Lục Ương Nhi has it at 28. She was refused at the gate by two points the year the tenant girl Thiều Mộ Vũ was
admitted on exactly 30. **Cốc Vũ Quán**: the third son Lục Đạo Hoa runs its spirit-rice terraces, the Lục tenants' children make
the 375 km walk there, most of a month, when the Tower fails them, and the head has sent the abbess rice every autumn for 150 years. The **Thiều**
tenants: Lục Mạch Thu married Thiều Xuân Nương against the head's word and was given the far bank, the dry one.

**Inside.** The rain of 1171 was bought. The head paid the abbey's prior Thời Tri Tiết to bring it to the Lục paddies
only, and the payment is in the prior's second receipt book (coc_vu hook 1). Thiều Xuân Nương has wondered for thirty
years why only one bank had rain. The heir Lục Kế Điền is 140, still at Foundation, with sixty years left, and his
father has three centuries. Every pill goes to the tax first and to him second.

## 5. The Hỗ house of Yên Ba (扈家, 煙波)

**Seat and land.** Yên Ba Thành (30), the cape's port city, where Thúy Vi's port house ships the sect's pills. Water
38 %, Wood 29 %. The Hỗ are Water-Wood. Mẫn Tú Chi brought Metal from the pine-soot village. They own a coasting fleet,
the warehouses on the quay, and the jetty at Bạch Sa (57).

**History.** In 1040 the captain Hỗ Độ Hải married a daughter of the Thẩm of Bạch Sa and built the jetty that made
the white sands a trading town (A029). She never cultivated. Her kinsman Thẩm Tịnh Sa is still the only cultivator the
Thẩm have had. Độ Hải was lost off the cape with three low-riding ships in 1120 (N-clans-05), and his son **Hỗ Tế
Xuyên** took the house at seventy-four.

**Trade.** The port house's pill cargo along the cape under Diêm Hải Tảo; salt, tea and timber for anyone. In 1170 Kim
Vạn Lý offered to buy the fleet, and Tế Xuyên sent him a sandalwood model of it and no reply.

**Method.** None, by choice. A merchant buys what a clan inherits. The head runs a bought Still Moon Tide Scripture and
formed his core at 150 on bought pills, and the house buys its children what methods it can.

**Marriages and grudges.** The Thẩm of Bạch Sa (kin since 1040). **Thúy Vi**: the house's best customer, and the sect
that will not sell a Hỗ child an inner-gate place. **Vân Khởi Quán**: the Yên Ba merchants pay its school to take
their restless sons, and Hỗ Kinh Điểu keeps running away from it (van_khoi hook 8). **Kim Vạn Lý**, a rival.

begun to wonder why they never quite match.

## 6. The Đới silk house of Tang Điền (戴家, 桑田)

**Seat and land.** Tang Điền Trấn (94), the mulberry town on the silk plain below Phượng Minh's Fire Court at Hỏa Ti
(449). Earth 27 %, Fire 21 %, the rest even. The Đới are Fire-Earth with Metal or Water. They hold mulberry fields
along the road to the Fire Court, reeling sheds, a cellar, and a table at the silk market.

**History.** When Hỏa Ti burned in 590, its reelers' daughters fled to Tang Điền with the reeling songs (N-chuc_ha-01).
One of them, Nhan Dư Ti, married a Đới cocoon-buyer about 600 and taught his house to reel (N-clans-01). The house
passes to its best reeler, son or daughter, and takes her husband into its name. Đới Tàm Nương has held it since her
father Đới Vạn Ti died in 1150. Her brother Đới Liệt Cương left for Phần Vũ's vents to stop being a silk trader's son
(phan_vu), and now captains its levy, whose work since 1162 has been guarding the palace's road from Tang Điền to
the Fire Court, through the Đới fields.

**Trade.** Raw silk, sold to two buyers: **Chức Hà Các**, whose weavers need Tang Điền thread for the fire-silk Thần
Vân Cẩm weaves without the vents, and the **palace's factor Chiêm Lợi Châu**, who buys first and cheapest and has set
the market's price since the seat moved to Hỏa Ti in 1162 (chuc_ha §8).

**Method.** *Reeling Thread Breath* (Trừu Ti Tức, Yellow, Fire and Earth): a breath drawn as fine and unbroken as a
thread from a cocoon in hot water. It is sung, not read: the layers are verses of the Hỏa Ti songs.

**Marriages and grudges.** The **Long** mulberry pickers (Long Tang Chi married the head's son; Long Chước Sinh of Phần
Vũ is her brother's grandson). **Phượng Minh's Hall of Trust**: the factor and his mistress Vưu Cẩm Thư. **Đới Liệt
Cương**: the head has not forgiven him and uses his levy all the same. His son Đới Sơ Cương, whom she raised, does not
go out to watch him ride past.

**Inside.** In her cellar the head has reeled three bolts of true fire-silk from the old songs. The palace holds that
the last fire-silk of Hỏa Ti was given to its founder in 592 and laid into the Nest Array (N-phuong_minh-04). Only her
granddaughter Đới Thu Kiển has been in the cellar. Her son Đới Hậu Cẩm thinks her war with the palace will cost the
house everything, and he sells the factor a little more each year than she knows.

## 8. Story hooks

1. **The boy at the gate, again.** Tiêu Thừa Âm sends Tiêu Minh Khê up to Lê Hoa Am in 1202 with the question his

3. **The cracked pillar.** A loaded ore cart breaks through the north bridge at Song Kiều. The Pivot blames the Ôn, the
   Ôn blame the toll it would not raise, and Ôn Lập Trụ is the only witness to how long the crack was known. (Ôn Trọng

## 9. Notes for the cross-sect pass

- **Uncovered great sect: Tùng Đào.** The natural seventh clan is the **Lâm woodcarvers of Lộc Minh** (60, A059),
  with Lâm Điêu Bách and Lâm Thanh Mộc in Tùng Đào's files and Lâm Dã Hạc loose.
- **Builds name stand-ins.** `depth_check.py` reads techniques only from `sects/*.techniques.json`, so a clan method
  in `people/clans.techniques.json` cannot be a build's `main`. Clan members carry `hoang_/huyen_<element>` stand-ins,
  and `build.note` names the clan method.
