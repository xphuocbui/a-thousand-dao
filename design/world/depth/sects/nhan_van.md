| Leans | Water, Wood (unchanged; the seat fits) |

| Seat | **stays** at Nhàn Vân Quán (place 364, island steppe, Cô Vụ Đảo); a **Pool House** in Đàm Ảnh Thành (37) |

| Way of life | a Taoist abbey of painters who paint with qi in the ink: door scrolls whose guardians step down, and doubles painted from reflections |

The abbey is named for a line the island knows by heart: idle clouds and their reflections in the pool, day after day
(nhàn vân đàm ảnh nhật du du). Its painters paint the sky the pool of Đàm Ảnh Thành holds, upside down. They sell door
scrolls to the island and pay their lord in unsigned paintings. The rest of the west thinks they are idle, and they
agree.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): stay.** The seat is island steppe at Water 32 %, Earth 23 %, Wood 16 % (density 0.80). Water is the top
element and Wood is over 15 %, so the seat passes. No move, no re-lean, no founder's array.

**Why the abbey is not at the pool.** The painters came for the pool of Đàm Ảnh Thành (A152), but the city is Lạc
Hà's. It was Cô Phàm's before that (C087). In 905 Lạc Hà would not give a school of painters a quarter of its city. It
granted them instead a dry steppe 22 tiles west that no one wanted. The founder said it was the right place, "where the
clouds have nothing to stop them" (N-nhan_van-01). The abbey has lived in two places ever since:

- **The abbey (364)**: novices, the paper and the ink brought in from the forests, the head's studio, and the graves.
  A road runs east to the pool and another west to Xích Ngạn Trấn (70), and a track goes to Ngưu Đẩu Thôn (468).
- **The Pool House (Ảnh Đường)** in Đàm Ảnh Thành (37): a studio by the water with a window onto the pool, kept by the
  elder Mễ Phù Vân. Painters work there in turns. Lạc Hà's page says they are "at Đàm Ảnh Thành", and the painters you
  meet in the city are the Pool House's.

**What the land gives.** Ink and paper are the island's. The soot is burned from Kỳ Nam agarwood (455) at Trầm Hương
Trấn (456). The paper is beaten from the canes of Vạn Can Lâm (475) at the mill in Lục Trúc Thôn (474). Water comes from
the abbey's well and the pool. The island is Water and Wood country, from the mangroves at Water 40 % to the bamboo at
Wood 44-48 % (R1), and its children are born Water- or Wood-rooted far more often than Earth. The abbey's people are
village children from the coast (Water) and from the cane and agarwood forests (Wood). A few are city children from the
Pool House streets. Lạc Hà's Placement Day sends it the mainland's Wood and Water children who failed the dusk test.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **Be the pool.** A cloud keeps no shape and the pool holds every cloud without keeping one. The abbey's one teaching
  is stillness that receives: a painter does not reach for the subject but waits until it is reflected. Diệp paints one
  cloud a day. A painting is finished when the painter stops wanting it.
- **Reflections, never faces.** A living person is painted only from their reflection in water or bronze. "The face is
  its owner's; the reflection is the world's." The rule was custom until 1158 and law after it.
- **No signatures.** A painting belongs to the sky it came from. The abbey's scrolls are unsigned, and its tribute to
  Lạc Hà is paid in unsigned scrolls that Lạc Hà's envoy prices.
- **The day.** Novices grind ink at dawn, one breath to a stroke. Painters walk out at the hour the clouds are best and
  paint until the light goes. On a cloudless day the abbey fasts, because there is nothing to paint.
- **Dress.** Undyed grey silk with the cuffs inked black from work. The hair is knotted up and pinned with a brush;
  the head's brush is the oldest. Pool House painters wear a blue thread at the collar.
- **Food.** Rice, pond fish, Hải Yến Trấn's swallow-nest broth on feast days (A149). No wine on painting days.
- **The dead.** A dead painter is burned, and the ash is ground into ink. The abbey paints them once more with their own
  ash, from memory, as if reflected. The scroll hangs for a year and is then washed off in the pool at Đàm Ảnh Thành.
  The painters say the dead go into the water, and they mean it.
- **Taboos:** painting a living face; signing; painting in anger; painting the sky of a day the painter did not see
  (the founder's art is the one exception, and its holders use it rarely).
- **Outsiders** may sit in the courtyard and watch all day for nothing. Nobody will explain anything. The abbey sells to
  anyone and haggles with no one, except through Mễ Phù Vân.

**Why it fits.** The island gives Water first and Wood second, and so does the art: the pool's stillness is Water, the
brush, paper and soot are Wood. The canon is a defensive, patient method. The painter's double takes blows while she
works, and the scrolls send out guardians that hold a doorway (memory: sustain is king). It is weak against a rush,
which is why the abbey hires no one and fights almost never. The founder's secret is the dangerous one: it paints an
old sky out of the water, and in a fight it sets a foe back a few seconds. The abbey believes it costs the painter a
piece of herself each time.

## 6. Admission

- **Outer gate:** Water or Wood at 30 or more. The abbey does not recruit. Children come up the road and grind ink,
  and the ones still grinding after a season are kept. Placement Day children come from Lạc Hà with a contract.
- **Inner gate and above:** Water at 45 or more. The test is to grind ink for a day without speaking and then paint the
  sky the pool held that noon from memory.
- **Exceptions:** the Pool House servant Ô Lão Nghiễn (tạp dịch). No guest elders, and no hostages.

## 8. Relations

- **Lạc Hà Tông (lord):** a loose rein and an honest ledger (loyalty 65). Tribute is assessed in sheets and scrolls.
  Lạc Hà's collector Cận Thủ Chuyết argues the sheet count with Cù Lục Trúc every autumn. Its envoy Hách Ánh Hà
  prices the unsigned scrolls, the one haggle Diệp allows. The Counting House master Tông Thủ Tín likes Mễ Phù Vân,
  because he prices things. Placement Day sends Wood and Water children; Nghiêm Thu Lộ came in 1198, placed by Tô Bình
  Chu. The island warden Lận Viễn Phàm asks every year for the fleet's sky and is refused.
- **Đoạn Thương Hải** (loose, Nguyên Anh, blind; A155): the abbey's neighbour at the pool and its best friend. Diệp
  and Tần paint for him; the servant Ô Lão Nghiễn tells him the clouds. He heard the sail.
- **Bình Thủy Môn** (fellow vassal on the island): its ferries carry the scrolls across the strait. Its head Bùi Phiêu
  Bình took Phiêu Bồng Quán's books in 1102 (V093) when the abbey had asked for them. Diệp still wants one of them, a
  painter's notebook on the library list.
- **Nam Phố Đường** (fellow vassal, talisman scribes who paint the clouds of the southern shore, A198): a rival in
  painting. Châu Phi Vân's scribes paint clouds onto talismans, and the abbey thinks they paint the wrong side of the
  cloud.
- **Lạc Hà's Tăng Vân Tiêu** buys Đới Kim Trục's signed scrolls for his stalls on the mainland. **Giang Vọng Phàm**,
  Cô Phàm's last blood, trades rumours of the fleet with Tần Đảo Ảnh.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **Idle against priced.** Diệp gives the abbey's work away, or lets Lạc Hà price it. Mễ Phù Vân, the founder's other
  disciple, runs the Pool House as a business, and Lạc Hà's Counting House prefers him. When Diệp dies Mễ is the obvious
  head. He would make the abbey rich and stop it painting old skies, which is the part of the art he fears.
- **The founder's half sky.** Kha died with half of 705's sky on his silk. Diệp has finished it once, secretly, in 1062,
  and burned it (see her secret). Tần Đảo Ảnh is trying the same art untaught, because Đoạn heard a sail.
- **Two Tầns.** Tần keeps a reflection-double at her desk, as her mother did. Đinh Hồng Thụ has noticed. Mễ has not.
- **Signed scrolls.** Đới Kim Trục signs some of the scrolls he sells, the abbey's one real crime. Mễ suspects it and
  takes his cut without asking.
- **The placed girl** counts every brush, because a tenth of her earnings is Lạc Hà's until 1228.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The sail in the pool.** Tần Đảo Ảnh paints an old sky out of the pool for the first time, and a sail is in it.
   Lận Viễn Phàm will pay, Giang Vọng Phàm wants to sail after it, and Diệp knows where it leads. (Tần, Đoạn Thương
   Hải, Lận Viễn Phàm, Giang Vọng Phàm, Diệp; places 37, 226.)
2. **The fog west of the Evening Isle.** If the player earns Diệp's trust she says what she saw in 1062: Cô Phàm's sails
   going into a fog bank west of Tịch Chiếu Đảo. Lạc Hà's elders go to that island to die. (Diệp, Lận Viễn Phàm, Bồ Tàn
   Chiếu; places 196, 226.)
3. **Two at the desk.** Đinh Hồng Thụ asks the player whether a reflection-double can learn to want things. Tần's
   double has started to paint by itself. (Tần, Đinh Hồng Thụ, Mễ Phù Vân; place 37.)
4. **The founder's half sky.** Someone unrolls Kha's last silk at the pool's edge. The half sky finishes itself overnight.
   (Diệp, Mễ, Tần; places 37, 364.)
5. **Signed scrolls.** Lạc Hà's Hall of Scales finds a signed Nhàn Vân scroll on Tăng Vân Tiêu's stall and calls it
   fraud against the tribute. Đới Kim Trục needs the player to buy back the rest before Từ Bình Hành counts them.
   (Đới Kim Trục, Tăng Vân Tiêu, Từ Bình Hành, Mễ; places 72, 51.)
6. **The notebook in Bình Thủy's library.** Diệp asks the player to fetch one book from the Phiêu Bồng books Bình Thủy
   Môn took in 1102 (V093), and Bùi Phiêu Bình wants a scroll in exchange. (Diệp, Bùi Phiêu Bình; places 18, 380.)
7. **The sheet count.** Lạc Hà raises the tribute by a tenth after Mễ shows the Counting House what the scrolls fetch.
   Cù Lục Trúc and Cận Thủ Chuyết argue it out at the mill, and the paper runs short. (Cù Lục Trúc, Cận Thủ Chuyết, Mễ,
   Tông Thủ Tín; places 474, 364.)
8. **Clouds against clouds.** A painting contest at Hạc Đinh Thị, like the talisman contest of 1190 (A203): Nam Phố's
   scribes against the abbey's painters, and Tô Mộ Vân asked to judge. (Châu Phi Vân, Tần, Diệp, Tô Mộ Vân; place 51.)
