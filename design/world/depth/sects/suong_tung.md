A pavilion of scribes in the high pines above the long lake. Its people peel birch bark, boil resin, burn pine soot
for ink, and write wards. They are the only house the pines allow to write for them. Each autumn
they carry four hundred lanterns round the lake, and no village that hangs one has been flattened by the ice since.
Their head keeps a box his founder sealed, and he hopes never to open it.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): stay.** The seat fits, just: Wood 58.4 % leads easily, and Water 13.6 % is below the second lean's 15 %
but is the seat's second share, which the test also accepts. Water is thin in the high pines, so the pavilion takes it
from the frost. Its paper is pressed with melted frost and its canon writes Wood ink on a body of Water. The thin Water
is the craft's reason, not a fault. No move, no cost.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** The high pines (Wood 52-60 %, Water 13-20 %) breed Wood roots with a little
Water: the pavilion's papermakers and ink-burners, who mostly pass on their Wood. The lake shore (Water 33-46 %) breeds
the reverse. The pavilion's lantern-walkers bring back a few shore children every autumn who hold enough Wood for the
inner gate, and the north-shore boys who do not stay at the outer gate and carry lanterns.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **A ward is a tool.** The pines teach that what is heard must be earned and that a book can be stolen. The pavilion was
  founded on one exception: a ward is a tool, not a teaching, so it may be written. Every scribe is taught that line on
  their first day, and every scribe learns in time how thin it is.
- **Paper before stroke.** No student writes until she has pressed paper for three years. A ward is only as strong as
  what it is written on: birch bark, resin and melted frost. The best paper uses Hàm Nguyệt's pearl dust.
- **The lanterns.** Every autumn the lantern-walker carries the ward lanterns round the lake, hangs one in every village
  and collects what the village can pay. Many pay in fish. The head does not count it.
- **Fire.** The pines forbid open fire under the canopy. The pavilion's ink is burned at the charcoal village, and its
  lanterns are carried down unlit and lit on the shore.
- **Dress and food.** Undyed linen, ink to the second knuckle, a birch-bark hat in the paper yards. Pine nuts, millet and
  lake fish from the lantern payments.
- **The dead.** A dead scribe's last sheet is burned on the shore in a lantern and the ash scattered on the lake. The
  founder's last sheet was a ward she had not finished, and the pavilion keeps it unburned.
- **Taboos:** writing a method of the pines; writing on paper you did not press; lighting fire under the canopy;
  breaking the founder's box. **Outsiders** may buy wards, never brushes.

**Why it fits:** the outer method draws Wood from bark and Water from frost while the student makes paper. The canon
writes the cultivator's own meridians in Wood ink on a body of Water, so that qi can be laid on paper and lit later. In a
fight it lays wards in advance that root and wound the foe each time it acts. Thorn and Ink Verses is taught as the ink: the
Wood stacks that make a ward bite. The Lantern Ward holds a winter in a paper lantern, and in a fight it sets a light that
heals allies and slows foes. The canon is written, the one ward canon the pines let a vassal write for them, and it stops at the Profound
tier because the founder would not write anything deeper.

## 6. Admission

- **Outer gate:** Wood or Water 30 or more, and three years of paper. Children come from the bark and kiln villages, and
  from the shore on the lantern-walker's pole.
- **Inner gate:** Wood 45 or more, and one ward that holds a night.
- **Exceptions:** none. The pines' paper-maker Lư Chỉ Mặc (`tung_dao`) works in the paper yards as Tùng Đào's own.

## 8. Relations

- **Tùng Đào Cung (lord):** the pavilion exists because Đàm Tùng Lại let a ward-writer of his Array Hall write (V007). It
  makes the palace's wards and the River Hall's sluice wards. The pines check its paper: Lư Chỉ Mặc counts every sheet.
  Cận Tùng Hàn of the Array Hall orders its wards and is dying. His successor Khuông Hàn Ly wants the pavilion to write
  the songs the hall has lost. Văn does not tell him he may have three.
- **Hàm Nguyệt Trang:** the pavilion's lanterns hang over the pearl beds, and Kiều Nguyệt Châu pays in pearl dust, which
  goes into the strongest wards. Its lantern-walker drinks with Hàm Nguyệt's divers.
- **Vạn Hác Viện:** the other house under the pines that writes, music in tablature. Văn buys its worn zither strings for
  lantern threads.
- **The lake villages and Băng Tâm's north-shore fishers:** the lanterns hang on both sides of the arbitration line
  (C171), and the north shore pays in fish like the south. Cao Hàn Chúc grew up among Băng Tâm's people.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The founder's box.** Three ward-scripts the founder copied in 1150 from the wards an old singer of the Array Hall laid
  with three songs; she never heard the songs, and he died the next year with them (N-tung_dao-05). Written as wards they were allowed. Read as songs
  they are the canon written down. Only Văn knows what is in the box. He is teaching his disciple Chu Ánh Tuyết to read
  the founder's hand and has not told her why.
- **No Golden Core.** The pavilion has been led by a Trúc Cơ for thirty years. Văn cannot teach a Trúc Cơ. Nhạc Hoa Bì,
  who expected the headship in 1170, thinks the pavilion needs a stronger head. She does not want the post herself any
  more.
- **The debts.** Nhạc says Văn gives the lake villages too many lanterns and lets their debts stand. He has never refused
  a lantern.
- **The ice wall.** Hạng Băng Tường wants to write the ward that would have saved Nghênh Nguyệt. The Lantern Ward stops
  ice drifting ashore. He wants one that would stop a wall of it.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The box.** Cận Tùng Hàn dies, and Khuông Hàn Ly comes to the pavilion asking for any ward that might hold a lost
   song. Văn can open the founder's box and give the Array Hall its songs back, which would admit the pavilion wrote the
   canon down, or he can lie to a dying hall. (Văn Hàn Đăng, Chu Ánh Tuyết, Khuông Hàn Ly, Cận Tùng Hàn; places 948, 2.)
2. **Two written canons.** If Vi Thanh Âm's zither is opened (`tung_dao.md` hook 2), the pines search every house that
   writes. The founder's box is the next thing they will find. (Văn Hàn Đăng, Hà Thính Tùng, Sử Trường Ngâm.)
3. **The wall comes back.** A spring storm drives the ice ashore in a wall again, this time at Tịch Ba Thôn. Hạng Băng
   Tường and Khổng Dạ Hành have one night to write and hang a ward that has never held. (Hạng Băng Tường, Khổng Dạ Hành,
   Văn Hàn Đăng; places 944, 943.)
4. **The north-shore lanterns.** Băng Tâm's skiff master objects to Tùng Đào's vassal hanging lanterns on his side of the
   arbitration line, or he wants them for his own villages. (Khổng Dạ Hành, Cao Hàn Chúc, Lục Hàn Chu; places 974, 976.)
5. **The pearl paper.** Hàm Nguyệt's pearl dust stops coming when the manor's books are opened. The palace's strongest
   wards are due before winter. (Văn Hàn Đăng, Nhạc Hoa Bì, Kiều Nguyệt Châu.)
6. **The founder's last sheet.** The unfinished ward on the founder's desk begins to glow on the anniversary of her death.
   Chu Ánh Tuyết can read enough of the hand to see that it is not a ward. (Chu Ánh Tuyết, Văn Hàn Đăng, Quản Sương Thư.)
7. **Fire at the kilns.** A kiln at Thán Diêu catches the pines, the gravest crime on the ground, and the Coiled Root Hall
   wants the ink-maker's name. (Quản Sương Thư, Nghê Tùng Yên, Nhiếp Cô Tân; place 932.)
