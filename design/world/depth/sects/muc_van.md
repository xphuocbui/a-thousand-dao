A manor, not a hall. The herders of the western plain founded it in 740 to breed spirit horses that run like clouds
(A166), and it still lives by the herd. Its horses win every race at Lạc Hà's festival (A172). Lạc Hà sends it the
outer-gate children who ride better than they cultivate, and hires its riders when it needs hard bodies. Its head has
been quietly deducting three stones from the tribute since 1121, with interest.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R3/R4): stay, and become no-lean.** The seat is wet grassland by the plain's rivers: Water 46 %, Wood 16 %,
Earth 15 %, no Wind. It fails both old leans. Wind is an innate variant with no required element (Linh Căn rule 9), and
R1b raises its odds only where the land holds wind qi. No place in the manor's ground has any: the highest Earth is
Song Tỉnh Thôn (40 %) and Khoáng Dã Mạch (41 %), and wind is 0 everywhere. A Wind-lean sect here could recruit only by
luck, and there is no seat to move to. Re-leaning to Water would make the manor a river sect it has never been.

The manor's specialty has always been the body (thể tu). Its herders ride the long ride and its head cultivates on
horseback. So it gives up the elements: `leans: []`, a body lineage, recruitment by bone and by the herd. The Wind
lean survives as what it always was, a gift of the head and of one elder, and a book kept for the rare Wind-born child
(section 5). Nothing moves; nothing is repainted.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** A no-lean sect looks like its region. The manor's ground is the river grass
of Trường Thiên Nguyên (region 11), Water 28-46 % near the seat and the coast, with the steppe villages inland running to
Earth 30-41 % (Song Tỉnh, Mục Địch, Thảo Lang Nguyên). So the manor's people are Water-rooted river children and
Earth-rooted steppe children in about equal numbers, with Wood from the farm towns (Thanh Điền, Điền Viên). None of that
matters to the herd. Wind turns up once in a generation, and the head and Thôi Dương Giác are the whole of it.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **A horse is a disciple's first master.** The founder's first page. A child admitted to the manor sleeps in the
  stable for a year, and the herd decides whether they stay. A colt that will not let a child near it is a verdict the
  head does not overrule.
- **The long ride.** The body canon is cultivated on horseback. A disciple rides a day and a night at the gallop, one
  hour more for each layer, and comes back or does not. The founder went out on his last long ride at 370, and the
  horse came home without him. The manor counts that as a good death.
- **The herd is not fenced.** The manor has no walls round its grass. The horses run the open plain and come to the
  herd call. Fencing a herd, beating a horse, and selling a horse to anyone who has beaten one are the three things
  the manor forbids.
- **Dress.** Felt coats in grass-brown and undyed wool, sheepskin in winter. A rider who has won at the lord's festival
  braids the ribbon into their hair and wears it until it rots.
- **Food.** Mare's milk, mutton, millet, salt from Diêm Điền. The manor never eats horse, even in the cold year, when
  people did.
- **The dead** are buried in the open grass with their horse's halter, with no stone, so the herd can graze over them.
- **Contracts.** Lạc Hà prices everything and has taught its vassals to. The manor learned the lesson exactly, and its
  steward seals every account at sunset as Lạc Hà's law requires. That is the joke of the tribute (section 9).
- **Outsiders** are welcome to the races and to buy horses. A horse is sold only to a buyer it lets near it, and the
  manor will not argue about it.

**Why it fits.** The bone art roots a disciple like a mountain, which is the first thing a rider needs. The founder's
canon is the gallop made into a method: momentum, a beast in step, and a grip that holds. It needs bone, not root, so
any steppe or river child with a good frame can learn it (memory: sustain is king; the canon trades a slow start for a
body that does not tire). The Herd Call turns the head's horses in a fight. The Gale Courier Scripture came down from
Phù Dao Quán in the cold year. Only the head and Thôi Dương Giác can run it, and she has taught it to no one.

## 6. Admission (R3)

- **By bone and the herd.** Any root. A child is tested by căn cốt (body talent) and by a year in the stable. The
  herd's verdict counts for more than the test.
- **Placement Day:** Lạc Hà sends its riders, children who failed the dusk test three times but sit a horse well, of
  any root. Tiết Bộ Vân came this spring with Fire and no Wood.
- **Wind-born:** a child with Wind is sent to Thôi Dương Giác. There has been none since Khấu.
- **Exceptions:** the groom Hứa Tịch Câu (tạp dịch, hired). In a no-lean sect the R2 gate does not apply, so the
  exception only marks that he is not taught the canon.

## 8. Relations

- **Lạc Hà Tông (lord):** a fond lord and a running quarrel. The manor gives Lạc Hà its remounts, its riders, its
  festival and the hard bodies the Geese Hall hires. Khấu's horses win every race and Kim Vạn Lý loses his bet anyway
  (A172). The tribute has been "late" since 1121, and the Hall of Scales' collector Cận Thủ Chuyết has twice been put
  off the ranch. Hách Ánh Hà bets on the horses and keeps the quarrel from reaching the head. Tô Vãn Chiếu was the girl
  who saw the three stones shorted in 901, and the manor believes she has never said Khấu is wrong.
- **Trầm Tịch Dương:** the debtor. He shorted Khấu's father three stones in 901 and will not pay them, because paying
  them would admit it. The interest is now nearly five thousand stones.
- **Kim Vạn Lý (loose, Nhạn Thanh Thành):** a friend of the races. He bought the plain's wool in 1040 (A170), bets every
  festival, and has a grandson in the stables learning to lose bets more slowly.
- **Thiên Bảo Trai (fellow vassal):** sold Khấu the white bear. The manor dislikes how the bear was got and distrusts
  its buyer, Hồ Tầm Kỳ, and it buys nothing else there.
- **Nhạn Trận Môn (fellow vassal):** the goose gate drills on foot on the same plain and buys no horses. Khấu's riders
  and the gate's files have never fought together, and each thinks the other would lose.
- **Phất Y Cốc and Cửu Cao Phái (the Pivot's Wind vassals):** strangers. Thôi Dương Giác has written to both (her
  secret).
- **Phù Dao Quán (fallen 1121):** the Whirlwind abbey on the steppe at 496. Lạc Hà weighed it for its new seat and passed
  it over as "the vassal's"; the manor holds its book, its pennant and its last junior, and Thôi wants the ruin.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The three stones.** In 901, at the horse fair below Lạc Hà's seat, Lạc Hà's treasurer Trầm Tịch Dương shorted the
  herder Khấu Thường Thanh three stones, and a market girl of eleven named Tô Vãn Chiếu said so in front of them both.
  Trầm took her into the inner gate for it. The herder forgave it the same day. His son did not. After the cold year, when
  the Counting House would not delay the tribute, Khấu began paying it less the three stones with interest at Lạc Hà's
  own posted rate for late debts. His daughter Khấu Thanh Thảo has done the sum each spring since and sealed it at
  sunset. It now comes to more than a year's tribute. The Hall of Scales cannot find the error, because there is none.
  The case can be closed only by Trầm paying three stones by his own hand, and Khấu wants that at a festival.
- **The horse and the man.** Đạp Vân, the spirit horse born on Khấu's night (A169), is 260 and failing. Khấu believes
  they share one Wind and that he will not outlive it. The manor tells time by the horse now: Đạp Vân walks a shorter
  round of the north paddock each week, and Khấu has had his long-ride saddle restitched.
- **The abbey's book.** Thôi Dương Giác has written to the Pivot's Wind sects offering the Phù Dao book for three
  Wind-born disciples. If Lạc Hà's Vassal Hall learns of it, the manor is in breach.
- **The bear.** The white bear from Vô Tung Lâm (A142) lives in the north paddock with ice in its straw. It frightens
  the mares, and the herd master wants it gone. It loves the groom and the head's grandson, and nobody knows what it
  eats in winter.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **Three stones at the festival.** Khấu wants Trầm Tịch Dương to pay three stones by his own hand at the next Lạc Hà
   festival. The player can carry the account, broker a face-saving payment through Hách Ánh Hà, or help Cận Thủ
   Chuyết finally collect. Tô Vãn Chiếu is the only witness left. (Khấu, Khấu Thanh Thảo, Trầm, Hách, Cận, Tô; places
   19, 74, 558.)
2. **The last long ride.** Đạp Vân sickens. Khấu sets out on a long ride he does not mean to come back from. Tần Thiên
   Lý asks the player to follow, and Thôi Dương Giác knows what the book says about horse and rider. (Khấu, Tần, Thôi;
   the open grass, 492.)
3. **The Whirlwind abbey.** Thôi wants Phù Dao Quán (496) rebuilt with Wind-born children. A player with Wind is
   exactly what she needs. A letter answered by Phất Y Cốc arrives at the wrong desk in Lạc Hà's Vassal Hall. (Thôi,
   Mạc Khiên Phong, Hách; places 19, 496.)
4. **The white bear.** The bear pines in the heat. Hứa Tịch Câu and Khấu Tiểu Câu want to walk it home to Vô Tung Lâm
   in the north. Hồ Tầm Kỳ of Thiên Bảo Trai wants it back for a buyer who wants its gall. (Hứa, Khấu Tiểu Câu, Hồ Tầm Kỳ;
   places 19, 68.)
5. **Race the Geese.** Dư Bôn Vân challenges the Geese Hall's cadets on their own field. Thịnh Thiếu Hàng will not race
   a vassal, and Thịnh Kinh Hàn, who drills him, thinks he should. (Dư, Thịnh Thiếu Hàng; place 38.)
6. **The horse that will not be sold.** A rich buyer from the Pivot's lands wants a spirit colt that will not let him
   near it. Kim Vạn Lý offers to "arrange" it, and the manor's third taboo is tested. (Kim Vạn Lý, Kim Đắc Lộc, Tần.)
7. **The cold year again.** Geese fly low over Nhạn Thanh Thành, as they did in 1120 (A171). The herders read it as a
   warning. If the snow comes, the manor needs the tribute delayed, and the Counting House has a long memory. (Khấu
   Thanh Thảo, Tông Thủ Tín; places 19, 38.)
