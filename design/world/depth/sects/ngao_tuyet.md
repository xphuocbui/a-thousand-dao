A school of swordsmen who train barefoot in the snow. It is ninety-five years old and its founder still runs it. Its
sword is the winter plum, which blooms on a hard branch when nothing else will, and its founder's rule is that a child
without Metal in their root will never be a sword. He turns them away himself at the plum gate. He was once one of them.

**The story.** The tiger-stripe ore of Thạch Khẩu ran thin and a spirit tiger took the last miners, and in 1161 the
town was empty (V053). In the spring of 1162 the tiger took the founder's daughter, Lăng Nhược Mai, on the mountain road.
Lăng hunted it to Hổ Khiếu Cương (242) and killed it, and that summer he moved the sword hall into the empty smelting
houses, because the mountain still held iron and the plum needs a hard branch (**N-ngao_tuyet-01**, **-02**). His sworn
brother Trác Thiết Cán, a miner's son of the town, lit the forge again. The plum garden above Hàn Mai became the gate.
Every winter the outer disciples come down to it and train barefoot under the plums.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** The mountain villages round the ore town (Ma Bố, Vân Biên, Tầng Điền, Thạch
Lâu: Metal 12-17 %, Earth 15-18 %) breed some Metal. That is little anywhere in the pines, but it is the most Metal in
Cửu Khúc Xuyên, and the school's disciples come from them. The plum villages below (Hàn Mai, Wood 50 %, Metal 7 %) breed
Wood. Their children climb to the gate every winter, and a child of Hàn Mai who passes, like Phí Tầm Mai, is talked
about for years. The founder himself was born in Hàn Mai.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **A blossom needs a hard branch.** The school's one teaching, from the plum that blooms in the snow: hardness first,
  then beauty, and beauty only at the worst moment. Its sword fights hurt and opens when it is losing.
- **Defy the snow.** Disciples go barefoot in every season, train in the snow at dawn, and do not light fires in their
  sleeping houses. The founder says the cold drives qi to the bone. Most of the outer gate has chilblains.
- **No Metal, no sword.** At the plum gate every child's root is read. Metal 30 or more and they are taken; less, and
  they are sent away, often by the founder himself. It is the school's hardest rule, and the pines' outer gate is full
  of the children it turned away.
- **Five petals.** Every sword form has five movements, named for the five petals of the plum. A disciple who reaches
  the fifth petal of the canon has a plum blossom etched on their sword by the smith.
- **The dead.** Buried under the plums of the gate, one tree to a grave. Lăng Nhược Mai's tree is the oldest after
  the founder's own.
- **Dress and food.** White hemp in every season, a plum-branch pin, bare feet. Millet, salted meat, mountain tea; the
  school eats one hot meal a day.
- **Taboos:** wearing shoes in the sword hall; lighting a fire in a sleeping house; carrying a sword the smith did not
  make; breaking a plum branch. **Outsiders:** duellists are welcome and are expected to lose. Others are given tea at
  the gate.

**Why it fits:** the canon tempers the meridians with Metal until they will not bend and then runs a thread of Wood
along them: a hard branch and a blossom. In a fight its five petals land Metal hits and leave Wood bleeds, and it
strikes harder below half health. The Iron Hide Manual is the branch. The Lone Branch Sword is a widower's art for
fighting alone. The mountain's iron is why the canon could be finished. The Profound tier suits a school ninety-five
years old whose founder has not formed a Golden Core.

## 6. Admission

- **Outer gate:** Metal 30 or more (the founder's rule, stricter than R2's Metal or Wood 30). Read at the plum gate in
  the first frost. Children walk up from Hàn Mai and the mountain villages.
- **Inner gate:** Metal 45 or more, and the five thrusts of the primer done barefoot on the ice of the ore tailings.
- **Exceptions:** none. The founder has never made one, and the one exception the school could have made, its founder,
  is a secret.

## 8. Relations

- **Tùng Đào Cung (lord):** sworn since the founding (V051). The pines' outer gate takes in the Hàn Mai children the
  school turns away, and Ninh Hàn Chi has gone there to prove Lăng wrong. The envoy Chử Lan Giang brings the oath cup
  every new year and lets the founder beat him at swords. The Coiled Root Hall's duellists, Nhiếp Vãn Đào above all, are
  the opponents La Thiết Mai dreams of.
- **Tuần Tùng Đường:** the school's young men walk a season to Tùng Hạ Thị every few autumns to pick fights with the road wardens.
  Hứa Vãn Cung beat Ân Lãnh Phong with a staff in 1198, and the school has not forgiven it.
- **Thải Chi Cốc:** sets the school's broken bones. The valley-mistress thinks the barefoot rule is cruel.
- **The sword sage Tư Không Hạc Niên** (`tu_khong_hac_nien`), who taught the founder for three winters and gave him the
  fruit that put Metal in his root. The founder has not seen him since 1088.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The founder's root.** Lăng Đạp Tuyết was born with no Metal. Tư Không Hạc Niên gave him a Spirit Root Fruit (Linh
  Căn Quả) on the third winter, and he raised the thread of Metal it left by marrow-washing over sixty years. He owes
  the sage the fruit and has told no one. Every child he turns away is the child he was. If it came out,
  his rule would fall. Phó Ngạo Sương would open the gate, and Ninh Hàn Chi would come back.
- **The heir he will not name.** Phó Ngạo Sương reached Trúc Cơ in 1160 and has been his equal since. Trác Thiết Cán
  says she should be heir. Lăng says no one is ready, meaning that no one is better than he is, and she is.
- **The road of 1162.** Phó Ngạo Sương was with Lăng Nhược Mai when the tiger came, and lived. The founder has never
  asked her what happened, and she has never told him.
- **The iron.** The mountain's ore is thin. Trác Thiết Cán knows the seam he forges from will last perhaps forty years.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The child he was.** Ninh Hàn Chi learns, from a letter of Phí Tầm Mai's or from the sword sage, that the founder was
   born without Metal. She comes back to the plum gate with the truth. (Ninh Hàn Chi, Phí Tầm Mai, Lăng Đạp Tuyết, Mạc
   Chiết Chi; place 284.)
2. **The sword sage.** Tư Không Hạc Niên passes through Cửu Khúc Xuyên. Lăng wants a Golden Core and the sage knows where
   the fruit came from and what it is owed. (Lăng Đạp Tuyết, Tư Không Hạc Niên, Phó Ngạo Sương.)
3. **The road of 1162.** Phó Ngạo Sương finally tells what happened on the mountain road: Nhược Mai ran toward the tiger
   to give her time. The founder must decide whether that makes her his heir or his enemy. (Phó Ngạo Sương, Lăng Đạp
   Tuyết, Trác Thiết Cán.)
4. **The last seam.** Trác Thiết Cán's seam runs out, and the iron of Thạch Khẩu is gone. The school needs Metal ground,
   ore, or the chain links the Cổ Độ smiths drag from the river (A061). (Trác Thiết Cán, Dư Hổ Văn; places 1262, 61, 229.)
5. **The duel at Tùng Hạ.** Ân Lãnh Phong challenges Hứa Vãn Cung again, this time with a real sword. The road wardens
   want an apology and the founder wants a better duel. (Ân Lãnh Phong, Hứa Vãn Cung, Phó Ngạo Sương; places 46, 376.)
6. **A second tiger.** Something has taken a charcoal-burner on Hổ Khiếu Cương, and the miners' grandsons say the tiger of
   1162 had a mate. (Lăng Đạp Tuyết, Trác Thiết Cán, La Thiết Mai; place 242.)
7. **The sealed abbey.** Cô Hạc Quán's door (V049) is four tiles from the iron town. La Thiết Mai has found a way in.
   (La Thiết Mai, Ân Lãnh Phong; place 1266.)
8. **The pines' duellist.** La Thiết Mai walks to the palace to challenge Nhiếp Vãn Đào. Winning makes the school famous;
   losing gives the Root a reason to look at its vassal's swords. (La Thiết Mai, Nhiếp Vãn Đào, Lăng Đạp Tuyết.)
