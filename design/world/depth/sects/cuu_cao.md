"The crane cries in the nine marshes, and its voice is heard in heaven" (Kinh Thi). The school is a flock with people
attached. The grooms are marsh villagers who stand in the reeds all summer and raise the birds. The riders are
plateau children born with Wind, the only root a crane will carry. They fly the Pivot's letters, circle its vein day
and night, and follow the cranes south every autumn to winter on the windiest ground in the heartland.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): stay, keep Wind, and admit riders by the variant.** This is the honest answer, and it breaks the fit
test.

- **Why not move.** The school's chronicle, craft and people are bound to the nine marshes of Dao Quang Lĩnh: it was
  founded there (C140), the cranes land at Hạc Quy every spring (A089), it holds Phá Quân's old ground nearby (C163),
  and it circles Tinh Tủy Mạch 24 tiles away (A096). No place in region 6 has any Wind. The five wind places on the
  continent are 114-118 tiles south on the Pivot's plateau (Thương Ưng Đài 236, Tập Trận Khoáng 603) or in Thính Vũ's
  mountains (823, 827, 853). The plateau pair has no Wood to speak of (12-14 %); Ủng Vân Phong (823, Wind 35, Wood 17)
  would fit Wind, Wood, but it is Thính Vũ's ground.
- **Why not re-lean.** A Wood-only school would be a school of grooms. Wind is innate and needs no element (Linh Căn
  rule 9). It is not drawn from the marsh; it is born in a child, and R1b says it is born more often on wind land.
- **What the school does.** The seat stays in the marsh for the summer flock. The cranes winter on the plateau, and
  the school keeps a winter hall there on Thương Ưng Đài, the Grey Hawk Terrace, where the wind never stops. Its riders
  are found there, among the hawk-catchers' camps and the miners of Tập Trận Khoáng (Wind 31). The founder was a
  hawk-catcher of the terrace. The head was born in the marsh with Wind against the odds (A094). Every other rider
  since has been a plateau child.
- **The rule problem.** The R4 test asks a variant lean to have 5 variant qi at the seat. For an innate variant that
  is the wrong place to look: it should look where the sect recruits. Reported to the user.

**Cost:** none on the map. The winter terrace at 236 is a beast lair, unheld, inside the Pivot's territory. Proposal:
record it as a Cửu Cao outpost, granted by the Pivot (**N-cuu_cao-01**), with a seasonal route 14 ↔ 236.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** The marsh forest is Wood 31-56 % and Water 15-44 % (Hạc Quy 112 Wood 44
Water 24; Lý Ngư 1296; Mộc Phiệt 1318 Wood 52). Its children are Wood-rooted far more often than the even rate and
almost never Wind-rooted. They become grooms, and the best of them, Mai Lô Tuyết, has outgrown every rider and still
cannot fly. The plateau terrace is Earth 42 % with Wind 20, and the mine Wind 31. Under R1b a child born there is three
to four times likelier than elsewhere to carry Wind, and comes with an Earth root under it. So the school has two
peoples: green-rooted grooms of the marsh and brown-rooted riders of the plateau.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **The crane chooses.** A crane will not carry a rider who has no Wind, and the school does not argue with cranes.
  Riders are not ranked above grooms in anything but the air. Mai Lô Tuyết, a groom, is the second strongest
  cultivator in the school.
- **Shared breath.** A rider is bonded to one crane by breathing with it on its first flight. A crane bonds once, and
  when its rider dies it does not take another. Nhan Tuyết Linh's crane lived ninety years after 872 and never flew
  with anyone.
- **The migration.** In the ninth month the flock and the riders fly south to the plateau terrace, and in spring they
  come back to Hạc Quy on the same day (A089). The grooms stay in the marsh. A rider's year is half north and half
  south, and most riders have two homes and a family in neither.
- **The ledger.** Every flight since 700 is written in the school's ledger: who flew, where, what they saw. The
  founder kept it himself for four centuries. It is the most exact record of the north that exists, and nobody but
  the riders has ever asked to read it.
- **Food, dress, burial.** Grooms eat carp, lotus root and rice from the marsh; riders eat what the hawk-catchers eat.
  Riders wear grey felt and crane-down cloaks; grooms wear reed capes. The dead are laid out on the marsh platforms for
  the sky, and their bones are buried in the reeds in spring.
- **The cry.** The crane's cry is the school's signal and its one sound art. The Pivot does not count it as music.
- **Taboos:** caging a crane; riding another rider's bird; eating crane or heron; opening a letter carried.
- **Outsiders** are welcome at the marsh and fed. Nobody but a rider is ever taken up.

**Why it fits.** The Gale Courier Scripture was written by couriers who outran storms, and the school's riders are the
Pivot's couriers; there is no better method for them in the catalogue. The grooms' breath is a marsh method on the
marsh's Wood. The bond rite joins the two: Wind to lift the bird and Wood to hold the rider on it.

## 6. Admission

- **Grooms (outer gate):** Wood 30, or any Wind. Grooms come from the marsh villages, Hạc Quy first. A groom stays a
  groom at any realm: the inner gate is the air, and the air needs Wind.
- **Riders (inner gate and above):** Wind 45 or more (R2), and a crane that will carry them. A Wind child below 45 is a
  groom, however well the cranes take to them. Each winter the master of
  the terrace walks the hawk-catchers' camps on Thương Ưng Đài and the mine of Tập Trận Khoáng for Wind children. A
  child the cranes come down to is a rider.
- **The quarrel:** Phất Y Cốc wants the same children for its founder's art. In 1195 it got one first.
- **Exceptions:** none named.

## 8. Relations

- **Thiên Xu Tông (lord, loyalty 55):** the school is the Pivot's post and its eyes. Its cranes carry the envoy **Vi
  Thừa Phong** to the vassals (thien_xu §8) and circle Tinh Tủy Mạch for the warden **Hình Thủ Tinh**, who flies the
  night circuit with the head. Since 1189 the circuit has worn out eleven old cranes, and the grooms blame the Pivot.
  The head holds the one record that proves the vein went unwatched in 803-806 (N-cuu_cao-02), which would undo the
  Pivot's 1188 ruling.
- **Họa Tinh Đường:** the talisman hall 34 tiles west, whose talismans the riders carry. **Kỷ Ngân Chúc** and the
  head are friends.
- **Phất Y Cốc:** rivals for the plateau's Wind children. **Thạch Tàng Danh** took Lộ Khinh Trần in 1195; **Cừ Vân
  Lý** does not forgive it.
- **Phá Quân Môn** (fallen, 874): the school holds its ground and carries its dead rider's story. One groom, **Hạ
  Tri Hàn**, carries the rebel founder's surname in secret.
- **Băng Tâm Các:** whose border the cranes watch; the riders see the Pavilion's patrols from the air every night.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The ledger.** Ung Hạc Minh read the 803-806 pages in 1189 while planning the vein circuit. If she gives them to
  Hình Thủ Tinh, the Pivot's head is shown to have ruled on a false fact. If she gives them to no one, they stay in a
  chest in the marsh hall. Băng Tâm's envoy would pay anything for them.
- **The circuit.** The grooms want the old cranes home. **Đồng Dược Hương** has buried eleven and says so to every
  Pivot rider. **Mai Lô Tuyết** counts them and says nothing.
- **Riders and grooms.** The riders are few, young and from somewhere else; the grooms are many, old and from here.
  The head is the one person who is both.
- **The roc.** **Khổng Tường Vũ** means to ride the roc of Bằng Sào Nhai, which would be the first beast the school
  has flown that is not a crane.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The ledger.** Băng Tâm's envoy Ngụy Thanh Hàn learns that the crane school writes down every flight. He wants
   the 803-806 pages. The head wants her cranes home from the circuit. The Pivot wants neither. (Ung Hạc Minh, Ngụy
   Thanh Hàn, Hình Thủ Tinh, Thích Minh Thư; places 14, 205.)
2. **The night circuit.** Flying the vein with Ung Hạc Minh and Hình Thủ Tinh, the player sees lights on the Băng Tâm
   side of the border. Ung writes them in the ledger; Hình wants them left out. (Ung, Hình, Mộ Viễn Sơn; place 205.)
3. **A Wind child.** A child the cranes come down to is reported at the hawk camps. Cừ Vân Lý and a Phất Y sword set
   out the same morning. (Cừ Vân Lý, Lộ Khinh Trần, Thạch Tàng Danh; places 236, 603.)
4. **The crane king.** Thanh Điền throws Vi Thừa Phong a third time, over a Pivot letter that matters. Someone has to
   carry it, and the king will carry only Ung. (Ung, Vi Thừa Phong, Tống Phi Thư.)
5. **The roc.** Khổng Tường Vũ asks the player to help her reach the roc of Bằng Sào Nhai. Tống Phi Thư asks the
   player to stop her. (Khổng, Tống; place 234.)
6. **The Wolf Star's name.** Hạ Tri Hàn's family spearhead carries the Wolf Star mark of Phá Quân Môn's armoury.
   If he is Hạ Lang Tinh's blood, the school holds the rebel's ground and the rebel's heir, and the Pivot's first
   elder Đỗ Tinh Hà, who took the rebel's spear in 874, will want to know. (Hạ Tri Hàn, Mai Lô Tuyết, Đỗ Tinh Hà;
   places 1340, 218.)
7. **Eleven cranes.** Đồng Dược Hương buries a twelfth crane worn out by the circuit and goes to the Pivot's seat to
   complain in person. (Đồng, Mai Lô Tuyết, Hình Thủ Tinh, Doãn Ngọc Hành; seat 5.)
