The hall takes its name from Vương Bột's couplet: the painted beams at dawn fly with the clouds of the southern shore;
the pearl curtains at dusk roll up the western hills' rain. It is a school, not a fortress. Its scribes paint
talismans, copy contracts, sell charms at Hạc Đinh Thị and supply Lạc Hà's war chest with paper. It is the only house
in Lạc Hà's west that works at dawn. Since the Ferry War it has been the least loyal of the eight vassals, because
its mistress learned what her talismans had burned.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): stay.** The seat is temperate forest on the river shore: Wood 34 %, Water 26 %, Earth 17 %, density
1.42, the best of the four seats in this batch. It fits Wood, Water by the audit. Nothing moves.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** Ngư Ca Xuyên's forest and river villages run Wood 30-43 % with Water 17-36 %:
Phong Lâm, Hoa Khê, Ngân Than, Sơn Trà, Tang Ma Thành. Children born there are Wood-rooted far more often than not, and
the hall's inner gate (Wood 45) is a forest people: mulberry pickers, bark-beaters, scholars' children from Phong Lâm.
The river mouths (Phong Lâm Trấn 36 % Water, Phương Thảo 27 %) send the Water children to the outer gate. The karst to the
north (U Lan Cốc, Oanh Hồi Hạp, the Jade Milk spring at 249) carries Darkness at 16-26 variant qi. A Water and Earth
child born there may wake Darkness, and one scribe at the spring already has.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **A talisman is a promise to heaven.** The hall's teaching: a stroke of qi on paper binds the writer to what the
  paper does. A scribe answers for every talisman they paint, wherever it is used. This is why Đào Sa broke the
  mistress's trust in her lord, and why the hall is righteous in a neutral lord's land.
- **Dawn.** The scribes rise before light and paint the morning cloud as it comes off the river. Lạc Hà does its
  sacred work at dusk; Nam Phố does its at dawn. The two houses tease each other about it, and Lạc Hà's dusk councils
  are always over before the hall's scribes wake.
- **Grinding ink.** Every disciple grinds ink for an hour before dawn, every day, from the first day to the last. The
  hall-mistress grinds her own.
- **Paper and dress.** The hall's paper is Tang Ma mulberry bark. Scribes wear undyed hemp, mulberry-grey, with ink to the
  elbow; the hall-mistress pins her hair with a brush. The dusk line wears violet at the hem.
- **The stamp.** Every talisman the hall sells carries the cloud stamp of the founder's seal. A talisman without it is
  not Nam Phố's, and the hall will say so to anyone who asks.
- **The dead** are wrapped in their own last unfinished talisman and buried under the maples of Phong Lâm, facing east.
- **Taboos:** painting a talisman without knowing what it is for (since 1173); selling an unstamped talisman; painting
  at dusk in the dawn line's hall; burning paper with writing on it.
- **Outsiders** like the hall: it is honest, cheap and polite. Lạc Hà's Counting House finds it slow and lately
  forgetful with war orders.

**Why it fits.** The primer is ink and paper, Water from the well and Wood from the bark, so it takes either of the
river's elements. The canon draws the waking forest's Wood into the brush at dawn and fights with laid paper: Wood
stacks that bind, wards that heal, and a long fight in which the talismans do the work (memory: sustain is king). The
Thorn and Ink Verses are wandering singers' blood-ink charms, and the hall uses them for its contract seals. The founders'
art has a dawn half (Wood) and a dusk half (Water). The dawn half is whole in the mistress's line. The dusk half has
been fragments since 1060, held by Kha Triêu Phi.

## 6. Admission (R2)

- **Outer gate:** Wood or Water at 30, and a steady hand. The hall recruits in the forest villages and at its stall in
  Hạc Đinh Thị, and takes Lạc Hà's Wood and Water children on Placement Day (Phùng Lục Ý, 1196).
- **Inner gate and above:** Wood at 45.
- **Exceptions:** none named. Đới Huyền Nghiên's Darkness woke after he joined, and he still passes on his Wood. If it
  grows, he is the hall's first marked member (R2: a root changed after joining).

## 8. Relations

- **Lạc Hà Tông (lord):** a loyal hall that has stopped trusting. Lạc Hà buys its talismans from Nam Phố rather than
  admit crafters off its own root, and its contract copyists are Nam Phố-trained. In 1173 the Counting House ordered a
  hundred fire talismans "for the reed beds at Thủy Khẩu", and Lô Xướng Vãn's raiders burned Lãng Đào's boats at Đào
  Sa with them (A080). Châu has painted no fire talisman since. Four war orders have been "lost". In 1190 Tô Mộ Vân,
  fourteen, took the prize seal at Hạc Đinh Thị from the hall's champion after eleven contests (A203). Châu was glad,
  and has asked twice to borrow the girl for a season. This spring she asked for the placed boy Tạ Quy Yến by name.
  Kha Triêu Phi and Hách Ánh Hà trade poems and favours, and Kha is the hall's friend at court.
- **Lãng Đào Môn (Tùng Đào's ferry vassal):** the enemy's boats. Châu sends Giang Cửu Khúc unsigned wards every year
  (her secret).
- **The Tạ family of Phong Lâm (A193, A200):** one Tạ is in the outer gate (Tạ Quy Hồng), and another (Tạ Quy Yến) is
  wanted.
- **Tang Ma Thành:** the hall's paper and its people. The mill made the blessing papers for the new rope bridges in
  1174 (A202).
- **Yên Quang Các (fellow vassal):** Tào Mộ Tử's evening-herb alchemists sit on the steppe above the river. They work at
  dusk and the hall at dawn, and they get on.
- **Thiên Bảo Trai (fellow vassal):** sells the hall's best talismans at its auction for a fee the hall thinks too high.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The dark ink.** **Đới Huyền Nghiên**'s ink from the Jade Milk spring holds longer and stains darker every year, and
  its talismans are the best the hall sells. Everyone at the hall knows what it is. In 1097 **Châu Phi Vân**, not
  yet mistress, gave Sào Ẩn Lan a year's stones and sent her up the karst for the same darkening. Now Đới
  paints at night in the ink-house at the spring, where the dark takes best, and the hall pays him by the sheet. **Hàn
  Tiểu Chúc**, who carries the ink down, paints his own village's stern-lamp charms with it because they hold three
  times as long, and his hands are grey to the wrist.
- **The war chest.** Châu has painted no fire talisman since Đào Sa, and four of Lạc Hà's war orders have been "lost".
  The hall is the least loyal of the eight, and its lord's orders must be met. This year Châu fills them with Đới's
  sheets and does not ask what they are for. It is the taboo she wrote after 1173, broken with open eyes. She sets the
  count each season and has raised it twice since spring. **Văn Tang Chỉ** knows it by the reams he sends up from the
  mill.
- **Who knew.** **Kha Triêu Phi** knew in 1173 what the fire talismans were for, and wrote "reed beds" on the slip so
  that Châu would paint them. He keeps a record of the four lost orders, copied by his scribe Doanh Mộ Vũ, and has told
  Châu that a fifth sends it to Lạc Hà's Vassal Hall. He would keep the hall for its lord after her. The dark sheets
  keep the record in his drawer.
- **The wards on the river.** Châu's yearly gift to Lãng Đào would read in Lạc Hà as treason and in the hall as
  penance. No one at the hall knows but her and Văn Tang Chỉ, who makes the paper.
- **The seal.** Ninh Họa Vân lost the prize seal in 1190 and wants it back from Tô Mộ Vân at the next contest. Kha has
  promised her a rematch. The mistress would rather teach the winner than beat her.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The dusk half.** Ôn Châu Liêm died at Châu Liêm Trấn (514) in 1060. His house, his curtains and perhaps his
   paintings of the western hills' rain are still there. Whoever finds them can complete the founders' art, and Châu
   and Kha both want them. (Châu, Kha, Lý Khê Thư; place 514.)
2. **The list of lost orders.** A war order goes missing on the road from the spring, and Kha Triêu Phi takes the list
   to Lạc Hà's Vassal Hall. Hách Ánh Hà, whom Nam Phố owes a favour, would rather not receive it. (Kha, Châu, Hách,
   Doanh Mộ Vũ; places 21, 249, 558.)
3. **Wards on the river.** A Lãng Đào ferry survives a Tùng Đào-Lạc Hà skirmish because of a ward with no stamp. Lạc
   Hà's Boat Hall wants to know who painted it. (Châu, Văn Tang Chỉ, Lô Xướng Vãn, Giang Cửu Khúc; Ngư Ca border.)
4. **The rematch.** The next Hạc Đinh Thị contest. Ninh Họa Vân against Tô Mộ Vân, with Kha's dark-ink talismans in her
   sleeve. (Ninh, Kha, Đới, Tô Mộ Vân; place 51.)
5. **The dark ink.** Talismans painted with Đới Huyền Nghiên's ink start to curse the people they were sold to protect:
   Lạc Hà's soldiers, and the boats of Vãn Đăng. The player traces it to the Jade Milk spring. (Đới, Hàn Tiểu Chúc,
   Châu; places 249, 555.)
6. **Placement Day.** Tạ Quy Yến is placed this spring. Châu asked for him, and Hách Ánh Hà kept him off Lạc Hà's
   thirty contracts for her. Now Hách wants the favour paid in war orders filled first. Kha would rather the boy went
   anywhere else: one more good dawn hand makes the dusk line smaller. His cousin Tạ Quy Hồng is torn. (Tạ Quy Yến, Tạ
   Quy Hồng, Châu, Kha, Hách; places 4, 21.)
7. **Reed beds.** Someone at Thủy Khẩu remembers what the reed beds were really cleared for in 1173. The player learns
   Kha wrote the slip. (Kha, Châu, Vi Lan Châu; place 139.)
