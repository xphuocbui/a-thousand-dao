A gate of boat families on the Nine-Bend River. They carry the river trade between the pines and the plains, keep nine
landings and stamp every pass with a bronze seal. In 1173 Lạc Hà burned their fleet at the Đào Sa landing, and the
pines went to war for them. Most of the gate believes it was a war for its honour. The man who started it is its head.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): stay.** The seat fits: Water 30.1 % (the first lean needs 25 %), Wood 41.0 % (the second needs 15 %).
The gate sits on the river among pine woods; its water is the river and its wood is the boats.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** River-born children (the dike and crossing villages, Water 23-40 %, Wood
30-50 %) carry Water and Wood together, which is the gate's lineage, and boat children start on the outer pole method as
soon as they can stand on a deck. Gold-washers' children of Đào Sa are Water-heavy. A pine-born child with no Water goes
to the pines instead. The gate's people are the river's people. Nearly all its named members were born within a day's
pole of a landing.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **Go round.** A river does not break a rock; it bends nine times and arrives anyway. The gate's teaching is to go
  round what cannot be moved, whether a serpent, a toll quarrel or a lord, and to arrive. The ferries went round the
  serpent for forty years and then fed it. The head's name is the river's nine bends.
- **Everybody pays.** The gate lives on tolls and believes in them the way the pines believe in listening: a fair toll
  is the river's own justice. A pass stamped with the seal is a promise that the boat will be carried. A ferryman who
  takes a passenger without stamping them is fined a season's pay.
- **A boat is a house.** Each boat family owns its boat, names it for its founding mother, and lives on it. The gate is
  a guild of boats, not a hall of disciples; its "halls" are landings. Children are born aboard.
- **The spring landing.** At the first thaw the whole fleet gathers at the gate, the head reads out the names of the
  dead boats, and the eighth-bend pilot rows a pig out to the serpent.
- **The dead.** A dead ferryman is laid in their boat and let go down the river at night with a lamp in the bow. Since
  1173 the gate does not burn a boat for any reason, and the dead of Đào Sa had no boats left to be laid in. Their names
  are read at every spring landing.
- **Dress and food.** Oilcloth coats, straw hats, bare feet on deck; the head wears his toll seal on a cord at his wrist.
  River fish, millet, rice wine. A ferryman drinks before a crossing, never during.
- **Taboos:** cutting another family's mooring rope; carrying a passenger without a pass; burning a boat; fighting on the
  eighth bend. **Outsiders** pay the toll like anyone. The gate is friendly and expensive.

**Why it fits:** the pole method draws Water up a bamboo pole from a moving river, so it takes either root and is
learned standing on a deck. The canon washes the cultivator's qi a thousand times through the meridians, as the
gold-washers wash sand, and in a fight strips a foe's wards a layer at a time. The Nine Bends Art slips blows in long
curves and arrives behind the foe. The Returning Wake Verse came from the pines' River Hall, which has trained the
gate's inner disciples beside its own since the rebuilding of 1175.

## 6. Admission

- **Outer gate:** Water or Wood 30 or more, and a boat family to stand for the child. Deck children of the gate's own
  families are taken first; a landing orphan may be stood for by the landing.
- **Inner gate:** Water 45 or more, and a crossing of the eighth bend at night with the pilot.
- **Exceptions:** none named. The River Hall's man at the gate, Đặng Hoành Giang, is the pines' own and sits outside the
  gate's ranks.

## 8. Relations

- **Tùng Đào Cung (lord):** the lord who went to war for the gate's boats and took no land (C192). Đàm Tùng Lại calls
  Giang a friend, and the envoy Chử Lan Giang calls the gate "the easiest vassal". Dụ Tĩnh Lưu of the River Hall
  rebuilt the fleet and split the tolls with Lạc Hà (C193), and the gate owes her both. Đặng Hoành Giang is the River
  Hall's man at the gate, and Giang Hồi Lan tells him the war should be fought again.
- **Lạc Hà Tông:** grudge with the lord (−10). Lô Xướng Vãn (`lo_xuong_van`) led the raid that burned the fleet at Đào
  Sa, and he raised the dead ferryman's son, Lữ Tẫn Chu (`lu_tan_chu`), in Lạc Hà's Boat Hall. Giang writes to the boy
  every spring and asks him home. The gate still buys Lạc Hà's hemp (A201) through a smuggler.
- **Thải Chi Cốc:** the ferries bring the river's orphans up to the valley, and Giang buys the valley's pills monthly,
  though the whole river knows the valley-mistress treated his boat-burners in 1174. His ferrymen spit on her landing.
  Lữ Thừa Lãng's daughter, Lữ Niệm Hà, is the valley-mistress's true disciple.
- **The gold-washers of Đào Sa:** the gate's neighbours and its second landing. They fished the nails out of the river
  for years after 1173.
- **Hàm Nguyệt Trang:** lake people. The two houses think little of each other's boats.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **Who started the war.** Giang's secret: ten years of an unset toll on Lạc Hà's boats at Ngư Ca made the quarrel
  that became the Ferry War. Lữ Tẫn Chu has never answered the spring letters because Lô Xướng Vãn, the Lạc Hà elder
  who raised him, kept the stamped passes for that toll, ten years of the Nine Bends seal on a toll the pines never
  set, and showed them to him on his fifteenth birthday. Every one of Giang's letters offers the boy a boat of his
  own. Sài Kết Lãm's smuggler has heard the same story told from the other bank.
- **The free crossing.** This spring Lữ answered at last, and not to Giang. He sent one pass back inside the letters
  Giang Tiểu Loan carries home, addressed to her grandmother Thương Bạch Phàm, who poled nine children off the burning
  landing. Thương has said nothing to her Dao partner. She has put the Ngư Ca boats on a free crossing for Lạc Hà's
  hulls until ten years of that toll are paid back, and Tiết Ấn Thủy stamps the passes at no charge and writes the
  order into the toll book in her name. Giang cannot ask her why without telling her.
- **War or letters.** The heir, Giang Hồi Lan, wants the war fought again. His father writes to Lạc Hà's foster son.
  His daughter, Giang Tiểu Loan, carries the letters to the Ngư Ca landing and writes her own on the back of each.
- **The old gate.** Đổng Cựu Môn wants the name of Phân Thủy Môn, sunk in 788, read at the spring landing. The families
  descended from the side that sank it would rather it was not.
- **The serpent.** Lại Bát Khúc feeds the serpent of the eighth bend every spring, and it has grown. Mã Trường Cao has
  seen its eye open.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The free crossing.** At next spring's landing the fleet hears that Ngư Ca has carried Lạc Hà's hulls free all
   season on the boat-mistress's order. Giang can stop her and say why, let his richest landing carry its enemies for
   nothing, or have Tiết Ấn Thủy lose the order, and Lạc Hà's boatmen want to know what the gate is paying for. (Thương
   Bạch Phàm, Giang Cửu Khúc, Tiết Ấn Thủy, Dụ Tĩnh Lưu; place 13.)
2. **The son across the river.** Lữ Tẫn Chu asks Thương Bạch Phàm to meet him on the Ngư Ca landing, and to bring the
   ferry master. The meeting brings the boy's sister from Thải Chi and the Lạc Hà elder who raised him, with the rest
   of the passes, to the same place. (Giang Cửu Khúc, Giang Tiểu Loan, Lữ Tẫn Chu, Lữ Niệm Hà, Lô Xướng Vãn.)
3. **The serpent grows.** It has been fed for 260 years. One spring it does not take the pig and takes the boat. Fight
   it, as the gate never has, or find what it wants. (Lại Bát Khúc, Mã Trường Cao, Giang Cửu Khúc; the eighth bend.)
4. **The rope.** Lạc Hà finds out who buys Tang Ma Thành's hemp and stops the smuggler, or poisons the rope. A ferry
   parts its hawser at the eighth bend. (Sài Kết Lãm, Thạch Thập Đinh; places 13, 80.)
5. **The second Ferry War.** Giang Hồi Lan and the pines' Root party push for war over the split tolls. The player is
   asked to carry a forged complaint to the Pivot, or to stop it. (Giang Hồi Lan, Đặng Hoành Giang, Nhiếp Cô Tân, Giang
   Cửu Khúc.)
6. **The old gate's name.** At the spring landing Đổng Cựu Môn reads Phân Thủy Môn's name aloud, and two boat families
   cut each other's moorings. (Đổng Cựu Môn, Thương Bạch Phàm; places 13, 1281.)
7. **The nails of Đào Sa.** Thường Thiên Đào still fishes nails out of the landing. One spring she brings up a Lạc Hà
   raider's seal ring with a name on it. (Thường Thiên Đào, Giang Hồi Lan, Khang Tẩy Kim; place 47.)
8. **The dry city.** The spring flood of 1198 uncovered Đông Lưu Thành's old street (A081). The gate's boatmen say the
   river spirit that left the boatmen in 270 (A066) is the serpent of the eighth bend. (Lại Bát Khúc, Đổng Cựu Môn;
   place 224.)
