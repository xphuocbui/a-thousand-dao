A school of zither players at the bottom of a ravine where the wind in the pines comes back off the walls a hundred
times. It was founded round a monk's zither. It takes any child with an ear, whatever their root, and teaches them one
piece of music in twelve movements that takes a lifetime to play. Its neighbours sleep through its practice. Its lord
forbids writing, and the academy writes music.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4, R3): stay, and become no-lean (Wood, Wind → none).** The seat fails both old leans: Wood is 23.5 % (the
first lean wants 25 % or the top place), and there is no Wind qi anywhere in Thâm Tùng Hải. The ravine is Metal country.
The malachite of Thạch Lục Khoáng (167, Metal 43 %) is three tiles away, and the green ravine forest of Bích Hác Sâm
(1490) is Metal 38 %. A move to Wood land would take the academy out of the ravine, and the ravine is the instrument.
The canon is played to its walls.

**Why no-lean is true.** The academy's methods have never run on an element. Sound carries qi, and the sound takes the
colour of whoever plays: a Metal child's notes ring, a Wood child's hum. The monk who founded it was a Buddhist abbot,
not a Wood cultivator. Hà Thính Tùng's Wind is his own, from a childhood of listening to the pines, and it was never
taught. The old leans described the head and the pines, not the school. The seat costs nothing: no move, no art.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** Two peoples, as the school has always had. **Ravine-born and mine-born**
children (the rim, Thạch Lục Khoáng, Thạch Tỉnh Thôn: Metal 22-48 %) are mostly Metal-rooted and make the strings and
the ringing players. The pines' Wood test turns them away, and the academy is where they go. **Pine-born** children of
Tùng Phong, Tùng Hoa and Tùng Hạ Thị (Wood 43-51 %) are Wood-rooted. Some of them have ears and some only want out of
the woodcutters' villages. About half the named members are each.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **The ravine answers.** The academy believes a note is only half played until the walls send it back. Its people play
  to the echo, not the hearer, and they say the pines taught them that. Tùng Đào listens to the pines; Vạn Hác plays back.
- **The written score.** The academy writes its music in tablature and has done so since 621. Its lord's canon is
  heard and never written (`tung_dao.md` 4). The pines allow the scores because "a score is a tune, not a method". Every
  player of the academy knows that the canon is in the tune. Nobody says so at the palace.
- **The day without music.** On the anniversary of the monk's withering (641) no instrument is touched, and the ravine
  is so quiet that the woodcutters of Tùng Phong cannot sleep.
- **Dress and food.** Students wear grey hemp and cut their nails square; players who have reached the canon's sixth
  movement grow their right-hand nails as plectra. The academy eats what the villages bring it: millet, pine nuts,
  mushrooms. No meat on practice days, which are most days.
- **The dead.** A dead player's zither is unstrung, and its strings are buried with them on the ravine floor where the
  echo is strongest. The zither goes to the instrument rooms to be restrung for a student.
- **The one rule.** Never play to hurt someone who cannot hear you. The academy fights rarely and badly by its own
  account. Hàn Thanh Hưởng, who plays the marching movement for the pines' patrols, is its only soldier.
- **Taboos:** breaking another player's string; playing on the day without music; playing the twelfth movement unless
  you mean to finish it. **Outsiders** are welcome to sit on the rim and listen, and many pilgrims to the pines do.

**Why it fits:** sound carries the player's qi out and the ravine sends it back to be drawn in, so the method needs a
ravine and an ear, not an element. In a fight the canon stacks Resonance on everything that hears it and then sets it
ringing. The Monk's Rest is a silence that makes a foe's arts falter. Thorn and Ink Verses came to the academy in the
700s as a score from a scholar of the north. It runs on Wood and is taught to Wood-rooted elders only, as the one
element-led art in the house.

## 6. Admission (R3: by ear)

- **What it recruits by:** thần hồn. A child must sing back a phrase of the pines they heard once. Root does not
  matter. The academy's students come from the ravine, the mine and the pine villages, and about half of them failed
  the pines' Wood test first (Cổ Minh Thạch).
- **Outer to inner:** the third movement played to the walls and the Monk's Rest held for one breath.
- **Exceptions:** guest craftsmen (the makers of Cầm Tượng, such as the dead Cù Cổ Đồng) who are not taught the canon.

## 8. Relations

- **Tùng Đào Cung (lord):** a gentle lord and a gentle vassal. The academy plays at the palace's festivals, and Hà
  chose the zither of the pines' heir, Vi Thanh Âm, in 1060 (`td-clear-sound-zither`) and taught her tablature. He knows
  what she writes in it (section 9). Hàn Thanh Hưởng plays for Địch Kinh Lâm's patrols. The Hall of Voices thinks the
  academy frivolous. Vi, who holds "a good part of what Vạn Hác plays", is its friend there.
- **Thiên Xu Tông:** the envoy Bách Vong Ngôn (`bach_vong_ngon`), who did not forget his message in 1080 (A062), has
  learned the zither from Hà by letter for 120 years in a sect that forbids music. Hà sends him the scores. Khương Lạc
  Huyền, who played that day, still thinks the envoy forgot.
- **Thiên Xu's surveyors** pass the ravine on their way to the grey pine vein on the Pivot's border, and stop to
  listen. The academy lets them.
- **Sương Tùng Các:** the only other house of the pines that writes. Văn Hàn Đăng buys the academy's worn-out strings
  for the threads of his ward lanterns.
- **The malachite mine and its Tùng Đào wardens:** the academy's Metal children come from it. Cổ Minh Thạch's cousin
  still digs there.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The twelfth movement.** Hà is 370. He has played the canon to the end, and no one else living has. Quan Tịch Âm,
  his heir, has reached the ninth and is afraid of the twelfth, which takes a night and must not be stopped.
- **The heir and the famous one.** Khương Lạc Huyền, the player of 1080, expected the succession and teaches the outer
  gate instead. He tells the envoy story at every banquet. If he learned the envoy only pretended to forget, he would
  lose the one thing he has.
- **The score that is not music.** In 1162 Vi Thanh Âm played Hà a piece in the academy's tablature, and he heard the
  pines' canon in it. He has told no one. If her zither is opened (`tung_dao.md` hook 2), the pines will ask who taught
  her to write, and the answer is the academy.
- **The weight of a zither.** Cù Đồng Mộc keeps his dead father's ledger with the weight of every zither Cầm Tượng made,
  including the one made in 1011 for the pines. He restrings it every forty years. The next restringing is due.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The twelfth movement.** Hà means to play it once more, for his heir, and may not survive the night. Someone must
   keep the ravine quiet till dawn: no patrol drum, no envoy, no beast. (Hà Thính Tùng, Quan Tịch Âm, Hàn Thanh Hưởng;
   place 11.)
2. **Who taught her to write.** If Vi Thanh Âm's zither is opened, the pines come to the ravine for the scores. Hà can
   burn five centuries of tablature, lie, or tell the truth. (Hà Thính Tùng, Vi Thanh Âm, Cù Đồng Mộc, Sử Trường Ngâm.)
3. **The restringing.** Cù Đồng Mộc's ledger gives the 1011 zither's weight. When Vi brings it for new strings, he will
   know. The player may be asked to carry it for her. (Cù Đồng Mộc, Vi Thanh Âm, Ngô Tố Âm; places 11, 2.)
4. **The envoy's letters.** A Thiên Xu inspector finds Bách Vong Ngôn's scores, and the trail leads to the ravine. Hà
   has a century of letters from a man whose sect forbids music. (Hà Thính Tùng, Bách Vong Ngôn, Ngạc Tri Âm.)
5. **The famous player.** Khương Lạc Huyền learns that the envoy pretended to forget. He can turn on the envoy, on Hà,
   or on the story he lived on. (Khương Lạc Huyền, Bách Vong Ngôn, Quan Tịch Âm.)
6. **The withered tree.** On the day without music, Âu Tiểu Miên hears a zither in the ruins of Khô Mộc Tự. The monk's
   art turned on him in 641. Something there is still playing. (Âu Tiểu Miên, Hà Thính Tùng; place 1205.)
7. **The war zither.** The Coiled Root Hall asks for the marching movement for a winter war on the Cold Ford ice. Hàn
   Thanh Hưởng wants to go; Hà's rule forbids playing to hurt. (Hàn Thanh Hưởng, Địch Kinh Lâm, Hà Thính Tùng.)
8. **The buried workshop.** A spring flood in the ravine opens the half of Cầm Tượng Trấn that was buried in 1012. The
   makers' unfinished zithers are down there, and so are their dead. (Cù Đồng Mộc, Mẫn Ti Trúc; place 1491.)
