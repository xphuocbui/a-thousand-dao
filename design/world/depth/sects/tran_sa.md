A walled caravanserai on the steppe, with camel pens, a well, a watchtower and a long hall where the guard families eat
together. Its blades are made in the mountains at a forge on the Sword-Washing Spring. Trấn Sa guards the Pavilion's
caravans and sells the Pavilion its swords. It was founded for a third purpose, which everyone on the road knows: to
watch the raiders who now ride escort.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): stay, and re-lean Metal, Earth → Earth, Metal.** The seat is steppe (Earth 38 %, Fire 33 %, Metal 21 %).
With Metal first it fails the test; with Earth first it passes, since Earth is the seat's top element and Metal clears
15 %. The audit offers mountain forts with Metal 40 % (Ngưng Băng Bảo, Huyết Đao Trại), but a caravan guard that moves
into the mountains stops guarding caravans. And the hall's name was always Earth: *trấn sa*, to hold the sand. The order
of the leans changes; the people and the place do not. Metal comes from the forge in the hills (Tẩy Kiếm Tuyền, Earth
37 %, Fire 24 %, Metal 23 %, density 1.57), where the smiths work.

**What it costs:** nothing on the map. The lineage puts Earth first (section 5).

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** Most of the hall's people are born in Trường Vân Thành (Water 28 %, Earth 25 %:
a mixed city) or the steppe villages (Earth 28-43 %). Their roots run Earth first, with Metal and Fire in the guard and
smith families. Water roots from the city go to the Pavilion's Desert Pavilion instead. An Earth root is the steppe's
commonest, which is why a guard hall that holds the sand never lacks recruits.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **Hold, do not chase.** A guard who rides after a raider leaves the caravan. The hall's forms all wait, hold and
  answer. Its people despise the raiders' charge and are a little afraid of it.
- **The long table.** The guard families eat together in the long hall, and the dead keep their seats for a year.
  Viên Trường Hà's seat is still kept, 100 years on.
- **A cup is a declaration.** A night-glowing cup found on the road is reported to the hall at once. The hall keeps
  every cup it has found since 1100 on a shelf above the head's chair. There are 312.
- **The forge is holy ground.** No blade leaves it unquenched in the spring, and no one is allowed to speak while a blade
  is in the water.
- **Dress:** dust cloaks the colour of the sand, sabres on the left, a horn whistle. Smiths wear leather and no
  eyebrows.
- **Taboos:** leaving a caravan; drinking from a cup found on the road; selling a Trấn Sa blade to Dạ Quang.
- **Outsiders:** travellers are fed and watched. Dạ Quang riders are fed and watched more closely.

**Why it fits:** a guard roots in the sand (Earth) and answers with a blade (Metal). The canon is built against riders:
slow them, hold the ground, cut the horse. The smiths' temper method is the reason the Pavilion, which cannot light a
fire inside its Array, buys its swords here.

## 6. Admission

- **Outer gate:** Earth or Metal 30+. The guard families' children first, then steppe and city children who can sit a
  horse and stand a night watch.
- **Inner and above:** Earth 45+ for the canon. Smiths are taken for the craft too (Metal 45+ for the temper method).
- **Exceptions:** none named. Deserters are not taken back, publicly.

## 8. Relations

- **Băng Tâm Các (lord):** a loyal, useful vassal (loyalty 65). Its swords are the Pavilion's swords. Ôn Sa Ngọc of the
  Desert Pavilion licensed the hall in 1125, and Tống Thối Kim forged Thẩm Hàn Giang's sword. Võ Kình Sơn's cousin Võ
  Băng Kiên guards for the Pavilion; the guard families are on both payrolls.
- **Dạ Quang Môn:** the reason the hall exists. Viên Cô Yên's father was killed by its raiders, and to the guard families
  Hạng Dạ Bôi is the guard who turned (A120). Seventy-five years of watching have found nothing. The hall's captain
  Bàng and Dạ Quang's escort captain Ô Liệt Phong share tea at Đà Linh, and the watcher Nhiếp and Ô share more than that.
  The head's niece writes to Hạng Lưu Hỏa.
- **Lê Hoa Am:** the grey sisters' swords came from Trường Vân's smiths before the hall existed. The forge still sharpens
  them.
- **The merchants of Hồi Lan Thành:** pay the hall and trust Ô Liệt Phong more.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The head's war.** Viên Cô Yên is waiting for Dạ Quang to raid and has a spy in its camp (Tất Dạ Lang, posted as a
  deserter). Her husband Bàng fears that waiting has become wanting, and that she will make the raid happen if it does
  not come.
- **The smith above the head.** Tống Thối Kim is the only Kết Đan in the hall and refused to lead it. If Viên drags the
  hall into a war, he is the one who can say no.
- **Letters across the road.** Viên Tiểu Yên writes to Hạng Lưu Hỏa. The two heirs of the road's feud are friends.
- **The watcher's tea.** Nhiếp Cô Đăng has found nothing on Ô Liệt Phong in seventy years, and she has stopped looking
  for it.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The 313th cup.** A cup is found on the road with blood in it. Viên Cô Yên calls it a raid; Ô Liệt Phong says it is a
   taunt by the young blades. The player rides with Nhiếp Cô Đăng to find out. (Viên, Nhiếp, Ô, Hạng Lưu Hỏa; places
   1418, 203.)
2. **The spy.** Tất Dạ Lang's false routes send Đồ Xích Linh's riders into an empty gorge, and Đồ starts to suspect him.
   (Viên, Tất, Đồ Xích Linh.)
3. **A war she makes.** Bàng Trấn Nhạc finds out that his wife means to stage a raid in Dạ Quang's colours, and comes to
   the player, or to Ôn Sa Ngọc. (Bàng, Viên, Ôn Sa Ngọc; place 3.)
4. **The Pavilion's sword.** Băng Tâm orders a blade for its heir. Thẩm Hàn Giang's faction and Khương Lưu Ly's each
   want Tống Thối Kim to make it for their candidate first. (Tống, Thẩm Hàn Giang, Khương Lưu Ly; place 255.)
5. **Heirs of the feud.** Viên Tiểu Yên and Hạng Lưu Hỏa are caught meeting at Ôn Thạch Thang. (Viên Tiểu Yên, Hạng Lưu
   Hỏa, Viên, Đồ.)
6. **The hoard.** If the sand at Mai Sa Bảo gives up Dạ Quang's hoard, the hall wants it as blood money for a century of
   dead guards. (Viên, Nhiếp, Hạng Dạ Bôi; place 1425.)
7. **The spring.** Tẩy Kiếm Tuyền's qi weakens. Tống Thối Kim thinks the forge has drunk it dry; the player can find a
   new spring or a way to rest it. (Tống, Mễ Tiểu Chùy.)
