The abbey is named for the Grain Rain, the solar term in late spring when the rain makes the rice grow. Its abbess is
named for a line of Đỗ Phủ: *the good rain knows its season; it comes in spring, follows the wind into the night and
wets everything without a sound.* For three hundred years the abbey called rain from the sea, and it came less each
century. For the last two hundred and forty it has called rain from the forest, and the farmers have had their rain.
The Night Rain Market opens only in rain. In dry years the abbey's prior sells it some, at a price written on the
abbey gate since 1180, the year half the abbey walked out over it.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): stay at 441 and re-lean Water, Wood → Wood.** The hill holds almost no Water, and the abbey's rain now
comes from the forest, which is Wood.

| **Re-lean Wood at 441** | **as above** | **fits, margin 25.8** | **the abbey's rite already draws the forest's breath; the land breeds Wood** | **seat** |

**Why Wood is true.** The founder Hạng Đảo Vũ was a coast priestess who called rain from the sea with Water. On a
bamboo hill that holds 9 % Water, her rite weakened each century, and when she died in 958 it barely worked. Dương Hảo
Vũ spent forty years learning to call rain the way a rain forest makes its own: draw the canopy's Wood qi up until the
trees breathe out mist, and let the mist fall (N-coc_vu-02). The old Water, Wood lean was the founder's root. The
abbey's Water now lives on only in its rain-callers who happen to have it.

**What it costs.** Nothing on the map. The sect record changes `leans` to `["Wood"]`. V154 stays true.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** The abbey's ground is the bamboo and rice country around Phong Đăng: the hill
(441: Wood 51 %, Metal 22 %), the lightning-bamboo grove it holds (Lôi Trúc Lâm 440: Wood 43 %), the bamboo-shoot
village below (Xuân Duẩn Thôn 883: Wood 46 %), and the farm towns and villages it serves: Phong Đăng (92: Wood 34 %,
Water 31 %), Ương Ca (157), Thượng Độ (159), Hà Châu (160) and Hoa Điền (155), all Wood 39-45 % with Water 18-20 %. It
breeds **Wood, with Water or a little Metal under it**. Nearly every child of the valleys passes the outer gate. The
farm children with more Water than Wood go to the rain-callers. The Metal children go up the road to the Tower's Bell
Test, and many come back.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **The good rain knows its season.** The abbey's teaching: help comes in its time, at night and without a sound, and
  asks for nothing. Its monks do their work unseen and refuse thanks. The abbess has never let her name be put on a
  rain.
- **The forest breathes the rain.** The abbey holds that a forest is a cloud standing on the ground. Its monks do not
  cut a living tree, and they plant ten for every one the farmers fell.
- **The planting.** Every spring at the solar term of Cốc Vũ the whole abbey goes down to the valleys. The farmers sing
  the old planting song (A287), the monks plant beside them, and the abbess holds the rite on the terraces above. A
  novice's first planting is their entry into the abbey.
- **Dress.** Grey hemp robes kilted to the knee, rice-straw hats, bare feet in the terraces. The rain-callers who go to
  Thu Trì wear black rain cloaks over the grey.
- **Food.** Spirit rice and vegetables. The abbey eats no meat, and it gives every traveller a bowl of rice.
- **Burial.** The dead are buried in the terrace walls, so that their bodies feed the rice.
- **The dead.** The abbey prays for all the dead to rest, and especially for Lôi Trạch's. It says the singing in the
  graves is suffering, and it is right. Each autumn it sends rice to Thu Phần to buy the river's drowned a rest (§9).
- **Taboos:** cutting a living tree; selling rain (the rule the prior has broken in the open since 1180); calling rain
  out of season; wasting rice.
- **How others see them.** The farmers of the south love the abbey. The Tower counts its grain. The Night Rain Market
  counts on its rain in dry years and pays the price on the gate.

**Why it fits.** The abbey's land is Wood and its work is growth. The primer breathes with seedlings, and the canon
calls rain through the forest. The verdant cycle (sow, root, mend, harvest) is the terraces' year. The rite is the
canon at the scale of a valley. In a fight the whole lineage is sustain: soft rain that heals and roots, buff stacks
that grow, and a fight that the abbey wins by still standing at the end.

**Its weaknesses.** Little damage and no ward against a burst. The great rite costs the caster's own qi and health,
and the abbess has paid too much of it already.

**Members on the lineage:** 12 of 12 living, the hermitage's included.

## 6. Admission

- **Inner gate and above:** Wood at 45, shown by a seedling that grows a hand's breadth overnight under the novice's

## 8. Relations

- **Thính Vũ Lâu (lord):** dutiful (loyalty 55). The Tower takes its grain tax from the Lục harvests first (A288) and
  counts the abbey's rice with it. The Night Rain Market's master Kiều Mãn Lâu hires the abbey's rain in dry years,
  through the prior, at the price on the gate. The Tower's Bell Test sends the abbey its failures: the
  tenant girl Thiều Mộ Vũ is expected to come to the terraces when she fails, and her brother already has.
- **Giả Hồi Xuân** (healer of Hạnh Hoa Thị, loose): the abbess's friend for a hundred and eighty years. They trade herbs
  and the hard cases.
- **Fellow vassals:**
  - **Thu Phần Các** (Lý Hận Bích): rivals. The two founders were born the same year. The abbess wants the dead of Lôi
    Trạch at rest; the Pavilion sings them to keep what they give up. Ương Ca's farmers, the abbey's people, sing louder
    at planting to drown the graves (A314).
  - **Xuyên Lâm Môn** (Úc Diệp Thanh): friends. The Gate's guides walk the abbey's rain-callers to Thu Trì for no fee.
    Both want the isle at Hà Châu, and the abbey has not said so.
  - **Vũ Linh Đường** (Ân Thanh Linh): casts the abbey's rain-calling bells, the only bells the hall makes that ring for
    the rain.
  - **Hồng Đậu Trang** (Nghê Hồng Đậu): the abbess has twice asked to see the garden and been refused. She suspects what
    is in it.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The walkout of 1180.** In the dry year of 1179 the prior **Thời Tri Tiết** sent the rite master **Hà Tĩnh Cần**
  to Thu Trì, six months down the road, to call the Night Rain Market's rain beside Lã Tiềm Dạ. She came home in the
  spring of 1180 to Phong Đăng's dead seedbeds, and at that planting she told the valley where she had been and what
  the rain was paid. Half the abbey walked out with her to the lightning-bamboo grove at Lôi Trúc Lâm, where the rite's
  poles are cut, and built a hermitage there. The abbess struck no one off. On the Tower's roll they are still Cốc Vũ.
- **Rain at a price, and rain for nothing.** Since then the prior sells rain in the open, to the market and to any
  landlord who pays, and the money buys the pills that keep **Dương Hảo Vũ**'s scarred meridians open. The hermitage
  calls rain for any farmer who asks. The grove is eight days from Phong Đăng, the abbey twenty-five. One house is rich
  and ashamed of it. The other eats what the farmers leave at the grove's edge, and in 1186 a winter rite held past
  his strength gave it its first grave, the old keeper of the grain rolls.
- **The short poles.** The abbey still buys its rite poles from the grove, and **Sở Lôi Duẩn** cuts for both houses.
  Since the spring the prior has paid him to cut the hermitage's own poles a hand short. A short pole calls drizzle.
  Three of Hà's free rites have come down as mist on dry fields this year, two villages have gone back to paying the
  abbey and told Lục Đạo Hoa why, and Hà, who thinks the failing is hers, now holds each rite twice and pays for it in
  years. The abbess keeps back one pill in three and sends it to the grove.
- **The abbess's health.** She has 130 years by her realm and much less by her meridians. Each great rite costs her
  more, and so does each pill she sends away. The next great drought may be her last rite.
- **The graves.** In 1093 the abbess stood at Thu Phần's graves and felt the chorus drawn up the way her own rite draws
  the forest's breath. Since then her autumn rice has been a price, a sack for each drowned stranger, so that he is laid
  at the edge to rest. The rice leaves at planting to reach the graves by the time they sing. Thu Phần's singing master
  writes back each year that they rest.
- **The isle.** The Hà Châu farmers' boy Lô Tân Châu says the risen isle is rice mud, not a fort site, and the abbey's
  farmers agree. The hermitage wants it planted. The abbey has never claimed land against its lord, or against the Gate.
- **The struck bamboo.** The rite's poles are cut from lightning-struck bamboo at Lôi Trúc Lâm, and they work better
  than any other. Sở Lôi Duẩn will not say why. The grove lies in Khải Lôi's old country.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The second receipt book.** The prior's second book still holds one sale nobody has seen: the bought rain of 1171,
   which fell on the Lục paddies and not on the tenants' bank across the river, the winter three Thiều children died
   (N-clans-08). (Thời Tri Tiết, Lục Thượng Lâm, Thiều Xuân Nương, Thiều Mộ Canh; places 441, 92.)
2. **The last great rite.** A drought comes to Phong Đăng. The abbess means to hold the Grain Rain rite again. The prior
   wants Lã Tiềm Dạ to call the market's rain instead, which pays for the abbess's pills, and the hermitage offers to
   hold the rite for nothing under poles a hand short. (Dương Hảo Vũ, Thời Tri Tiết, Hà Tĩnh Cần, Lã Tiềm Dạ; places
   441, 92, 440.)
3. **The song answered.** At Ương Ca's planting the graves sing the farmers' own song back to them. The abbess writes
   to the Pavilion to ask which of the dead her rice paid for is singing it, and the answer must be a lie or a
   confession. The two women are both 370. (Dương Hảo Vũ, Lý Hận Bích, Bào Thu Xướng, Đổng Tiểu Ương, Điền Xướng Ương;
   places 157, 230.)
4. **Rice mud.** Lô Tân Châu plants spirit rice on the risen isle at Hà Châu at night, between the Gate's staff and the
   Tower's claim. (Lô Tân Châu, Đinh Vãn Độ, Hứa Thanh Diêm, Sở Lôi Duẩn; place 160.)
5. **The Bell Test.** Thiều Mộ Vũ fails the Tower's test at Kinh Trập and walks down to her brother's terrace. A Tower
   steward follows her to bring her back. (Thiều Mộ Canh, Thiều Mộ Vũ, Bộc Thủ Chung, Lục Đạo Hoa; places 874, 441.)
6. **The struck bamboo.** Sở Lôi Duẩn's lightning bamboo works because the grove remembers Khải Lôi's storm rites. A
   Thunder-rooted traveller asks to buy a pole. (Sở Lôi Duẩn, Úc Diệp Thanh, Hầu Vọng Xuân; place 440.)
7. **The garden.** The abbess, who has twice been refused, is invited to Hồng Đậu's garden at last, and sees a gardener
   she knows. (Dương Hảo Vũ, Nghê Xuân Chi, Tề Vong Quy, Lam Nhuận Chi; place 263.)
8. **A healer's case.** Lam Nhuận Chi brings Giả Hồi Xuân a farmer poisoned by red beans. (Lam Nhuận Chi, Giả Hồi Xuân,
   Tề Niệm Quy; places 54, 263.)
9. **The short pole.** Tống Tiết Trúc lays one of the abbey's poles beside one of the hermitage's, and they are not the
   same length. (Tống Tiết Trúc, Sở Lôi Duẩn, Hà Tĩnh Cần, Thời Tri Tiết; place 440.)
