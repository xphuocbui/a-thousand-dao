The valley lives by its founder's saying, cut on the lintel of its hall: **the fee is the whole of the thanks** (thù
tức thị tạ, 酬即是謝). Its swords are hired by the deed. They guard the camel trains on the hot steppe, the ore roads
of Ngọc Hành and forty places in the Pivot's war hall, and they take nothing past the fee. The sect is rich, quiet and
half empty. Its head has left the valley once since 1130, for a Wind child in 1195.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): stay, and re-lean Metal, Wind → Metal, Fire.** Region 15 has no Wind qi at any place; the five wind
places on the continent are on the Pivot's plateau (236, 603) and in Thính Vũ's mountains (823, 827, 853), 70 tiles
and more from the valley. There is no candidate to move to. The valley's own land is Metal and Fire: the seat is Metal
33.5 % and Fire 16.1 %, which passes the fit test with Fire only 1.1 points over the bar, and its steppe holdings to
the west are hot ground (Thập Giáp Thôn 640, Fire 34 %; Liệt Nhật Mạch 647, the Blazing Sun Vein, Fire 36 %; the
springs of Mã Ẩm Tuyền 666 and Sa Nhãn Tuyền 645, Fire 36 %). Metal, Earth would fit with more margin. Fire is the
truer second lean, because the valley's outer gate is filled from the hot steppe and its swords are made for the
noon road.

**Wind** is kept where it belongs: in the founder's art, the Brushed Sleeve Step, which only a Wind root can learn and
which sits outside the admission rule. The founder and the present head were both born on the windy plateau terrace
of Thương Ưng Đài (236). Since Cửu Cao Phái began fetching the plateau's Wind children for its cranes, no strong Wind
root has come to the valley. The one boy with a breath of it, Lộ Khinh Trần, was fetched a week ahead of Cửu Cao's
rider in 1195.

**No cost.** Nothing moves. The `leans` record changes from Metal, Wind to Metal, Fire.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** The valley sits where the ore mountains meet the hot steppe. Children of the
mines (Can Tương 175, Metal 46; the copper and silver mines, Metal 38-44) come in Metal-rooted. Children of the steppe
villages and springs come in with Earth and Fire and a thin Metal. Under R1 a child of Mã Ẩm Tuyền is nearly twice as
likely to be Fire-rooted as the even rate. The outer gate is therefore half miners' sons on Metal and half steppe girls
on Fire. Only the Metal ones pass the inner gate, and the steppe girls who make it are the valley's best road swords.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **The founder's saying.** "The fee is the whole of the thanks." Quách Lĩnh Thù cut it on the lintel of the first hall,
  and every disciple learns it before the sword. The valley takes it literally: a hired sword finishes the work, takes
  no reward beyond the fee, and does not stay to be thanked.
- **No names.** Valley swords sign contracts with the seal, never their names: the seal signs, the sword does not.
  They do not boast, and a disciple who tells a tavern what she did on a hire is sent home. The head's own name means
  "owes no thanks".
- **The grey sleeve.** Disciples wear undyed grey with one long left sleeve, and brush it once across the hilt when a
  hire is done. The outer gate wears short sleeves until its first contract. Only the head wears the founder's robe,
  and it opens only to a Wind root.
- **The contract is the law.** A valley sword never breaks a hire once sealed, even against a friend. It may refuse to
  seal one. The Pivot's writ comes first by oath, and every contract carries a line that ends it if the Pivot calls.
- **Food and burial.** Steppe food: millet cakes, mare's milk, dried mutton carried on the long roads. A sword who dies
  on a hire is buried where they fell, and only the sleeve is brought home and hung in the hall. There are 2,114
  sleeves on the hall's beams.
- **Taboos:** breaking a sealed contract; taking a second fee; telling the story of a hire; asking the head about
  Cùng Mục Phong.
- **Outsiders** are clients. They are received courteously at the valley mouth and never shown the hall or its
  lintel.

**Why it fits.** The primer takes either of the valley's two roots, Metal from the mines or Fire from the steppe. The
canon inside is pure Metal and pure contract work: it counts the terms of a hire to one finishing cut. The secret
arts are a mercenary's manual and a fast blade. Wind is honest in this lineage: it is in one art that one man can use,
and the valley does not pretend its land can give it more.

## 6. Admission

- **Outer gate:** Metal or Fire at 30. Each spring Kiều Tĩnh Sơn walks the steppe villages and the mine camps. A family
  that sends a child gets a guard for its next caravan free.
- **Inner gate and above:** Metal 45 and one hire finished. The first contract is a small one, usually a mule train,
  and the disciple's sleeve is cut long on the day they come back.
- **Wind:** no rank requires it. A Wind root of 45 or more, of any rank, may ask the head for the Brushed Sleeve Step,
  and the head opens the founder's robe for them in the hall. None at that bar has come since 1090.
- **Exceptions:** none named. Clients are not admitted, however much they pay.

## 8. Relations

- **Thiên Xu Tông (lord, loyalty 55):** the Pivot's war hall pays a standing fee for forty swords, signed each spring
  with the drillmaster **Đái Hổ Thần**, and the valley supplies Can Tương's ore to the smiths who make the Pivot's
  blades (thien_xu §8). Loyalty is the lowest of the Pivot's vassals because the valley's loyalty is sold by the
  contract, and both sides know it. Each spring **Ngũ Trấn Bắc**, master of the war-writ office, drafts a writ of war
  against Thính Vũ, and **Mâu Tri Viễn** buries it.
- **Thối Tinh Môn:** forges the valley's swords from its own Can Tương ore. **Âu Dương Kiên** has noticed that these
  blades ring more clearly than the gate's "star iron". The gate's best Fire root, **Đinh Hỏa Lân**, is a steppe boy
  from the valley's own village of Thập Giáp.
- **Cửu Cao Phái:** the sore point. Since Cửu Cao's riders began taking the plateau's Wind children for the cranes,
  the valley has had no one to give the founder's art to. **Thang Vô Tạ** fetched **Lộ Khinh Trần** from Tập Trận
  Khoáng a week before Cửu Cao's rider came for him.
- **Tư Không Hạc Niên** (loose, Nguyên Anh): the sword sage of the 1130 duel. The chronicle calls it a draw (C186). It
  was not one, and he let it stand. The valley does not know that he was its founder's one disciple: Quách Lĩnh Thù
  took him on the ore road after walking out in 790 and lived until 900, when he left Tống Khách Thị as the traveller
  of A138 (`hermits-sages` N-hermits_sages-03, -05).
- **Kim Vạn Lý** (loose, the western merchant): the valley's largest private client, who owes **Khúc Liệt Dương** a
  year's pay.
- **Thính Vũ Lâu:** officially none. A company of grey sleeves guards its iron in the south under another name
  (N-phat_y-03), and the contract carries no Pivot line, the only contract the valley has sealed without one. The Tower
  would not pay without that, and it pays three times the fee.

## 9. Inside the sect

The valley lives by two laws, and they have not yet met. Every contract it seals carries one line: *void if the Pivot
calls*. And a valley sword does not break a sealed contract.

- **The contract with no line.** In 1190 **Lam Khế Ngôn** sealed the southern company, which guards Thính Vũ's iron
  under the name of a camel train of Đà Đội Thôn (N-phat_y-03). It is the only contract the valley has sealed without
  the Pivot line. The Tower would not pay without that, and it pays three times the fee. Each spring **Ngũ Trấn Bắc**
  drafts a war writ against Thính Vũ, and **Mâu Tri Viễn** buries it. If one passes, the valley's own law sends a grey
  company to fight its own lord's army, or the valley breaks a sealed contract for the first time since 560.
- **The road book.** The camel train exists only on paper, and **Giang Bích Sa** writes the paper. Since 1196 she has
  entered its stages, its fodder and its dead in the contract house's road book at Lam's word, and she copies each
  year's renewal fair. She asks a higher copying fee at every renewal, and Lam pays it. **Mạnh Thư Cầm**, who wants
  Giang's desk, has offered to copy the road book for half the fee, not knowing what is in it. Lam must take the
  offer and let a second clerk into the book, or tell her own disciple why not.
- **Nine sleeves.** The southern company has sent home nine sleeves since 1190: two to Phượng Minh's raiders on the
  southern road, seven to the rain forest's fever and its beasts. Lam books each death on the Đà Đội camel road, and
  the families are told of a road that has no camels. **Thôi Ấn Thư** pays the company's wages out of the Tower's
  money and the sleeve price to the families of the dead, so she keeps both books. She takes a stone in ten from
  every sleeve price, and charges Lam for her silence by the sleeve, a little more each time one comes home.
- **Her brother's sleeve.** **Yên Lạc Tụ** sweeps the hall of sleeves, and the newest is her brother's. In his second
  year on the hire he broke the valley's taboo and wrote her one letter, from an iron town on the southern road; she
  has kept it. She was given his sleeve price, a stone in ten short, to carry home to their mother at Sa Nhãn Tuyền.
  She has not carried it home. She is spending it at the valley mouth on wine for the Đà Đội drivers, as their trains
  come in for guards, and asking which of them rode with him. Thôi has told her to stop.
- **The robe.** **Thang Vô Tạ** knows about the southern company and not about the missing line, and asks nothing,
  because a sword does not tell the contract house its business. The founder's robe opens only to a Wind root, and
  **Lộ Khinh Trần**'s Wind is far under the bar. Since the winter of 1199 the head has held the folds open for him
  with his own Wind. Each fold costs the head what a season of seclusion would give him, and the boy bleeds at the
  nose after it. **Liêu Vọng Phong** holds the lamp at the door. The head pays it because Cửu Cao will take the next
  Wind child, and there may not be another.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The writ passes.** One spring Mâu Tri Viễn cannot bury it. The war hall invokes the line in every contract, and
   every grey sleeve on hire must come home. One company cannot: its contract has no line, and a valley sword does not
   break a sealed contract. Đái Hổ Thần asks Lam for the southern contract over the go board; Khúc rides south to
   fetch the company or to hold it. (Lam Khế Ngôn, Đái Hổ Thần, Ngũ Trấn Bắc, Mâu Tri Viễn, Khúc Liệt Dương; places
   24, 5, the southern road.)
2. **Nine sleeves.** A mother comes to the valley mouth asking which camel road her son died on. No Đà Đội driver
   has heard of the train, and Yên Lạc Tụ has been buying them wine to ask the same question. Lam sends the player to
   Giang Bích Sa for the train's stages in the road book. The player can answer the mother, send her to Lam, or take
   her to the hall of sleeves. (Yên Lạc Tụ, Lam, Thôi Ấn Thư, Giang Bích Sa; places 24, 662.)
3. **The paymaster's price.** Thôi Ấn Thư raises it, and asks for the tenth sleeve's price before the tenth sleeve
   comes home. Lam hires the player to carry the purse; Yên Lạc Tụ asks the player what is in it. (Thôi Ấn Thư, Lam,
   Yên Lạc Tụ; place 24.)
4. **The year's pay.** Khúc Liệt Dương goes west to collect what Kim Vạn Lý owes her. Valley swords do not take a
   second fee. Does a debt count? (Khúc, Kim Vạn Lý, Viên Tịch Chiếu.)
5. **A Wind child.** A Wind-born child is reported at a hawk-catchers' camp on Thương Ưng Đài. A Cửu Cao rider and a
   Phất Y sword set out the same morning. (Thang Vô Tạ, Lộ Khinh Trần, Cừ Vân Lý, Ung Hạc Minh; places 236, 603.)
6. **The fold at a time.** Lộ Khinh Trần has written of the robe to his cousin Lộ Phi Sương at Cửu Cao's roc camp,
   three months off by the post. When the letter comes, the cousin carries it to Cừ Vân Lý on the plateau, and
   Cửu Cao's rider rides four months down the road to the valley mouth to say the boy was promised to the cranes
   and to see him fold the Step. The head cannot show him without showing what
   it costs. (Cừ Vân Lý, Lộ Khinh Trần, Lộ Phi Sương, Thang Vô Tạ, Liêu Vọng Phong; places 24, 234.)
7. **The sword marks.** Among the marks on Cùng Mục Phong, forty days' ride from the valley, one cut crosses all the
   others and ends in a torn thread of grey cloth caught in the rock. This year a new disciple on Kiều Tĩnh Sơn's
   climb pulls the thread out and carries it home in her sleeve. It matches the mended seam under the head's arm: he
   lost, and Tư Không cut his sleeve and called it a draw. The player can buy the thread from her, bring it to the
   head, or carry it to Tư Không Hạc Niên. (Thang Vô Tạ, Tư Không, Kiều Tĩnh Sơn; place 186.)
