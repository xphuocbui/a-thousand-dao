The valley lives by one verse of Lý Bạch's, cut into the cliff at its mouth: ten paces, one man; a thousand li, no
trace; when the deed is done, brush your sleeve and go, and hide your name. Its swords are hired by the deed. They
guard the camel trains on the hot steppe, the ore roads of Ngọc Hành and forty places in the Pivot's war hall, and
they leave when the work is done. The sect is rich, quiet and half empty, and its head has not left the valley in
seventy years.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): stay, and re-lean Metal, Wind → Metal, Fire.** Region 15 has no Wind qi at any place; the five wind
places on the continent are on the Pivot's plateau (236, 603) and in Thính Vũ's mountains (823, 827, 853), 70 tiles
and more from the valley. There is no candidate to move to. The valley's own land is Metal and Fire: the seat is Metal
33.5 % and Fire 16.1 %, which passes the fit test with Fire only 1.1 points over the bar, and its steppe holdings to
the west are hot ground (Thập Giáp Thôn 640, Fire 34 %; Liệt Nhật Mạch 647, the Blazing Sun Vein, Fire 36 %; the
springs of Mã Ẩm Tuyền 666 and Sa Nhãn Tuyền 645, Fire 36 %). Metal, Earth would fit with more margin. Fire is the
truer second lean, because the valley's outer gate is filled from the hot steppe and its swords are made for the
noon road.

**Wind** is kept where it belongs: in the founder's art, the Brushed Sleeve Step, which only a Wind root can learn and
which sits outside the admission rule. The founder and the present head were both born on the windy plateau terrace
of Thương Ưng Đài (236). Since Cửu Cao Phái began fetching the plateau's Wind children for its cranes, no third Wind
root has come to the valley, and the art may die with Thang Vô Tạ.

**No cost.** Nothing moves. The `leans` record changes from Metal, Wind to Metal, Fire.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** The valley sits where the ore mountains meet the hot steppe. Children of the
mines (Can Tương 175, Metal 46; the copper and silver mines, Metal 38-44) come in Metal-rooted. Children of the steppe
villages and springs come in with Earth and Fire and a thin Metal. Under R1 a child of Mã Ẩm Tuyền is nearly twice as
likely to be Fire-rooted as the even rate. The outer gate is therefore half miners' sons on Metal and half steppe girls
on Fire. Only the Metal ones pass the inner gate, and the steppe girls who make it are the valley's best road swords.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **The verse.** "Ten paces, one man; a thousand li, no trace. When the deed is done, brush your sleeve and go, and
  hide yourself and your name." Every disciple learns it before the sword. The valley takes it literally: a hired
  sword finishes the work, takes no reward beyond the fee, and does not stay to be thanked.
- **No names.** Valley swords sign contracts with the seal, never their names. They do not boast, and a disciple who
  tells a tavern what she did on a hire is sent home. The head's own name is "hides his name".
- **The grey sleeve.** Disciples wear undyed grey with one long left sleeve, and brush it once across the hilt when a
  hire is done. The outer gate wears short sleeves until its first contract.
- **The contract is the law.** A valley sword never breaks a hire once sealed, even against a friend. It may refuse to
  seal one. The Pivot's writ comes first by oath, and every contract carries a line that ends it if the Pivot calls.
- **Food and burial.** Steppe food: millet cakes, mare's milk, dried mutton carried for a thousand li. A sword who dies
  on a hire is buried where they fell, and only the sleeve is brought home and hung in the hall. There are over two
  thousand sleeves on the hall's beams.
- **Taboos:** breaking a sealed contract; taking a second fee; telling the story of a hire; asking the head about
  Cùng Mục Phong.
- **Outsiders** are clients. They are received courteously at the valley mouth, under the verse, and never shown the
  hall.

**Why it fits.** The primer takes either of the valley's two roots, Metal from the mines or Fire from the steppe. The
canon inside is pure Metal and pure contract work: it counts steps to one finishing cut, then steps away. The secret
arts are a mercenary's manual and a fast blade. Wind is honest in this lineage: it is in one art that one man can use,
and the valley does not pretend its land can give it more.

## 6. Admission

- **Outer gate:** Metal or Fire at 30. Each spring Kiều Tĩnh Sơn walks the steppe villages and the mine camps. A family
  that sends a child gets a guard for its next caravan free.
- **Inner gate and above:** Metal 45 and one hire finished. The first contract is a small one, usually a mule train,
  and the disciple's sleeve is cut long on the day they come back.
- **Wind:** no rank requires it. A Wind root of any rank may ask the head for the Brushed Sleeve Step. None has asked
  since 1090.
- **Exceptions:** none named. Clients are not admitted, however much they pay.

## 8. Relations

- **Thiên Xu Tông (lord, loyalty 55):** the Pivot's war hall pays a standing fee for forty swords, signed each spring
  with the drillmaster **Đái Hổ Thần**, and the valley supplies Can Tương's ore to the smiths who make the Pivot's
  blades (thien_xu §8). Loyalty is the lowest of the Pivot's vassals because the valley's loyalty is sold by the
  contract, and both sides know it.
- **Thối Tinh Môn:** forges the valley's swords from its own Can Tương ore. **Âu Dương Kiên** has noticed that these
  blades ring more clearly than the gate's "star iron". The gate's best Fire root, **Đinh Hỏa Lân**, is a steppe boy
  from the valley's own village of Thập Giáp.
- **Cửu Cao Phái:** the sore point. Since Cửu Cao's riders began taking the plateau's Wind children for the cranes,
  the valley has had no one to give the founder's art to. **Thang Vô Tạ** fetched **Lộ Khinh Trần** from Tập Trận
  Khoáng a week before Cửu Cao's rider came for him.
- **Tư Không Hạc Niên** (loose, Nguyên Anh): the sword sage of the 1130 duel. The chronicle calls it a draw (C186). It
  was not one, and he let it stand. The valley does not know that he was its founder's one disciple: Quách Vô Tích
  took him on the ore road after walking out in 790 and lived until 900, when he left Tống Khách Thị as the traveller
  of A138 (`hermits-sages` N-hermits_sages-03, -05).
- **Kim Vạn Lý** (loose, the western merchant): the valley's largest private client, who owes **Khúc Liệt Dương** a
  year's pay.
- **Thính Vũ Lâu:** officially none. A company of grey sleeves guards its iron in the south under another name
  (N-phat_y-03), and the contract carries the usual line: void if the Pivot calls.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The succession.** The head is three hundred and has no Wind heir. **Lam Khế Ngôn**, the contract keeper, has made
  the valley rich and wants it, but she is a Trúc Cơ and has never been on a long hire. **Khúc Liệt Dương** has spent
  twenty years on the road, and the swords would follow her. The head watches **Lộ Khinh Trần**, a boy with a breath of
  Wind, and says nothing.
- **The southern contract.** Lam has hired a company to Thính Vũ under a false train name. **Giang Bích Sa**, who
  copies the contracts, has found the train that does not exist. If the Pivot learns of it, the valley's standing fee
  and its loyalty are both in question.
- **The seam.** Thạch lost on Cùng Mục Phong; Tư Không cut his sleeve and called it a draw. The head's silence (A232)
  is the valley's founding virtue and its biggest lie.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The sword marks.** A disciple climbing Cùng Mục Phong notices that one cut crosses all the others and ends in a
   torn thread of grey cloth caught in the rock. The player can bury it, bring it to the head, or bring it to Tư Không
   Hạc Niên. (Thang Vô Tạ, Tư Không, Kiều Tĩnh Sơn; place 186.)
2. **The train that does not exist.** Giang Bích Sa brings the player a contract for a camel train of Đà Đội Thôn that
   has no camels. It leads to a company of grey sleeves guarding Thính Vũ iron in the south. (Giang, Lam Khế Ngôn,
   Thính Vũ's buyers; places 662, the southern road.)
3. **A Wind child.** A Wind-born child is reported at a hawk-catchers' camp on Thương Ưng Đài. A Cửu Cao rider and a
   Phất Y sword set out the same morning. (Thạch, Lộ Khinh Trần, Cừ Vân Lý, Ung Hạc Minh; places 236, 603.)
4. **The year's pay.** Khúc Liệt Dương goes west to collect what Kim Vạn Lý owes her. Valley swords do not take a
   second fee. Does a debt count? (Khúc, Kim Vạn Lý, Viên Tịch Chiếu.)
5. **The Pivot calls.** The war hall invokes the line in every contract. Every grey sleeve on hire must come home,
   including the company in the south. (Lam, Đái Hổ Thần, Ngũ Trấn Bắc; places 24, 5.)
6. **The head walks out.** One morning Thạch's grey robe is hanging in the hall, as the founder's did in 790. Lam,
   Khúc and the boy with a breath of Wind each have a claim. (Thạch, Lam, Khúc, Lộ; seat 24.)
7. **Ore that rings.** Âu Dương Kiên takes a Can Tương blade and a Thiên Ngoại blade to the smiths and asks why his
   rings better. (Âu Dương, Hầu Thiết Anh, Cát Lưu Quang; places 175, 23.)
