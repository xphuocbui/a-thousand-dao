A forge on a bare iron mountain, with a snow-melt pool below it, a star mine eight tiles off and a coal mine nine
tiles below, each about two weeks by mule. The gate makes the Pivot's swords, the halberds of its war hall, and the blades its vassals carry. It is a
working house of hammers, coal-carriers and polishers, and it lives on one claim: that the iron it digs fell from the
sky. One blade in its hall did.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): stay, and re-lean Metal, Fire → Metal.** The seat is Metal 53 %, Earth 25 %, Fire 9 %. Fire fails in
any order (it is neither 15 % nor in the top two). The places that fit Metal and Fire in Ngọc Hành Sơn (Thanh Đăng Tự
649, Khổ Tu Viện 646, Can Tương Khoáng 175, Lệ Thạch Khoáng 450, Vạn Trùng Hạp 204) lie 55-93 tiles away, and all but
the unheld gorge of Vạn Trùng Hạp are holdings of Phất Y Cốc. The
chronicle pins the gate to its seat and its mine: it was founded on the mountain river at place 23 in 330 (A221) and
claimed Thiên Ngoại Khoáng, eight tiles off, in 335 (A222). Moving the forge away from the star mine would undo the sect. So the
gate keeps its seat and admits that its land has no fire.

The fire it needs comes in baskets. **Thạch Thán Khoáng** (1483, the Coal Mine, Metal 56 %, Water 15 %) is the gate's,
nine tiles below (the coal comes up about two weeks by mule), and every forge fire on the mountain is a coal fire. A Fire root is a gift the mountain gives about
once a century, and the head is one: he was born at Xích Viêm Khẩu (250), the one fire vent in Ngọc Hành, Fire 51 %.

**No cost.** Nothing moves: no art, route, province or chronicle line changes. The `leans` record changes from
Metal, Fire to Metal.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** The gate's ground is pure Metal country. The seat is Metal 53, the star mine
(173) 57, the coal mine 56, the calamine mine Lô Cam Khoáng (657) 41. Children born there are Metal-rooted at more than
twice the even rate and Fire-rooted at half of it. Water comes a distant second at the coal mine and the pool, which is
why the quench-keepers tend to be coal-carriers' sons. The few Fire roots in the hall came from outside: the head from
the fire vent and the journeyman Đinh Hỏa Lân from Phất Y's hot steppe (640, Fire 34 %).

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **The mountain gives iron and the coal gives fire.** The smiths' first saying. A Fire-rooted smith is "his own
  coal", and is treated with the awe and resentment a forge gives anything rare.
- **Nine blows, one quench.** No blade leaves the gate until it has been folded nine times and quenched once, in the
  snow-melt pool below the forge. The outer gate's method (the Nine-Temper Scripture) is named for it.
- **The one who strikes does not quench.** The founder's rule. The quench-keeper holds the founder's River Tongs and
  carries every blade from the anvil to the pool; the smith who made it may not touch it until it is cold. Smiths say
  this is so the maker cannot lie to himself about the blade.
- **Listening.** A smith listens to a blade before looking at it. Good iron rings; star iron, they say, sings. The
  hall of the gate is kept silent while a blade is tested, which suits the Pivot.
- **Dress and food.** Black felt aprons, shaved forearms, iron rings on the thumbs for the tongs. Millet, salt mutton
  and strong tea; the coal-carriers eat first.
- **Burial.** A smith is buried at the edge of the star mine with his first blade, broken in two. Nhạc Bá Chùy's
  grave has none: his first blade was the halberd's first fold, and it lies under the barrow with the marshal.
- **Taboos:** quenching your own blade; selling a blade to a sect at war with the Pivot; letting the forge fire go
  out (it has burned since 330).
- **Outsiders** buy at the gate and are shown the star blade in its rack. They are never shown the star mine's ore
  next to it.

**Why it fits.** The land is all Metal, and so is the lineage, from the hammer method of the outer gate to the
quenching canon inside. The one rite that needs Fire is the one that keeps the forge alight when the coal fails, and
that is the sect's trouble in one line: the forge has burned since 330, and the mountain breeds almost no one who can
hold the rite. The
lineage is a smith's lineage in a fight too: it hardens with every hit and makes the foe brittle.

## 6. Admission

- **Outer gate:** Metal 30 or more. The coal mine, the star mine and the calamine mine send their children, and the
  town of Thanh Bách sends the odd clerk's son who came to see the star.
- **Inner gate and above:** Metal 45 or more, and a blade that rings: the apprentice forges a knife alone, and the
  quench-keeper listens to it.
- **Fire:** no rank requires it. The Rite of the Fallen Star does, and is taught outside the admission rule to whoever
  can hold it.
- **Exceptions:** none named. The gate lends rather than hires: its own son is the Pivot's guest armourer.

## 8. Relations

- **Thiên Xu Tông (lord):** the gate forges the Pivot's blades (thien_xu §5) and lends it an armourer, the head's
  second son **Cát Thối Phong**, who keeps the seat's forge and is drawing the halberd under the barrow without leave.
  The Plumb Line carried by **Công Tôn Duẫn** is Cát Đoán Nhạc's work. The Pivot weighs the gate's ingots at Song Kiều
  (A219), where **Hồng Chuẩn Bình** argues with everyone, and it has never assayed the star mine's ore for what it is.
- **Lạc Hà Tông:** **Vưu Chước Kim**, the smith thrown out in 1160, keeps Lạc Hà's armoury at Tử Điện Thành, and the
  blade there woke in his hand. He knows the gate's secret and has told no one, yet.
- **Chân Kim Các:** the assayers at Song Kiều, some forty tiles off, refine the gate's silver and test everyone's metal but the
  gate's. **Tưởng Bách Luyện** has never been asked to test the star ore, and Cát has never asked him.
- **Phất Y Cốc:** the swordsmen buy their blades here, forged from their own Can Tương ore, and the gate's Fire roots
  come from their steppe. The two sects are friendly in the way of a smith and a customer.
- **The Quý family:** **Quý Thủ Lũy**, the marshal's granddaughter, keeps the barrow and will not let the Cát son near
  the halberd. The gate remembers that its own elder died for the same wish (N-thoi_tinh-03).

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The Rite and the fire.** The Rite of the Fallen Star is not a headship rite. It is how a smith who is his own coal
  keeps the forge alight when the coal road fails. **Cát Lưu Quang** is 240 and the only one who holds it, and his
  Fire-rooted second son is the Pivot's armourer. Since spring he has taught it to **Đinh Hỏa Lân**, the steppe
  journeyman, when the hammers stop. **Cát Đoán Nhạc**, who has no Fire, signs for the coal and has cut the head's
  forge to a day's ration, so the lessons now burn the forge's winter store. **Cát Thanh Diệm** carries her own ration
  across the yard, against her father.
- **The iron that does not sing.** In 1160 Vưu Chước Kim proved that Thiên Ngoại's ore has been mountain iron
  since the sky seam gave out in 790 (N-thoi_tinh-04). The head threw him out and has gone on selling it as star
  iron. Bàng Lãnh Tôi heard it in the pool and has named his price for
  keeping quiet. Hầu Thiết Anh guesses, and salts the ingots with Vẫn Tinh
  discs bought each spring from Củng Tinh's Sầm Lạc Thiết, so they ring true under lightning. Mẫn Hắc Châu, who can
  weigh iron in her hand, is starting to ask.
- **The halberd.** Cát Thối Phong wants to draw Trầm Sa Kích from the barrow and reforge it, as Nhạc Bá Chùy wanted
  before him. His father has not forbidden it.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The singing test.** Someone strikes a Thiên Ngoại ingot with a Thunder art in front of witnesses: a Thính Vũ
   buyer, the Pivot's hound Đồ Vô Cữu, or the player. Four centuries of the Pivot's "star iron" are mountain iron, and
   the Ngọc Hành Hall has been paying for sky. (Cát Lưu Quang, Hầu Thiết Anh, Đặng Quân Bình, Hồng Chuẩn Bình; places
   173, 82.)
2. **The price of the pool.** This autumn Bàng Lãnh Tôi told Cát Lưu Quang that he heard the ingots in 1160 and will
   say so to Vưu Chước Kim unless the pool and the founder's tongs go to Kha Ô Lan when he leaves it, over the head's
   own pick. A sealed letter to Vưu waits in the pool-house. If the head stalls, the player is asked to carry it to Tử
   Điện Thành, and Vưu, who drank tea with him for forty years, learns that his friend sold what he heard. (Bàng, Vưu,
   Kha Ô Lan; places 23, Tử Điện Thành.)
3. **Coal for the lessons.** Cát Thanh Diệm asks the player to bring coal up from Thạch Thán Khoáng for her
   grandfather's forge, past her father's tally, or Cát Đoán Nhạc asks the player to see that none comes. With the
   winter store spent on the lessons, the next slide on the coal road (hook 7) can put out a fire that has burned since
   330 before Đinh Hỏa Lân holds the Rite. If Đinh holds it, he talks of taking it home to the steppe. (The Cát family, Đinh, Phan Lộ Kiến;
   places 23, 1483.)
4. **Drawing the halberd.** Cát Thối Phong asks the player to help him reach Trầm Sa Kích under the Vạn Cốt Trủng.
   Quý Thủ Lũy stands in the way, and the gate's own dead elder asked the same thing in 806. (Cát Thối Phong, Quý Thủ
   Lũy, Nhạc Bá Chùy; Chiết Kích Nguyên.)
5. **Three years of taxes.** A clerk of Thanh Bách Thành has found the 980 sale deed and says the city was cheated on
   the star's weight. Tào Bách Sinh's grandfather signed it. (Tào, Cát Lưu Quang; places 65, 23.)
6. **The girl who weighs iron.** Mẫn Hắc Châu weighs a star-mine ingot against black iron from Huyền Thiết and gets
   the wrong answer. She can take it to her master Hầu Thiết Anh, to the Pivot's overseer Ngưu Trọng Đĩnh, or keep it.
   (Mẫn, Hầu, Ngưu Trọng Đĩnh; places 173, 174.)
7. **The coal stops.** A slide at Thạch Thán Khoáng cuts the coal road in winter. A forge that has burned since 330 is
   one cold night from going out, and the head has to burn his own Fire to keep it. (Cát Lưu Quang, Kha Thán Sinh;
   places 1483, 23.)
