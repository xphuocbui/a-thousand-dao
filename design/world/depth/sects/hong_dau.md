The manor has two houses. The front house on the forest road has a gracious door that opens to guests of every sect,
a tea room and a mistress of the door who decides who is poured a second cup. The back garden, three days inland in the banana
shade, is where the red beans grow on trellises over a spirit vein. Those who stay too long do not leave (A275). They tend the
beans. The red bean is the seed of longing in the old poem, and the most poisonous seed in the south.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): move the seat 5 tiles from the manor house (27) to Tiêu Âm Mạch (263), and re-lean Poison, Wood →
Wood, Water.**

**Why not Poison.** Poison is not a root. In this world poison land is Darkness land, and the manor's ground has no
Darkness qi. The catalogue's poisons (`rotting-canopy-canon`, `wood-venom-drinker-pendant`) are Wood arts with a poison
tag, and so are the manor's. The honest lean is Wood with poison arts.

**The story.** The beans were always grown at the vein. Nghê Hồng Đậu planted the first trellis over Tiêu Âm Mạch in 912,
and her daughter was born there in 1013. The manor house on the road was the door. In 1110 the mistress moved her own
rooms and her canon back into the banana shade (N-hong_dau-03). Ân Thanh Linh's bell lines had begun to hang over the
forest road, and Nghê did not care to have what was said at her table rung back to Thu Trì. The manor house became
what it is now: a door, a tea room and the mistress of the door.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** The manor's ground is the wet south-west of Tiêu Vũ Lâm: the jungle coast at
the door (27: Water 33 %), the vein garden (263: Wood 29 %, Water 25 %), the fishing villages it holds (Lạc Triều Thôn
818, Lăng Đường Thôn 822: Water 33-42 %, Wood 25-32 %), the misty bridge at Yên Kiều (156: Wood 37 %) and the old orchid
port of Lan Tân (262: Water 40 %). It breeds Water and Wood in about equal measure, with a little Fire inland. **Water
children** fill the door: the tea, the charms, the fans (Liễu Đa Thải, Sầm Vãn Hương, Kha Tiểu Đậu). **Wood children**
go to the garden and rise: the inner gate needs Wood at 45, and that is the manor's quiet sorting. The mistress herself
was born in Thu Trì (Water 40 %) with a Wood of 82, which the city never explained.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **Gather more (nguyện quân đa thải hiệt).** The manor's motto is the third line of the poem it is named for: *red
  beans grow in the southern land; in spring, how many branches? I wish you would gather more; these most of all are
  longing.* Its teaching is that longing and poison are one thing seen from two sides. Both grow if left alone, and
  both are fatal only to those who stay.
- **The gracious door.** Any guest is welcome at the manor house, of any sect, and is poured one cup of tea. A guest
  who asks for a second cup is given one. The second cup carries the charm: a guest who drinks it wakes on the cart to the garden, and stays. The rule is
  never written down. Every guest who reaches the door has heard it, and some ask anyway.
- **The gardeners.** Those who stayed live in the back garden and tend the trellises. They are fed, clothed and kindly
  treated, and they do not wish to leave. The manor calls them guests. A sect that comes looking for a lost disciple is
  shown the garden, and the disciple will not come.
- **Red beans.** The beans are bright, pretty and deadly. Every member wears one on a red string at the throat, as a
  charm and a promise. The pickers sort them by touch in the dark. A bean given by hand is a declaration of love. A bean
  dropped in a cup is a death.
- **Dress.** Silk in red and rose at the door; grey cotton and banana-leaf hats in the garden. The mistress wears red.
- **Food.** Sweet red-bean soup, which no guest is ever given; river fish; tea from the manor's own bushes.
- **Burial.** The dead are buried under the trellises, and the garden says the beans there grow best.
- **Taboos:** pouring a second cup without the door mistress's leave; giving a bean by hand without meaning it;
  speaking of the gardeners' homes in the garden; drawing Phi Hồng.
- **How others see them.** The Tower sees a useful vassal whose poisons sell well and whose mistress is its first
  elder's oldest friend. The righteous see a den. Travellers on the forest road step around the red beans on the path
  (A279) and do not ask who dropped them.

**Why it fits.** The manor's land is Wood and Water, and its art is the slow kind: poisons that ripen, charms that hold a
foe in place while the poison grows. The primer takes either element, because the door needs Water girls as much as the
garden needs Wood. The canon is Wood, and so is everything above it. The sung charms of Thorn and Ink are the door's;
the rotting canopy is the garden's.

**Its weaknesses.** No body art, no ward and no sword: the mistress carries a sword whose art she cannot open. A foe who
does not stay, or who comes cleansed, beats the manor. Its poisons are sold through the Tower's market and depend on
it.

**Members on the lineage:** 10 of 11 living. The exception is the gardener Tề Vong Quy, a Phượng Minh swordsman under
the charm, who keeps his own sect's method.

## 6. Admission

- **Outer gate:** Wood or Water at 30. The manor takes girls from its villages at twelve, and boys more rarely. It
  chooses by nerve and looks as well as root: the door girls must be able to pour a cup for a killer and smile.
- **Inner gate and above:** Wood at 45. A Water girl with less Wood stays at the door all her life and may run it, as
  Liễu Đa Thải does. Her rank as chấp sự is the door's, not the canon's.
- **Exceptions:** the gardeners (servants, tạp dịch), who are guests held by the charm and taught nothing; Tề Vong Quy
  is the one named.
- **Kin:** the manor keeps no families. Children born in the garden are raised as the manor's, and are told they are
  foundlings.

## 8. Relations

- **Thính Vũ Lâu (lord):** warm and useful (loyalty 65). The manor's poisons sell on the best stall of the Night Rain
  Market (Kiều Mãn Lâu). Its mistress takes tea with the Tower's first elder Quan Vũ Thanh every winter at the door, and
  pours one cup only. The two women have known each other since they were girls in Thu Trì's teahouses. The Tower's
  guest alchemist Ô Thiềm Tử was her lover for one season in 1012. The courier La Tố Y ate a meal at the manor and
  lived, and the mistress laughed at her boast. The manor does not take sides between the Tower's Rain and Thunder
  factions. It sells to both.
- **Lạc Hà Tông:** the old Lạc Anh claim. Lạc Hà holds the bay of the Petal Shore, the Tower holds its forest (C128),
  and Phi Hồng is half of the right to lead the school that once held both. Hàn Tư Quân died trying to sell that half to
  Lạc Hà in 1062.
- **Phượng Minh Cung:** one of its swordsmen has gardened at the manor since 1153. Phượng Minh has not asked for him, or
  has not been told where he is.
- **Fellow vassals:**
  - **Vũ Linh Đường** (Ân Thanh Linh): rivals. Her bell lines on the forest road hear what is said near them, and the
    mistress moved her rooms out of their reach in 1110. The bells ring when a charm is cast nearby, and the door girls
    hate them.
  - **Xuyên Lâm Môn** (Úc Diệp Thanh): the Gate will not guide travellers to the door after dark. Its guides step around
    the beans.
  - **Thu Phần Các** (Lý Hận Bích): the other ma đạo vassal. The two mistresses trade: beans for the graves, grave-earth
    for the garden.
  - **Cốc Vũ Quán** (Dương Hảo Vũ): the abbess has twice asked to see the garden, and been refused.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The door and the garden.** Liễu Đa Thải loves the guest game. Nghê Xuân Chi thinks it a vanity: every guest kept is
  a sect that will come looking, and the beans grow without them. When the mistress dies, the door and the garden will
  fight over whether the second cup is poured again.
- **The daughter's father.** The manor believes Xuân Chi is a foundling. Her mother knows, and so does Xuân Chi, who
  has spent a hundred and fifty years answering her father's poisons with her own through the market under no name. Ô
  Thiềm Tử thinks his rival is a stranger.
- **The other sword.** Nghê Hồng Đậu wants Tàn Hồng. With both swords she could call herself heir of Lạc Anh Phái and
  claim the forest the Pivot gave the Tower. In 1199 a boy came to the door with a family tree ending in Diệp Phi Hồng,
  and she took him in at once.
- **The beans on the path.** Someone has been scattering red beans on the forest path since 1197 to turn travellers
  back (A279). It is the charmed gardener's daughter, Tề Niệm Quy, and no one has caught her.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **A disciple at the door.** A great sect's inner disciple vanished on the forest road. Their master comes to the
   gracious door and is poured one cup. The disciple is in the garden and will not come. Liễu Đa Thải wants to pour the
   master a second. (Liễu Đa Thải, Nghê Xuân Chi, Nghê Hồng Đậu; places 27, 263.)
2. **The gardener's daughter.** Tề Niệm Quy wants her father woken and sent home to Phượng Minh. The cure is in the
   garden, the Tower's market, or Giả Hồi Xuân's hands. Waking him wakes a Phượng Minh spy who has lived forty-seven years
   inside the Tower's vassal. (Tề Niệm Quy, Tề Vong Quy, Nghê Xuân Chi, Giả Hồi Xuân; places 263, 54.)
3. **The father in the market.** Ô Thiềm Tử finally traces the nameless poisons that answer his. If he learns he has a
   daughter, the Tower's alchemist and the manor are joined, or at war. (Ô Thiềm Tử, Nghê Xuân Chi, Mai Độc Ca, Nghê
   Hồng Đậu; places 42, 263.)
4. **The boy with the family tree.** Diệp Lạc Phương's blood may open Phi Hồng's art, or may only be wanted for it. The
   mistress is kind to him, and that frightens the garden. (Diệp Lạc Phương, Nghê Hồng Đậu, Tiền Khổ Đằng; place 263.)
5. **Tàn Hồng.** Word of the other sword reaches the door. With both, the mistress would claim Lạc Anh's land: the
   Tower's forest and Lạc Hà's bay. (Nghê Hồng Đậu, Quan Vũ Thanh, Tô Vãn Chiếu; slot V-1, not pinned.)
6. **One cup only.** At this winter's tea Quan Vũ Thanh tells her oldest friend about the peace terms from Phượng Minh.
   The mistress sells what she hears. Who buys this? (Nghê Hồng Đậu, Quan Vũ Thanh, Nhiếp Vô Thanh, Vu Ngô Đồng; place
   27.)
7. **The bells on the road.** Ân Thanh Linh hangs a new bell line within reach of the door. It rings at the second cup.
   (Ân Thanh Linh, Liễu Đa Thải, Trịnh Thủ Linh; places 27, 156.)
8. **Succession.** The mistress has a hundred years left. The door wants the cup; the garden wants it closed. A player
   can tip it. (Nghê Xuân Chi, Liễu Đa Thải, Sầm Vãn Hương, Kha Tiểu Đậu; places 27, 263.)
