The Palace is slow, gentle and very old, and it started two of the continent's great wars. It believes in
listening: its methods are chanted master to disciple and never written down, and its whole people stop twice a day to
hear the pines. It believes the pine shelters what grows under it, and for a thousand years it has taken in the fallen
abbeys of the north. Its founder read the same teaching the other way: a pine's roots go under everything near it.
The two readings are the palace's politics.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): stay.** The seat passes the fit test (`seat-audit.md`: OK). Wood is 48 % and Water 16 %; the test
asks a second lean for 15 % or a top-two place, and Water is the seat's second share, so it passes both ways, but only
just. The brief for this pass said the seat fails on Water; the audit says it does not, and this page follows the
audit. The thin Water is real, though, and the sect's answer to it is centuries old. Its Water does not live at the
seat. It lives on the river and the lake, in two halls of its own.

**Why not move.** No place on the ground has both more Water and anything like the seat's density. Tùng Gian Thành
(32) is evenly Wood and Water and has half the density. The lake and river lands are Water-rich and thin. Moving would
trade a palace that fits its first lean well for one that fits neither. It would also break the sect's oldest rule:
the founder chose this ground for the sound of the pines, and Tùng Đào is named for that sound, not for water.

**What staying costs.** Nothing to move. One proposal for the data: the River Hall (33) and the Lake Hall (97) become
sect halls on the Provinces record, so the sect's Water members have homes that fit them.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** The ground holds three peoples.
- **Pine-born** (Thâm Tùng Hải, region 4, and the inland woods of regions 1 and 5: 43-60 % Wood). Wood roots are about
  twice as common as on even land. They fill the inner gate and the elders' benches.
- **River-born** (Cửu Khúc Xuyên, region 5: Đào Thanh, Hà Bạn, Cổ Độ, 44-49 % Wood and 23-25 % Water). Their roots are
  Wood and Water together, the sect's ideal, and the River Hall is theirs.
- **Lake-born** (the long lake, region 1: Quy Điểu, Hàn Yên, Tuyết Lộc, Tạc Băng, 37-46 % Water, Wood often under 30 %).
  Their Water is strong and their Wood is thin. Most of them stop at the outer gate, because the inner gate tests Wood.
  The Lake Hall has argued about that for two hundred years.

There is also a Metal streak in the grey pine heights and the malachite country (Thạch Tỉnh, Quan Hạ, Thạch Lục Khoáng:
22-43 % Metal). The few of its children who pass the gate (Tân Mặc Bách, Cổ Lục Sơn) guard the vein and the mine. About
a fifth of the named members were born outside the ground: Pivot towns, Lạc Hà's river villages, a Băng Tâm fishing
village, a Phượng Minh cook's son.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **The pine listens.** The sect's one teaching is to hear before you act. Its methods are cultivated by listening:
  to qi moving through a pine sea in long waves, to an opponent's breath, to a council's silence.
- **Heard, never written (khẩu truyền).** No method, law or chronicle of Tùng Đào is written down. A master chants it to a disciple under
  the pines, line by line, in the rhythm of the waves, and the disciple chants it back. "What is heard must be earned;
  a book can be stolen." This makes the sect hard to rob and easy to wound: an art held by one person dies with them
  (N-tung_dao-05). The palace keeps accounts and sends letters like anyone; what it will not write is what it teaches,
  what it rules and what it remembers. The only arts it lets be written are the wards Sương Tùng Các makes for it.
  Writing the canon down is the gravest taboo the sect has.
- **The listening.** At dawn and at dusk one stick of incense burns in the Pine-Heart Censer on the palace terrace, and
  the whole sect, from the head to the kitchen, stops and listens to the pines until it is ash. Visitors are made to
  sit through one before any business. Most of them hate it.
- **The pine shelters.** Six fallen houses of the north have walked to Tùng Đào: monks, hermits, tide disciples,
  incense makers, flood and earthquake survivors (V044, V001, V035, V046, V004, V052). Others scattered instead (V039,
  V049, V050). The sect takes them in
  and lets them keep their own ways where these do no harm, which is why the Tide Rite still runs Vọng Triều Các's method.
  The founder's other reading, that a pine's roots go under everything near it, gave the sect its wars.
- **No living pine is cut.** It is the gravest crime on the ground. Halls are built round living trunks; timber comes
  only from fallen trees (A059); malachite is dug only where no root must be cut (A051). Open fire is forbidden under the
  palace's canopy. Furnaces burn in stone rooms set apart from the trees, and the Pill Room is the only hall with a chimney.
- **Pine names.** The first two heads gave each new elder a name with Tùng (松) in it: Tùng Lại, Tùng Nguyệt, Tùng
  Hàn, Tùng Chi, Tùng Ba. Đàm Tùng Lại has given none since 900, and says the pine names nobody. The pine-named are the
  old guard; the Coiled Root Hall's master would not take one from Đàm.
- **Dress and food.** Outer disciples wear undyed pine-bark linen and pin their hair with a pine twig; inner disciples
  add a needle-green sash; elders wear grey-green. The head wears the undyed bark robe of the listening. The palace eats
  pine nuts, mushrooms, river fish, resin honey and needle tea.
- **The dead go to the roots.** A dead member is laid in a hollowed fallen pine on the floor of Bàn Căn Lâm (956) and
  left for the roots and the moss. There is no grave marker. Their name is sung once at the dusk listening and after
  that lives only in the chronicle. A deserter's name is never sung, which is why Lộ Thu Hà's nightly breach matters.
- **Resin.** Pine resin is the sect's tithe from its own villages and its trade. The resin tax laid on the Cold Ford's
  fishers (A041) is the sect's stain, and the coast still names it after the governor's pine name.
- **Taboos:** cutting a living pine; writing the canon; open fire under the canopy; singing a deserter's name; speaking
  during the listening.

**Why it fits:** the outer primer takes either lean, so the lake children can start. The inner gate and the founder's

spoke it. An unwritten lineage cannot recover it from a book.

## 6. Admission (R2 as the Palace applies it)

- **Outer gate:** Wood or Water at purity 30 or more. The stewards walk the pine villages, the river towns and the lake
  hamlets each spring; the vassals send tribute children (Bồ Hạnh Lâm came from Thải Chi Cốc at nine).
- **Inner gate:** two tests at the primer's sixth layer. The disciple must chant the whole primer back from memory, and
  must hold Wood at 45 or more. Tuyên Bích Hà keeps both tests at Tùng Hạ Thị. Lake-born children with strong Water and
  thin Wood fail the second. Lương Tạc Băng and the Lake Warden want the Rain on the Pine Sea canon taught as a Water
  inner method so they can rise.
- **Exceptions kept:** the palace's mortal and servant staff (Bành Táo Nhi, a Fire root); the Tide Rite's own method;
  vassal pledges (Kiều Hàn Nguyệt, who carries Ice the pines cannot teach). The sect hires no guest elders. It will not
  chant its arts to a hireling, and an art that cannot be chanted to you cannot be paid for.

## 8. Relations

- **Thiên Xu Tông (the Pivot):** the oldest friendship and the worst defeat. The founders drank three days in 270
  (C070); in 502 Kỳ broke the peace and lost (C106), and the Compact was written against him. Chung Hoài Cực let the
  Cold Ford stand in 672; Đỗ Tinh Hà gave Thúy Vi the writ that took it back (C165) and arbitrated the Long Lake (C171).
  Doãn Ngọc Hành set aside the sect's Ferry War complaint with a smile (Chử Lan Giang). Thiên Xu's surveyors walk past the
  grey pine vein's fence, and Tân Mặc Bách counts their footprints.
- **Thúy Vi Tông:** grudge −5 (Cold Ford). Wood against Wood, pines against haze. Ngu Lam Sơn sends Đàm tea every year
  and gets no answer. Hồ Tùng Nguyệt keeps the border talks civil with Biện Vãn Tình and across the table from Hướng
  Dạ Đăng, Nhiếp Cô Tân's childhood neighbour, and she pays a spy, Phù Liên Kiều, in Thúy Vi's outer gate. Đàm Thanh
  Giang meets Hướng Vãn Chu on the river-mouth ice. Six of the Root Hall's men lie under that ice.
- **Băng Tâm Các:** grudge −5 (Long Lake). Thôi Khinh Hồng and Sở Ngọc Hồ respect each other since 1090 (C024). On the
  lake, Thiều Lăng Ba and Băng Tâm's skiff captain Lục Hàn Chu count each other's boats. The Lake Hall's pearl tallier
  was born on Băng Tâm's shore.
- **Lạc Hà Tông:** grudge −10 (Ferry War), the sect's worst. The founders were allies in 502. Dụ Tĩnh Lưu split the
  tolls with Lạc Hà in 1175; Nhiếp Cô Tân's son died on its border; Tô Vãn Chiếu is the name the Root Hall curses.
- **Vassals:** Hàm Nguyệt Trang (pearls; Kiều Nguyệt Châu's numbers never match the Lake Hall's), Vạn Hác Viện (the

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The Shade and the Root.** Two readings of one teaching. The Shade (Đàm Tùng Lại, Hồ Tùng Nguyệt, most of the Hall
  of Voices): the pine shelters, so the sect goes to war only for its vassals. The Root (Nhiếp Cô Tân, the Coiled Root
  Hall, the Cold Ford's descendants such as Giáp Tân Hồi): the founder's roots went under the Cold Ford, and the sect has
  given back everything since. The River (Dụ Tĩnh Lưu) votes for whatever keeps a quay above the flood line, and so
  decides most councils.
- **The named heir.** At the new-year listening of 1199 Đàm named **Vi Thanh Âm** heir (Kết Đan, the youngest Golden
  Core in three hundred years, who holds the canon to its twelfth layer), as he had always meant to. The Root did not
  argue. It went looking. Nhiếp Cô Tân saw the ink on her right hand that night, and the Coiled Root Hall has searched
  her rooms under its discipline writ whenever she leaves them since. Địch Kinh Lâm has twice lifted the zither off its
  stand and weighed it. Vi carries it to every listening now. Đàm lets the searches run: forbidding them would tell the
  palace there was something to find.
- **The first elder's spy.** Hồ Tùng Nguyệt is late Kết Đan and in good health, and her spying is for the canon. At
  the border talks of 1190 Hướng Dạ Đăng quoted the primer's fourth line to her across the table, word for word. Since
  1194 she has paid Phù Liên Kiều to sit in Thúy Vi's outer gate, where nothing is locked, and list what the lord's
  shelves hold of the pines' canon, written down from the Cold Ford families. The pay comes out of the Root Hall's
  purse: she tells Nhiếp the boy is learning the Haze Walk for his crossing.
- **Ears at Tùng Phong.** Vạn Hác now takes a child with an ear from the pine villages every year, before the stewards'
  spring walk reaches them. Nhiếp calls it a raid on his lord's gate and has answered it in kind: since last autumn a
  Coiled Root Hall patrol winters at Tùng Phong Thôn and tests the village children for ears first, with Hàn Thanh
  Hưởng, the academy's own marching player, sounding the phrase. The patrol walks the best hummers to the palace for the
  Root Hall, which wants war players of its own (`van_hac.md` 9).
- **The six on the ice.** Nhiếp sent the 1172 patrol without the head's leave. Địch Kinh Lâm chose the men, Xa Trọng
  Sơn heard the order read and has drunk ever since, Lộ Thu Hà sings her son's name every night, and Nhiếp Vãn Đào has
  found the written order.
- **The written canon.** Vi Thanh Âm, the heir, is writing the canon down in zither tablature inside her zither. Her
  disciple Ngô Tố Âm has noticed the weight. Sử Trường Ngâm has seen her ink.
- **The fifteenth layer.** Thôi Khinh Hồng has listened for it for five hundred years. A woodcutter's boy in the outer
  gate, Trình Văn Thanh, says the pines go on past the primer. No one has told her.
- **The river's secret.** Dụ Tĩnh Lưu knows the flood of 700 was sent onto her abbey's bank to save a city.
- **The rope-hand on the Ngư Ca bank.** In spring 1199 Nhiếp Cô Tân sent Mạc Thằng Kiều, who held the windlass line the
  night his son cut the last bridge, over to the Lạc Hà bank to see how the new bridge was hung. He did not come back.
  The roll carries him on long patrol, and the 1175 peace would not survive the word that Tùng Đào had a man on that
  bank. Mạnh Kiên Căn asks after him at the Ngư Ca ferries.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The fifteenth layer.** Trình Văn Thanh hears the pines chant past the primer above Tùng Hạ Thị. If Thôi Khinh
   Hồng hears of him, she will take him; the Shade wants him for Vi, the Root for Vãn Đào; and if he is right, the
   canon's lost layer is in the trees. (Trình Văn Thanh, Tuyên Bích Hà, Thôi Khinh Hồng, Yến Thu Hồng; places 46, 1489.)
2. **The heir's zither.** The Root Hall's searchers find the zither off Vi Thanh Âm's back at last, or Ngô Tố Âm opens
   it to end the searches. Exposed, the named heir falls, Đàm falls with his choice, and the Root gets the war council
   it wants; kept, the canon survives its holders. The player may be the searcher, the witness or the buyer. (Vi Thanh
   Âm, Ngô Tố Âm, Sử Trường Ngâm, Nhiếp Cô Tân, Địch Kinh Lâm, Đàm Tùng Lại.)
3. **The six under the ice.** A warm spring brings the patrolmen up at the Cold Ford's river mouth. Lộ Thu Hà wants her
   son's name sung; Xa Trọng Sơn can say whose seal the order carried; Nhiếp Vãn Đào holds the order; Thúy Vi's
   warden put them there. A war, a trial or a burial. (Lộ Thu Hà, Xa Trọng Sơn, Nhiếp Vãn Đào, Nhiếp Cô Tân, Hướng
   Dạ Đăng; place 31.)
4. **The shelf list.** Phù Liên Kiều's list reaches Hồ Tùng Nguyệt through his sister, and Thúy Vi's open shelves may
   hold more of the pines' canon than the pines do. Nhiếp asks where his Haze Walk is. The player can carry the letters,
   steal the book, or tell Nhiếp what his purse has bought. (Hồ Tùng Nguyệt, Phù Liên Châu, Phù Liên Kiều, Nhiếp Cô
   Tân; places 46, 1110.)
5. **The crossing.** Nhiếp has gone round Cận Tùng Hàn and asked Khuông Hàn Ly for a road across the river-mouth ice
   for a winter war. She brings Cận each flag to check, and he retunes each one to hold a patrol and break under a
   company. (Khuông Hàn Ly, Cận Tùng Hàn, Nhiếp Cô Tân, Đàm Thanh Giang, Hướng Vãn Chu.)
6. **The lovers on the ice.** Đàm Thanh Giang and Hướng Vãn Chu meet each winter where the crossing would run. A war
   would put him on one bank and her on the other; a marriage could end the grudge. (Đàm Thanh Giang, Hướng Vãn Chu,
   Đàm Tùng Lại, Ngu Lam Sơn.)
7. **The lantern of Hàn Tân.** The second piece of the Governor's Charge lies under the quay stones of Hàn Tân, in the
   hole Hướng Đông Lâm's lamp came out of in 905. Nhiếp would give a great deal for it; Giáp Tân Hồi would give more. (Nhiếp Cô Tân, Giáp Tân Hồi,
   Hướng Dạ Đăng; place 31.)
8. **The letter of 906.** Nhiếp can break Đàm with the letter he never sent, or Đàm can send it at last and end the
   Cold Ford quarrel. (Đàm Tùng Lại, Nhiếp Cô Tân, Ngu Lam Sơn, Dụ Tĩnh Lưu.)
9. **The river's choice.** A great flood comes down the Nine-Bend River again, and the River Hall's sluices can save
   Đào Thanh Thành or the low villages, not both. Dụ Tĩnh Lưu ran the hall for 360 years so no one would face that
   choice. (Dụ Tĩnh Lưu, Hoàng Đê Sinh, Lộ Thu Hà; places 33, 1219, 1233.)
10. **The debt at Đào Sa.** Triệu Huyết Y, who escaped Thôi in 1090, still writes to the woman she pulled from the
    river. She wants a favour from the River Hall's steward, and the River Hall's steward owes her a life. (Khang Tẩy
    Kim, Triệu Huyết Y, Thôi Khinh Hồng; place 47.)
11. **A Water gate.** Lương Tạc Băng brings the lake's thin-Wood children to the palace to sit the inner gate again and
    demands the Rain canon as an inner method. (Lương Tạc Băng, Thiều Lăng Ba, Tuyên Bích Hà, Hồ Tùng Nguyệt; places
    97, 46.)
12. **The voice in the cliff.** Trịnh Ức Vân hears her mother answer from the rubble of Đoạn Vân Viện every spring.
    Something under the fallen cliff halls is alive, or is pretending to be. (Trịnh Ức Vân, Chử Lan Giang; place 1259.)
