The sect was founded in 520 by castaways who shared no language and no road home (A147), and its name is Vương Bột's
line about duckweed and water meeting by chance. It runs the strait crossing between Cô Vụ Đảo and the mainland every
day of the year, sails the coast traders round the island, and lends its boats to anyone who pays. Its saying comes
from its second head: meeting, why must we have known each other before? Most of its people were born somewhere else.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): a short move, Water and Wood kept.** The old seat (18) is grass above the landing (Wood 26 %, Water
22 %, Earth 22 %). Water is under 25 % and not the top element, so it fails the first lean. Re-leaning to Wood first
would fit the land, but the head carries Water 74 and no Wood, and the lineage's main element is the head's (R2), so
the cheap swap fails him. The sect moves to the tide instead.

**The story.** For six centuries the sect cultivated a castaways' primer on the grass where the founders met, and it
needed nothing better. In 1102 the books of Phiêu Bồng Quán came down from the sea cliffs (V093), and with them the Still
Moon Tide Scripture, a canon that fills at the turn of the tide. It stalled on dry grass. Bùi Phiêu Bình, head from
1110, chose the crab flats five tiles inland of Lãng Thạch's harbour: low brackish grass at sea level, cut by creeks
that fill and empty with the tide, where Water comes up out of the mud. A great crab held it. In 1115 he fought the Claw King for two tides and killed it at slack water (N-binh_thuy-03), and in 1116 the
sect raised the Tide Hall on agarwood stilts above the flats (N-binh_thuy-04). The boats moor at Lãng Thạch (123) and
the ferry still runs from Tương Phùng Thị (49).

**What the move costs (to do when approved).**
- Chronicle: A147 (520) and A148 (525) place the founding and the market at 18 and stay true. A156 (1110) happened at
  the old hall. Add N-binh_thuy-01 to -07 (below). The seat record moves to 302.
- Art: a hall on agarwood stilts over grey grass and crab creeks, with a raft track running off toward Wave Rock. Place
  18 keeps its painting as the Strangers' Hall.
- Routes: a raft track 302 to 123 (5 tiles); the 18-49 and 18-123 roads stay.
- Density: 302 is 1.01 against 18's 1.31. **Founder's array** (amended 2026-11-25, ruling group 2: every moved sect
  gets one): the **Slack-Water Weirs (Đình Triều Yển)**, `seat_array_density` **+0.30** (proposed; World Generation
  rule 16a, Formula G: 1.31 − 1.01), so the Tide Hall reads 1.31. In 1116 Bùi Phiêu Bình drove the Claw King's shell
  plates into the flats as a ring of weirs that hold the slack water's qi round the Tide Hall (N-binh_thuy-04).
  Breaking the weirs (a hook) drops the seat to 1.01.
- **The old seat** becomes the **Strangers' Hall** (Khách Tương Đường): newcomers sleep their first night there, and
  the Book of Meetings is kept there.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** Cô Vụ Đảo's north shore is grass and dunes at 26-33 % Water, its mangrove
villages (Hồng Thụ, Nam Minh, Xướng Vãn) run to 39-40 % Water, and its bamboo and jungle interior is Wood country at 37-50 %.
The sect's island-born are Water-rooted shore children or Wood-rooted raft children from the canes, and both pass the
outer gate. The inner gate (Water 45) is mostly mangrove and Wave Rock born. But a third of the sect was born somewhere
else: refugees, castaways, placed children from Lạc Hà, a girl found beside a whale. Their roots look like the places
they came from, and the sect likes it that way.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **The crossing refuses no one.** The founder's rule. The ferry carries anyone who pays the fare, enemy or friend,
  criminal or envoy, and on the boat no one is asked their name. In 1173 the head refused Lạc Hà boats for the raid on
  Đào Sa on the same rule: the crossing carries everyone and fights no one. The ferry has been late only on the day of
  the Claw King.
- **The Book of Meetings.** Every newcomer writes where they came from in a book kept at the Strangers' Hall, and
  the sect's dead are crossed out of it, not out of the roll. The book is fourteen volumes thick. The purser reads one
  page aloud at every new year.
- **Strangers first.** A newcomer's first night in the Strangers' Hall is free, and they are fed before they are asked
  anything. A disciple born on the island is not ranked above one born elsewhere. The sect's joke is that it has no
  natives, only the earlier arrivals.
- **Dress.** Undyed sailcloth, salt-white with wear, tied with a cord of dried duckweed. Captains wear oilskin. The head
  wears whatever he slept in and carries the fan.
- **Shell coin.** The ferry is paid in Bối Sa shell coin (A144), not in stones, and the till is kept in shells. Lạc
  Hà's envoy paid that way at fifteen and still does.
- **The dead** are laid on a raft of cut canes with their oar and pushed off from Wave Rock's quay (123) on the ebb. The sect
  says they go back to wherever they came from. The founder went out that way in 860, and so did the empty raft for
  Tôn Viễn Hàng.
- **Taboos:** refusing a fare; asking a passenger's name on the water; fishing the Claw King's descendants on the shoal
  (they are the hall's watchdogs now); sailing west past the Cô Phàm charts without the head's leave, since 1187.
- **Outsiders** like them. The sect is cheap, quick and discreet. The only people who distrust it are the ones who know
  that a sect which asks no names can carry anyone.

**Why it fits.** The primer takes either of the island's two elements, which is what a sect of castaways would write.
The Still Moon Tide is a sustain canon: the tide fills the cultivator before it breaks on the foe, and on the flats, where the creeks fill and empty with the tide, it
fills twice a day. The Returning Wake Verse is the wake behind every boat, and the ferrymen sign it with one hand while
they steer with the other. The Undertow Litany is sung over the empty rafts. The head's own Gale Courier Scripture
(`gale-courier-scripture`, Wind) is his, not the sect's. No one else on the shoal has Wind except the girl he found.

**The missing fascicle.** The catalogue's Still Moon Tide has twelve layers. Phiêu Bồng's chest held only nine when
it came down. The other three are under the Tide Hall's floor (Lương Bạch Tần's secret).

## 6. Admission (R2)

- **Outer gate:** Water or Wood at purity 30. The ferry recruits from the shore villages and the canes, and from anyone
  who gets off the boat and will not get back on.
- **Inner gate and above:** Water at 45. Wood-heavy raft children who lack it stay in the outer gate as boat crews,
  and that is no shame here.
- **Placement Day:** Lạc Hà sends Wood and Water children who failed its dusk test three times. Bình Thủy pays the fee
  and Lạc Hà takes a tenth of their pay for thirty years (Đàm Thủy Sinh, 1199).
- **Exceptions:** none named. The Strangers' Hall takes in people of any root for a night, but the sect teaches only
  those who pass.

## 8. Relations

- **Lạc Hà Tông (lord):** a light rein and a long habit. The sect carries Lạc Hà's island children across to the outer
  gate, its envoys and its tribute. Hách Ánh Hà crossed on the ferry at fifteen and paid in shell coin she had counted
  twice, and she still drinks with Bùi on it. In 1173 Bùi refused boats for the Đào Sa raid, and the Counting House
  remembers. The sect takes Lạc Hà's Wood and Water children on Placement Day.
- **Lận Viễn Phàm (Lạc Hà's island warden):** a friend and a wound. Bình Thủy built and crewed both deep-water boats he
  paid for to look for Cô Phàm's lost fleet (N-binh_thuy-05, -06). Neither came back. Kiều Vọng Hải, widow of the
  second captain, is his enemy, and the shipwright Hầu Bách Chu is still owed for the second hull.
- **Lãng Đào Môn (Tùng Đào's ferry vassal):** a rival trade and a respect. Bùi and Giang Cửu Khúc trade weather and
  rope, and Giang knows Bùi would not lend boats to burn his.
- **Nhàn Vân Quán (fellow vassal, Đàm Ảnh Thành):** neighbours on the island. The ferry carries its scrolls across the
  strait. The abbey asked for Phiêu Bồng Quán's books in 1102, and the dying master left them to Bình Thủy because Lương
  Bạch Tần was there. Diệp Tố Quyên still wants a painter's notebook from them, and Bùi wants a scroll for it.
- **Minh Loan Các (fellow vassal):** every ferry of Bùi's carries one of its bells.
- **Nhạn Trận Môn and Nhạn Thanh Thành:** the far end of the crossing. The goose gate's drill fields are a day's ride
  from the mainland landing.
- **Cô Phàm Các (fallen, 705):** the sect lives among its leavings: the harbour it will not take, the charts in Wave
  Rock (A154), and the island's belief that the Lone Sail is still out there.
- **Phiêu Bồng Quán (fallen 1102):** the abbey whose books it holds. Its ruin on the sea cliffs (380) is Lương Bạch
  Tần's unfinished business.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The ledger in the sea-chest.** Bùi carries proof that the miasma of Chiết Kích Nguyên was Lạc Hà's own array, in
  Vô Định Thành's tallies for 802. He has kept it for four hundred years because the woman who gave it to him died for
  those archives. If it reached the Pivot, the Compact would have to answer it. If it reached Trầm Tịch Dương, it would
  tell him how his wife spent her last hour.
- **The fascicle under the floor.** The first elder is one layer past the head, and no one asks why. Bùi's heir Sa
  Kình Nhi will need the tenth layer one day, and Lương will not give it to the girl the man she left her master for
  found on a beach.
- **The heir.** Bùi is Fickle. He has named four heirs in ninety years and unnamed three. The whale-child is the fifth.
  The sect half believes she will be the one, because Bùi has stopped talking about the others.
- **The lost boats.** Kiều Vọng Hải wants the sect to stop lending to Lận. Hầu Bách Chu has built a third boat on his
  own time, and Tôn Niệm Triều and Sa Kình Nhi both mean to sail it.
- **The ferryman who sailed.** Ông Tế Độ took the ferry out on the day the news of the second boat came in, because the
  crossing refuses no one. Kiều has not spoken to him since. Most of the sect think he was right.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The ledger.** The player crosses on the ferry with Bùi on a stormy night, and he talks about Vô Định Thành. If
   trusted, he asks the player to carry a sealed packet to Trầm Tịch Dương after his death, or now. Cảnh Trường Thiên,
   or a Pivot agent, would pay anything to stop it. (Bùi, Trầm, Cảnh; places 302, 558, 223.)
2. **The third boat.** Hầu Bách Chu's hull is finished. Tôn Niệm Triều and Sa Kình Nhi plan to sail it west past the
   charts without leave, and Lận Viễn Phàm offers to pay for the voyage. Kiều Vọng Hải will stop her daughter at any
   cost, or go with her. (Hầu, Tôn, Sa, Kiều, Lận; places 302, 123.)
3. **The tenth layer.** Sa Kình Nhi reaches the ninth layer and stalls. Someone must ask Lương Bạch Tần why the canon
   ends there, or search the Tide Hall's floor. (Lương, Sa, Bùi; place 302.)
4. **The cliff path.** Lương asks the player to climb with her to Phiêu Bồng Quán and light its lamp. The abbey has
   tenants now: beasts, or a hermit who claims the master's books were never hers to give. (Lương; place 380.)
5. **No names on the water.** A passenger on the ferry is wanted by Lạc Hà's Hall of Scales. Ông Tế Độ will not give
   him up on the boat. Ashore is another matter, and Mộ Hành Vân waits on the quay. (Ông, Mộ Hành Vân; places 49, 38.)
6. **The Claw King's brood.** The crabs of the shoal have grown since 1115. One is as large as its father, and it has
   started taking rafts. The sect's taboo forbids killing them. (Ngô Tần Tần, Bùi; place 302.)
7. **The Đào Sa refusal.** The Counting House sends a bill to the ferry for the boats Lạc Hà had to hire in 1173,
   with twenty-seven years of interest. Bùi laughs. Hách Ánh Hà asks the player to make the bill go away before the
   Hall of Scales collects it. (Bùi, Hách, Tông Thủ Tín; places 49, 558.)
8. **The whale's road.** Sa Kình Nhi dreams in the tongue she spoke at eight. Đoạn Thương Hải, who listens to the
   pool at Đàm Ảnh Thành, tells her the tongue is older than Cô Phàm. (Sa, Đoạn Thương Hải; places 125, 37.)
