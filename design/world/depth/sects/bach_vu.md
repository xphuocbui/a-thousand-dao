The palace's second-youngest vassal, and the only one founded by people who were not cultivators first. Three bird
catchers of the cinnamon woods, two sisters and an old man, built tree-lofts in the jungle in 1110 and offered the
palace their birds. Their creed is one line: all birds bow to the phoenix. The pavilion is a family more than a sect.
It has no Kết Đan. It wants more than it has, and its head is taking it.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): stay at place 330 and re-order Fire, Wood → Wood, Fire.** The seat is a jungle clearing (Wood 33 %,
Earth 22 %, Fire 20 %, Metal 15 %, Water 10 %, density 1.11). Fire as the first lean fails: 20 % is under 25 % and not the
top element. As the second lean it passes (above 15 %). The audit found no place in the region where Fire leads and Wood
follows. The fire land of the palace's ground is all in Đan Huyệt Châu, a hundred tiles south-east. The pavilion's
lofts are trees and its birds roost in Wood. It has always been a Wood house that serves a Fire lord, and the order
of its elements now says so.

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** The cinnamon, peach and river-bend villages round the pavilion (Nhục Quế 34 %
Wood, Đào Khê 30 %, Hà Loan 29 %) give Wood roots about one and a half times as often as even land, with Fire at 16-19 %,
a little under even. A catcher's child here is likely to have the Wood to make the birds come and less likely to have
the Fire for the fan. The pavilion takes both. The ridge villages to the north (Trúc Phiệt, Kiều Mạch: Metal 27-43 %)
give the Metal roots that make cages, which the pavilion barely uses.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **All birds bow to the phoenix.** The founders' creed (V137). Keeping the phoenix's birds is keeping faith with the
  phoenix, and the pavilion counts itself the humblest and most devout of the palace's halls.
- **The hundred feathers.** Feathers are taken only from moults, never from a living bird and never from a dead one.
  Each keeper gathers one feather of every kind she can find, and her standing is the number of kinds in her fan.
  Tạ Thanh Thước has ninety-nine. The hundredth socket in every fan is left empty for the phoenix.
- **No cage for life.** A bird may be caged only to train it, and only one person in the pavilion may make cages. At a
  keeper's death her birds are let go, and that release is the funeral. She is buried at the foot of her roost tree.
- **Dress.** Cloaks sewn from moulted feathers, leather gauntlets, bare feet for the branches.
- **Food.** Rice, fish and fruit. Bamboo rats from Trúc Thực Viên for the hawks. No bird is eaten, not even by guests.
- **Taboos.** Plucking a living bird; eating fowl; killing a bird on the pavilion's ground; filling the hundredth socket.
- **Outsiders.** Friendly and curious. Any traveller who brings a feather of a kind the pavilion lacks is fed for a week.

**Why it fits.** A tamer's lineage. The cultivator becomes a tree for bonded birds to roost in, and carries a fan of
the birds' own moulted feathers. The outer method is the catchers' calls, breathed. The main method is the newest of
any of the palace's vassals and owes much to the Wutong Court's notes. The fan art is the pavilion's own answer to the
palace's Morning Sun Feather Fan. Non-lineage member (R5): the servant cage-maker Hoàn Trúc Lung (Metal, stand-in).

## 6. Admission

- **Outer gate:** Wood or Fire at 30 or more, and a bird that will come to the hand. Recruits come from the catcher
  families of the cinnamon woods and the river villages.
- **Inner gate and above:** Wood 45 or more, and a bonded bird.
- **Exceptions:** servants (tạp dịch). The pavilion sends its Fire-strong children to the palace's Feather Kindling and its
  bird girls to the Wutong Court's aviary (Trần Tước Nhi).

## 8. Relations

- **Phượng Minh Cung (lord):** devout and dependent. The pavilion flies the palace's ordinary post, makes its practice
  fans, and brings down the vent-bird feathers for the Feather Kindling. The writs fly on the Wutong Court's red-crested
  birds, which the pavilion may not breed. Vu Ngô Đồng (`vu_ngo_dong`) lends it birds and forgets to ask for them.
  The pavilion has two of its own at the Wutong Court: the head's nephew Ổ Linh Tước (`o_linh_tuoc`), second bird keeper,
  and the catcher's daughter Trần Tước Nhi (`tran_tuoc_nhi`). The fan master Thích Hồng Linh (`thich_hong_linh`) buys
  its fans.
- **Thính Vũ Lâu (through the post):** the palace's guest elder Chử Trì Âm (`chu_tri_am`) sends letters north on the
  pavilion's birds to the Tower. The replies come back with a second seal under the first. The post master has noticed,
  and so has the palace's Trình Hi Quang from the other side (`phuong_minh.md` §9).
- **Trúc Thực Viên:** its seed feeds the birds, and its rats feed the hawks. Tạ Thanh Loan and Ninh Trúc Thực are
  friends.
- **The dark apes of Huyền Viên Lâm:** the pavilion's only enemy, which killed Ổ Tùng Lâm.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The nine birds.** Since 1196 nine red-crested writ birds have hatched in a hidden loft from eggs Ổ Linh Tước
  carried out of the Wutong Court's aviary, a few from each clutch of its one breeding hen. In 1199 the hen would not
  leave her last clutch. Ổ wrung her neck on the nest, took the eggs, and told Vu Ngô Đồng she had died in the moult;
  Vu wrote her off and has not counted his eggs. The last basket came down to the pavilion lined with her down, kept
  warm for the road, plucked and bloody at the quills. Tạ Thanh Loan opened it, burned the down, and told her sister
  the eggs were Vu's gift.
- **The gift.** Tạ means to carry the nine to the Fire Court, give them to the palace head, and ask that the writs fly
  on Bách Vũ's birds. It is the finest thing the pavilion has ever made, and everyone who touched it is guilty. Hoàn
  Trúc Lung made nine travelling cages for it and charged double. Ổ's price for the hen is to come home as keeper of the
  writ loft, and Tạ has promised it: when the head comes back from the Fire Court, Ổ comes home, and Nhiêu Tiểu Yến, who
  has fed the nine for four years, hands him the key.
- **Where the birds go home.** No one has told Nhiêu that a gift should home anywhere else. At dawn she teaches the
  nine to come to the Blue Luan Whistle and to roost in the hidden loft, so on their first morning at the Fire Court the
  palace's new writ birds will fly back to Bách Vũ.
- **The second seal.** Giải Vân Tiêu flies the Tower's letters and has told only his head, who told him to forget it.
- **The silent day.** In 1195 the pavilion's birds fell silent with the ridge's (A248). Quế Hoàng Oanh remembers the
  moment, and Tạ Thanh Loan wants to know what could do that to a bird.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **Nine birds for the head.** Tạ Thanh Loan carries her writ birds to the Fire Court. The palace head is pleased and
   sends to the Wutong Court to ask where they came from. Lâm Thê Ngô has gone back up for a season to keep the dead
   hen's loft, and he can count eggs. (Tạ, Ổ Linh Tước, Vu, Hoa Triêu Dương, Lâm Thê Ngô; places 330, 449, 6.)
2. **The second seal.** The player carries the post for Giải Vân Tiêu and is asked by someone in the palace to open a
   letter to the Tower. (Giải, Chử Trì Âm, Trình Hi Quang, Quan Vũ Thanh; places 330, 449.)
3. **The first morning.** The nine fly home from the Fire Court on their first morning, writs on their legs, and
   Nhiêu Tiểu Yến wakes to nine sealed writs in the hidden loft. The player can carry them back before anyone reads
   them, or open one. (Nhiêu, Tạ, Ổ Linh Tước, Giải Vân Tiêu; places 330, 449.)
4. **The apes.** The dark apes of Huyền Viên Lâm come down to the lofts. The player can hunt them, bargain with them, or
   find out what drove them out of the jungle. (Tạ Thanh Thước, Trần Dã Minh; places 235, 330.)
5. **The silent tune.** Quế Hoàng Oanh hums what the birds heard in 1195. Every bird in the pavilion stops singing. Tạ
   wants to know who taught it; the palace would rather she did not ask. (Quế, Tạ, Lộ Văn Khánh; places 330, 53, 724.)
6. **The hundredth feather.** A traveller brings a feather that burns without burning. The sisters disagree about
   whether to fill the socket. (Tạ Thanh Thước, Tạ Thanh Loan, Hàng Thải Vũ; place 330.)
7. **The rat meat stops.** If Trúc Thực's grove fails, the rats overrun the coast. Bách Vũ's hawks are the fastest
   answer, and the pavilion gains a standing it has wanted. (Tạ, Khuất Liệp Nguyên, Ninh Trúc Thực; places 330, 1488.)
