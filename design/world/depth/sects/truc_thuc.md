The garden is the first of the phoenix's Three Refusals made into a hall: the phoenix eats only bamboo seed. It was
founded by the palace's failures, Wood-rooted outer disciples who could not hold the Feather Kindling, and it is the
proudest of the palace's vassals because its seed is in every aviary and granary of the south. Since 1050 the bamboo
has seeded every year. Nobody outside the head knows what that costs.

## 2. Land and seat

**Decision (R4): stay at place 25 and re-lean Wood, Fire → Wood, Water.** The seat is wet grassland (Wood 33 %, Water
28 %, Metal 16 %, Earth 13 %, Fire 11 %, density 1.31). Fire at 11 % is not in the top two, so the old leans fail. The
audit offers Long Não Lâm (744, a camphor jungle, Wood 32 %, Fire 20 %), Ẩn Phàm Trại (746, the pirates' burned boat
camp) and four thinner places. All are forest or ruin, and the garden's fields, sluices and grove cannot move. The
garden has been Wood and Water in practice for a century: half its hands came in through the flood terraces Úy Thương
Điền cut in 1108, and only its head still carries a working Fire. The re-lean writes down what is already true.

**What the re-lean costs.** No art, routes or province change. The sect record's `leans` becomes Wood, Water. Chronicle
lines stay true: A243 says spirit farmers founded it, and A246 says what the head did. Add N-truc_thuc-01 to -05 (§3).

**How the land shapes its people (R1).** The garden sits between the bamboo forests of Thê Ngô Lĩnh (Tử Trúc Lâm 42 %
Wood) and the jade-wave coast (Bích Lãng 37 % Wood, 31 % Water). Children born here or at Bích Lãng, Cầm Huyền Hương and
Trúc Phiệt Thôn are Wood-rooted about one and a half times as often as on an even plain, and Water-rooted nearly as
often at Bích Lãng. Fire is rare: the land runs 9-11 % Fire, so a Fire root here is half as likely as on even land. That
is why Ninh Trúc Thực's Fire is a wonder, and why none of his disciples has it. The ridge villages to the north (Trúc
Phiệt, Kiều Mạch) give Metal roots, which the garden cannot teach; they plough as servants.

## 4. Doctrine and customs

- **The first refusal.** The phoenix eats only bamboo seed (Trang Tử; the palace's Tam Bất). The garden holds that the
  seed is the phoenix's before it is anyone's. The first seed of every harvest is carried to the palace whole and
  unhusked, and no one in the garden eats seed before the palace's cart has left.
- **Sixty years for one seed.** The garden's old teaching is patience. A clump of bamboo grows for sixty years, flowers
  once, seeds, and dies, and a cultivator should store their strength in the same way. The second head made an art of it
  (§5). The young say the teaching died in 1050, when the seed began to come every year.
- **Dress and work.** Undyed bamboo-fibre cloth, wide straw hats, and mud to the knee. Rank is shown by the tool on the
  belt: a seed knife for outer hands, a sluice key for the inner gate, a clay censer for the head alone.
- **Food.** Bamboo rice (the seed itself) in the lean months, shoots in spring, carp from the terraces, and the coast's
  fish. No one in the garden kills a bird.
- **The dead** are buried in a clump that has flowered, so that they go into the ground with the grove. Since 1050 no
  clump has flowered and died, and the dead are buried at the grove's edge to wait. Tất Mạn Thu lies under the last
  clump of 1050.
- **Taboos.** Cutting a culm in flower; eating seed before the palace's cart has gone; lighting fire in the grove
  (except the head's censer).
- **Outsiders.** Generous and plain. Any traveller is fed bamboo rice and asked about the weather. The rats are the
  only outsiders the garden hates.

**Why it fits.** The Verdant Cycle Scripture teaches the body to be a field: sow, take root, mend in the shade, harvest
what the foe has grown. The garden took it in the founder's time as the nearest written thing to farming. The outer
method breathes rain into Wood, which is what the land around the garden does. The Water verse came with the flood
terraces, and the Sixty-Year Flower came from watching the bamboo die. Non-lineage members (R5): the servant
ploughman Phí Đông Canh (Metal, stand-in method). Every other named member runs a lineage method.

## 6. Admission

- **Outer gate:** Wood or Water at 30 or more. The garden takes the palace's Wood rejects (it was founded by them),
  the coast's Water children through the terraces, and any village child the head likes.
- **Inner gate and above:** Wood 45 or more. Water-rooted hands without Wood stay on the terraces under Úy Thương Điền,
  and most of them are content to.
- **Exceptions:** servants (tạp dịch) of any root plough and carry; none is taught the lineage.

## 8. Relations

- **Phượng Minh Cung (lord):** loyal, well fed and well thanked. The garden sends the first seed to the Hall of
  Benevolence at Hoàng Vân Thành, where Điền Phong Niên (`dien_phong_nien`) sows it in the spirit fields and pays in rice,
  and to the Wutong Court, where Vu Ngô Đồng's (`vu_ngo_dong`) birds eat it. The palace sends back the garden's Fire
  hopefuls, and in return the garden sends up its Wood girls. Kha Thanh Miêu (`kha_thanh_mieu`), the steward's daughter,
  is the latest, and the great wutong on the ridge has noticed her (`phuong_minh.md` §9). The palace's admission rule
  makes the garden's best Wood children useless to the palace and valuable here, and both sides know it.
- **Bách Vũ Các:** a good customer and a friend. Its messenger birds eat the garden's seed, and its hawks eat the
  garden's rats. Tạ Thanh Loan (`ta_thanh_loan`) pays in feathers and news.
- **Lễ Tuyền Phái:** the other refusal-hall (the sweet spring). The two heads exchange seed and wine at New Year and
  agree about nothing else: Mai Lễ Tuyền (`mai_le_tuyen`) laughs at Ninh's caution.
- **The villages of the coast (Bích Lãng, Cầm Huyền Hương, Trúc Phiệt):** the garden's people. Bích Lãng's paddies are
  the next thing the rats will eat.

## 9. Inside the sect

- **The censer.** Ninh Trúc Thực has made the grove seed every year since 1050 by warming it with his Fire for nine
  nights each spring. It is the old sixty-year bamboo, forced. When he stops, every clump flowers at once and dies, and
  the rats that have bred on the yearly seed for 150 years will come down on the coast. He cannot retire, cannot travel
  in spring, and cannot name an heir who has no Fire. His heir Mẫn Tiết Thanh has none.
- **The rains.** Úy Thương Điền's rain book says the land under the grove dries a little more each decade. She wants one
  fallow year. Four times the head has refused without a reason, and she has drawn her own: he is proud. Since 1150 she
  has kept a second grove of old seed at Tử Trúc Lâm, tended by her disciple Ân Cốc Vũ.
- **The rats.** Khuất Liệp Nguyên stopped counting at four thousand this winter, and they are tunnelling under the brook
  toward Bích Lãng. He believes the head knows something about them.
- **The ploughman.** The old servant Phí Đông Canh has noticed that the "new" bamboo stands in the old clumps.

## 12. Story hooks

1. **The spring the censer stays cold.** Ninh Trúc Thực is wounded, called away, or dies in spring. Every clump flowers
   at once. The palace's aviaries and granaries lose their seed for sixty years, and the rats come. Úy Thương Điền's
   hidden grove is the only seed left. (Ninh, Úy, Ân Cốc Vũ, Vu Ngô Đồng, Điền Phong Niên; places 25, 331, 85, 6.)
2. **A Fire for the grove.** Ninh needs a disciple with Fire. The player, a Fire-rooted village child, or a palace
   ash-born outer disciple could carry the censer. Mẫn Tiết Thanh learns why he was never handed it. (Ninh, Mẫn; place
   25, 449.)
3. **The tunnels under the brook.** Khuất Liệp Nguyên and Yên Mạch Nhi find the rats in Bích Lãng's paddies. Bách Vũ's
   hawks, a Water flood from the terraces or a poison from Lễ Tuyền could hold them, at a price each. (Khuất, Yên, Úy,
   Tạ Thanh Loan; places 1488, 147, 330.)
4. **The fallow year.** Úy Thương Điền asks the palace, over her head's head, to order the grove rested. The palace must
   choose between its seed and its vassal's land. (Úy, Ninh, Điền Phong Niên; places 25, 85.)
5. **The second grove.** Ân Cốc Vũ is seen in Tử Trúc Lâm with seed sacks. The head hears of it from someone who thinks
   it is theft. (Ân, Úy, Ninh; place 331.)
6. **The Wood girl on the ridge.** Kha Thanh Miêu writes home that the great wutong turns its leaves to her. Her father
   wants her home; the palace wants her for the tree. (Kha Tùng Canh, Kha Thanh Miêu, Vu Ngô Đồng; places 25, 6.)
7. **The flowering.** If the head must save the garden in a fight, he can use the Sixty-Year Flower he has stored for
   160 years. Garden lore says a head who flowers does not see the next spring, and Tất Mạn Thu did not. (Ninh, Tất Mạn
   Thu's grave; place 25.)
