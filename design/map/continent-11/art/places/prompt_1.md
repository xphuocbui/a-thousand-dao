The place: Thúy Vi Tông, great seat. Paint it as a great sect's seat: vertical, stacked pagoda roofs and terraces climbing the slope, the tallest outline in the land, walls and gates, a main hall.
How it looks: Twin summits rise out of the blue-green haze of the hills, their slopes stepped with herb terraces and white-walled halls under green-glazed roofs. Art brief: A tall sect seat on twin forested summits, the tallest outline in its land: stacked pagoda halls with green-glazed roofs and white walls climbing stepped terraces up both peaks. Signature element: a broad path of white stone winding in a spiral around the higher summit from the mist to the peak, its upper turns standing clear above a sea of blue-green mist. On the lower slopes: herb terraces in bright green stripes; at the base the slopes vanish into haze, with a stone stair emerging toward the market below. Warm golden light from the top left on the upper roofs and the white path. Avoid: text, people larger than specks, anything outside the twin summits.

Rules:
- Paint the place as a small, finely detailed miniature, as if a model-maker had built it into this painted relief: seen from above at a gentle angle so roofs, walls and towers read clearly, its base sitting naturally on the terrain that is there (follow the slopes, the shore, the river).
- Keep it centred and compact: the whole place fits inside a circle about 25 % of the image width across the centre. Short roads or paths may lead out of it.
- Match the map exactly: the same golden-hour light from the TOP LEFT with soft shadows to the bottom right, the same painterly style, colours and level of detail as the surrounding terrain; the building scale fits the trees and hills around it.
- Leave everything outside that central circle exactly as it is in the input.
- Never: text, labels, banners with writing, flags with symbols, people larger than specks, a frame, a vignette, clouds over the place.
